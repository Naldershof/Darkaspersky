# Major Unresolved Community Lore Debates

## Debate 1: Marika's True Motivation

**Position A — Marika is a liberator:**
She shattered the Ring to free the Lands Between from the Greater Will's control. Her actions are a rebellion against cosmic tyranny. She endured imprisonment within the Erdtree as the price of freedom.

**Position B — Marika is a manipulator:**
She shattered the Ring out of grief (Godwyn's death) or ambition (to reshape the world in her image). She's no different from the Greater Will — just another god seeking control. The DLC's revelations about her genocide in the Land of Shadow support this.

**Position C — Marika is both:**
She began as the Greater Will's vessel, grew disillusioned, and chose destruction. But her methods (genocide, exile, manipulation) make her morally complex — a revolutionary who uses tyrannical means.

**Key evidence each side cites:**
- Her words at the Erdtree (shared by Melina) suggest foreknowledge and deliberation
- The DLC shows she committed genocide before building the Golden Order
- Her imprisonment suggests the Greater Will views her as a traitor
- Radagon (her other half) tried to repair the Ring, suggesting internal conflict

## Debate 2: Who/What is Melina?

**Theory A — Marika's daughter / The Gloam-Eyed Queen:**
She says she was "born at the foot of the Erdtree" and given purpose by her "mother." Her sealed eye and Destined Death connections link her to the Gloam-Eyed Queen.

**Theory B — A fragment of Marika herself:**
Melina may be a split-off aspect of Marika (like Radagon), created specifically for the purpose of burning the Erdtree. She carries Marika's words and memories.

**Theory C — An independent spirit:**
She may be a spiritual entity born from the Erdtree itself — a manifestation of the tree's own desire to burn and be reborn.

**Unresolved:** No definitive in-game statement confirms her identity. The Frenzied Flame ending's open-eye scene is the strongest clue.

## Debate 3: Is the Greater Will Good, Evil, or Indifferent?

**Position A — Benevolent but authoritarian:**
It brought order to chaos, established civilization, and provides grace. Its methods are controlling but its intent is stability.

**Position B — A cosmic parasite:**
It invaded the Lands Between, supplanted existing civilizations (the Eternal Cities, the dragons), and uses Marika as a puppet. The Elden Beast is a parasite grafted onto the world.

**Position C — Simply alien:**
It's a cosmic entity that doesn't operate on human moral scales. It's neither good nor evil — it imposes order because that's its nature, not because it cares about the inhabitants.

## Debate 4: Which Ending is "Best"?

This is the most subjective debate but reveals philosophical positions:

- **Age of Fracture defenders:** Stability matters more than perfection. The broken order can heal.
- **Age of Order defenders:** The system needs reform, not revolution.
- **Age of Duskborn defenders:** Accepting death makes life meaningful. The most "natural" choice.
- **Frenzied Flame defenders:** The system is so corrupt that only total destruction can lead to rebirth.
- **Age of Stars defenders:** Freedom from all gods is the only true liberation.
- **Blessing of Despair advocates:** The DLC ending shows that even "good" gods are dangerous.

**Community consensus:** There is none. Miyazaki intentionally designed all endings as valid philosophical positions.

## Debate 5: The Crucible's Relationship to the Erdtree

**Theory A — The Crucible IS the Erdtree's origin:**
The Erdtree grew from the Crucible, which was the primordial state of all life. The Golden Order then sanitized it.

**Theory B — The Erdtree supplanted the Crucible:**
The Greater Will planted the Erdtree OVER the Crucible/Greattree, colonizing an existing power structure. They are separate things.

**Theory C — The Crucible is the Erdtree without the Greater Will:**
If you remove the Greater Will's influence, the Erdtree would return to its Crucible state — wild, unified, and without the order/chaos distinction.

**Evidence:** Crucible Knight armor says the knights served "in the age when the Erdtree's primordial form was still a common sight." This supports Theory A but is ambiguous about B and C.

## Debate 6: Did Miquella Plan His Own Abduction?

**Pre-DLC consensus:** Miquella was an innocent victim kidnapped by Mohg.

**Post-DLC consensus:** Almost certainly yes. The DLC reveals:
- Miquella used the cocoon to travel to the Land of Shadow
- He shed parts of himself deliberately at each cross
- His "charm" was supernatural mind control, not genuine kindness
- He planned to become a god and needed Mohg's blood ritual as a stepping stone

**Remaining question:** Did Miquella manipulate Mohg from the start, or did he adapt his plans after being taken?
