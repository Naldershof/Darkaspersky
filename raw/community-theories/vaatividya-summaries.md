# Community Lore Analysis — VaatiVidya and Major Lore YouTubers

## VaatiVidya — Key Theories and Analyses

### The Complete Story of Elden Ring

VaatiVidya's comprehensive lore video (his most-viewed Elden Ring content) presents the following narrative framework:

**The Timeline:** The Greater Will sent the Elden Beast to the Lands Between as a star. The Elden Beast became the Elden Ring, and the Erdtree grew around it. Before this, the land was ruled by dragons (under Placidusax) and populated by various pre-Erdtree civilizations (the Nox, the Giants, the Ancestral followers). Marika was chosen by the Greater Will as its vessel, and the Golden Order was established.

**Marika's Arc:** Vaati emphasizes Marika's trajectory from loyal vessel to rebel. She initially served the Greater Will faithfully, waging war against the Giants and establishing the Golden Order. The turning point was Godwyn's death — an event that either broke her faith or confirmed her suspicion that the system was flawed. She then set in motion a long-term plan: exile the Tarnished (so they could return when needed), remove Destined Death (to create conditions that would eventually become intolerable), and finally shatter the Ring.

**The Shattering's Purpose:** Vaati argues the Shattering was not destruction but liberation — Marika's attempt to free the Lands Between from the Greater Will's control by breaking the instrument of that control. Radagon's simultaneous attempt to repair the Ring represents the other half of her nature trying to maintain order.

[Context: VaatiVidya (YouTube) is the most prominent Soulsborne lore creator, with his Elden Ring videos collectively reaching tens of millions of views. His interpretations are widely influential in the community.]

### Miquella: The Most Dangerous Demigod

Vaati's analysis of Miquella argues that Miquella's childlike appearance and seemingly benevolent goals mask a deeply manipulative nature. Key points:

- Miquella's Haligtree was not just a sanctuary but a rival power structure, designed to replace the Erdtree entirely.
- His "unalloyed" nature (resistance to Outer God influence) made him the only demigod who could operate independently of any cosmic patron.
- His ability to compel affection (referenced in the Bewitching Branch item description) means that much of the loyalty he inspired may have been magical rather than earned.
- The DLC confirms and expands on this reading, revealing Miquella's willingness to sacrifice his own qualities (his love, his wisdom, his compassion) to achieve his goals.

[Context: This analysis anticipated the DLC's treatment of Miquella before the expansion was released.]

### The Night of the Black Knives

Vaati's reconstruction of the assassination night:

1. Ranni orchestrated the plot, using the Black Knife assassins (Numen women, like Marika herself).
2. Fragments of the Rune of Death were stolen from Maliketh and imbued into the Black Knives.
3. The assassination of Godwyn was simultaneous with Ranni's destruction of her own body — both required the Rune of Death.
4. Ranni's motive was to free herself from her Empyrean destiny (to potentially become the next vessel of the Elden Ring under the Greater Will's control).
5. Godwyn was the sacrificial counterpart: his soul died so Ranni's body could die, and vice versa. The Cursemark of Death is shared between them.

Vaati notes the unresolved question of Marika's involvement. Did she know? The Black Knife assassins are Numen, Marika's own people. The theft of Destined Death from Maliketh (who was wholly loyal to Marika) seems improbable without inside help.

### Radagon is Marika (And What That Means)

Vaati's analysis of the Marika/Radagon duality:

- They are one being with two personas, two wills, possibly two souls in one body.
- Radagon was "always" Marika — his marriage to Rennala, his fathering of Radahn/Rykard/Ranni, was all done by Marika in another form.
- The theological implications are staggering: the God-Queen of the Golden Order is also the husband who abandoned Rennala, the father of the demigods who are simultaneously also Marika's children/stepchildren.
- The Shattering is a self-inflicted wound: Marika broke the Ring, Radagon tried to repair it, and the result is a fractured being trying to accomplish two contradictory goals simultaneously.

## Smoughtown — Key Analyses

### The Nature of the Outer Gods

Smoughtown's comprehensive analysis of the Outer Gods catalogues the confirmed and suspected cosmic entities:

1. **The Greater Will** — The dominant Outer God, working through the Elden Ring and Erdtree. Possibly not benevolent but merely self-interested.
2. **The Formless Mother** — The blood god, patron of Mohg. Works through wounds and bleeding.
3. **The Scarlet Rot Outer God** — Sealed in the Lake of Rot. Uses Malenia as a vessel.
4. **The Fell God** — The fire deity worshipped by the Giants. Sealed in the Fire Giant's body.
5. **The Frenzied Flame** — Possibly an outer god, possibly a fundamental force. Associated with the Three Fingers and chaotic dissolution.
6. **The Dark Moon** — Possibly Ranni's patron, associated with the Nox civilization and the Carian royal family. Whether the Moon is an outer god or something else entirely is debated.

Smoughtown argues that the game's central conflict is not between good and evil but between competing cosmic parasites, each seeking to influence the Lands Between through mortal vessels. The Tarnished's journey is to navigate between these powers.

## Zullie the Witch — Technical and Environmental Discoveries

Zullie's contributions are primarily technical (camera manipulation, model examination, hidden details) but yield significant lore insights:

### Hidden Details in Boss Arenas

- Radagon's boss arena contains the fractured statue of Marika, and close examination shows Radagon literally pulls himself out of Marika's body during the transition.
- The Elden Beast's arena is located inside the Erdtree (confirmed by the skybox), placing the final confrontation within the tree itself.
- Placidusax's severed head stumps number three, making him originally a five-headed dragon. This connects to real-world mythology of multi-headed cosmic serpents.
- Godwyn's face appears in multiple locations underground, growing larger at each appearance, suggesting his corpse is spreading throughout the entire root network.

### The Erdtree's True Nature

Zullie demonstrated through model examination that the Erdtree is built on top of (or grew from) the Crucible — the primordial tree visible in the game's earliest architectural motifs. The Erdtree is not original; it is a parasitic growth on an older, more primal tree of life. This supports the theory that the Greater Will's order replaced, rather than created, the divine framework of the Lands Between.

## Tarnished Archaeologist — Environmental Analysis

### The Walking Mausoleums

Tarnished Archaeologist's analysis of the Walking Mausoleums argues they are mobile tombs carrying the bodies of demigods who died in the Shattering war. Their constant wandering represents an inability to find rest — the dead seeking burial in a world where death itself is broken. The spectral soldiers clinging to their undersides are loyal retainers who refuse to abandon their masters even in death.

### The Albinaurics and Their Purpose

Analysis of Albinauric lore across all sources suggests they were created by the Nox civilization as surface-world agents. First-generation Albinaurics (who look human but degenerate) were meant to pass among surface-dwellers. Second-generation Albinaurics (who are more bestial) represent a refined but differently purposed design. Their persecution by the Golden Order stems from their artificial nature — they exist outside the Erdtree's cycle, making them abominations by the Order's definition.

## Ratatoskr — Mythological Connections

### Norse Mythology Parallels

- The Erdtree as Yggdrasil (the World Tree)
- Marika/Radagon as Odin (self-sacrifice, hanging from the world tree, seeking knowledge)
- The Tarnished as Einherjar (dead warriors revived to fight in a final battle)
- Destined Death as Ragnarok (the death of gods)
- The Elden Ring as a metaphor for Fate/Wyrd
- Ranni's moon order as a Vanir perspective (nature/moon worship opposing the Aesir/golden order)

### Celtic and Arthurian Parallels

- The Roundtable Hold as the Round Table
- The Elden Lord as the Fisher King (a wounded ruler whose injury blights the land)
- The Shattering as the Wasteland (a land made barren by a broken covenant)
- Miquella as a Grail figure (the sacred object that could heal the world, stolen and hidden)

[Context: These mythological analyses provide frameworks for interpretation but should not be treated as authorial intent. Miyazaki draws from multiple mythological traditions without directly adapting any single source.]
