# Map Fragments and Painting Descriptions

## Map Fragments

Map fragments in Elden Ring are physical objects found on stone pillars (stelae) throughout the Lands Between. Each fragment reveals a portion of the world map when collected. The stelae themselves are significant: they are markers placed at consistent intervals, suggesting a systematic cartographic project that predated the Shattering. Someone (likely the Golden Order or an earlier civilization) mapped the entire Lands Between and placed these reference points for travelers.

### Limgrave, West

Found at the Gatefront Ruins. The fragment reveals the western half of Limgrave, including the starting area, the Church of Elleh, and the approach to Stormveil. The stele is positioned at a major crossroads, consistent with its function as a navigational aid. The map itself shows roads, landmarks, and terrain in a stylized manner consistent with medieval cartography.

### Limgrave, East

Found further east in Limgrave. Reveals the Mistwood, Fort Haight, and the eastern coastline. The placement of this stele near the boundary between Limgrave and Caelid is notable: the map shows the land as it was, not as it is. The Scarlet Rot's devastation of Caelid is not reflected on the map fragment, suggesting the maps were drawn before that catastrophe.

### Liurnia, West and East

Two fragments covering Liurnia of the Lakes. The map shows the lake, the Academy island, and surrounding settlements. The submerged ruins visible in gameplay are partially depicted on the map, suggesting the flooding occurred gradually (some structures are shown as above-water on the map but are submerged when the player visits). Alternatively, the cartographer depicted them as they were when the survey was conducted.

### Altus Plateau

Found on the plateau after ascending via the Grand Lift or the Ruin-Strewn Precipice. Shows the plateau's roads, the approach to Leyndell, and the surrounding geography. The map indicates a well-developed road network with multiple routes to the capital, suggesting the Altus Plateau was heavily trafficked in its heyday.

### Leyndell, Royal Capital

The capital's map fragment is found within Leyndell's outer approaches. The map shows the city's layout, including walls, major structures, and the Erdtree's position at the center. The level of detail on the capital fragment is notably higher than rural fragments, reflecting the city's importance.

### Mt. Gelmir

Covers Volcano Manor's surrounding region. The map shows the volcanic terrain, paths, and fortifications. Lava flows are depicted on the map, indicating volcanic activity was present when the map was drawn.

### Mountaintops of the Giants

Found in the frozen Mountaintops. The map shows Giant structures, the path to the Forge, and the general layout of the frozen landscape. The scale of structures depicted on the map hints at the Giants' enormous architecture.

### Consecrated Snowfield

A hidden map fragment in the secret snowfield. The area's perpetual blizzard makes navigating to the stele difficult without guidance. The map reveals the snowfield's layout and the path to Ordina, Liturgical Town.

### Siofra River and Ainsel River (Underground)

Underground map fragments are found on stelae within the subterranean regions. Their existence implies that the cartographic project extended below ground, meaning whoever commissioned the maps knew about the underground civilizations and considered them part of the Lands Between's territory. This is notable given the surface world's general ignorance of or disinterest in the underground.

### Mohgwyn Palace

A map fragment exists in Mohgwyn Palace, adding the blood-soaked domain to the player's map. The inclusion of this hidden, underground domain in the official cartographic record is unusual and may indicate the map system was updated or that the original cartographer had extensive knowledge of hidden locations.

## Paintings

Paintings in Elden Ring are found throughout the world, each depicting a specific location from a specific vantage point. When the player finds the real-world location shown in the painting, a phantom painter disappears, leaving behind an item. The paintings function as treasure hunts but also serve lore purposes: each painting was created by someone who traveled to that spot and captured what they saw.

### "Homing Instinct"

Found in Stormveil Castle. Depicts a view of what appears to be the seaside near Limgrave. The phantom painter is found at the corresponding cliff, leaving behind an incantation. The title suggests longing for home, and its placement in Stormveil (a place of imprisonment and grafting) creates a poignant contrast: someone in Godrick's castle painting a picture of freedom.

### "Prophecy"

Found in Stormveil Castle. Depicts a location associated with the Heretical Rise in the Mountaintops of the Giants. The title implies the painter foresaw something at this distant, frozen location. Its placement in the warm, lowland Stormveil suggests the painter had prophetic vision or had traveled extensively before being trapped in the castle.

### "Resurrection"

Found in the Artist's Shack in Liurnia. Depicts a view from a high vantage point. The title's theological weight in a world where death and rebirth are mechanically and narratively central is significant.

### "Champion's Song"

Found in the Artist's Shack in Limgrave, near the Mistwood. Depicts a location in the Altus Plateau region. The "champion" referenced may be a specific warrior, or it may reference the Tarnished champions generally.

### "Redmane"

Depicts a scene related to Redmane Castle or the Caelid region. The title directly references Radahn's knightly order, connecting the painting to the General's legacy.

### "Flightless Bird"

Found in the Fortified Manor (the physical Roundtable Hold in Leyndell). Depicts a view from near the Windmill Village area. A flightless bird is a creature denied its nature, a metaphor applicable to many characters in Elden Ring (Ranni denied her Empyrean destiny, Morgott denied his rightful throne, Malenia denied her health).

### "Sorcerer"

Found in the Academy of Raya Lucaria. Depicts a view of a location associated with sorcery. Its placement in the Academy connects it to the scholarly tradition, perhaps a student who documented their travels.

### Painter Ghosts and Their Meaning

The phantom painters who dissolve when found at their painting locations are spirits of dead artists. Their paintings outlived them, guiding others to the beauty they found. In a world of war, decay, and divine conflict, these small acts of artistic preservation represent a humanity that persists despite everything. The painters sought beauty; the player follows their vision. It is one of the game's gentlest mechanics and a quiet argument that the Lands Between was worth painting even in its ruined state.

## World Map General Observations

The complete world map of the Lands Between, when assembled from all fragments, reveals several lore-relevant features:

**The Erdtree's centrality:** The tree is positioned at the physical center of the map. All roads lead toward it. The geography itself orbits the divine tree.

**The underground mirror:** The underground map roughly mirrors portions of the surface map, suggesting the eternal cities were positioned deliberately beneath corresponding surface locations. Nokron is beneath Limgrave. Nokstella is beneath Liurnia. The Lake of Rot is beneath the Altus Plateau. This mirroring suggests a deeper connection between surface and underground than mere coincidence.

**The coastline:** The Lands Between is bounded by sea on multiple sides. The coastline is jagged, with cliffs and inaccessible beaches. The world feels like an island or peninsula, isolated from whatever lies beyond. The opening narration mentions a "foggy sea" that the Tarnished cross to arrive, suggesting the Lands Between is geographically separate from the wider world.

**Roads and infrastructure:** The map shows a comprehensive road network that has largely fallen into disrepair. The roads connect major settlements, divine towers, and fortifications in patterns that suggest centralized planning. This infrastructure represents the Golden Order at its functional peak, a civilization that built and maintained connections across an entire continent.

**The Divine Towers:** The towers are visible on the map as prominent landmarks at the edges of regions. Their positions form a rough circle around the Erdtree, suggesting they were placed to project the Ring's power (or monitor it) across the entire territory. After the Shattering, they serve as activation points for Great Runes, but their original purpose may have been different.
