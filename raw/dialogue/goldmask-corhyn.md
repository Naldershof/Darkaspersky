# Goldmask and Brother Corhyn — Complete Dialogue

## Brother Corhyn — Roundtable Hold (Initial)

> "Ahh, another seeker of knowledge. I am Brother Corhyn. A humble man of the cloth. I deal in incantations. The sacred arts of the Golden Order."

> "The Golden Order is the great tapestry of the Erdtree's law. Woven by the Greater Will. It governs all life in the Lands Between."

> "Would you like to learn an incantation? I have much to teach, for those who walk in grace."

[Context: Corhyn is an incantation merchant found at the Roundtable Hold. He is a devout follower of the Golden Order and deeply reverent of the Two Fingers.]

### On the Two Fingers

> "The Two Fingers speak for the Greater Will. They are the interpreters of divine law. Without them, we would be lost. Without direction."

> "Heed the Fingers, Tarnished. They will guide you true."

### On His Faith

> "I have devoted my life to the study of the Golden Order. Its laws. Its incantations. Its purpose. Everything has purpose, under the Order."

> "The fundamentals of the Golden Order are immutable. They do not change. They do not falter. That is the beauty of it. That is the truth."

### Altus Plateau — Bridge (Near the Erdtree-Gazing Hill)

> "I have heard tell of the great Goldmask. The most brilliant theologian of our age. He is said to have discovered fundamental truths about the Golden Order. Truths that no one else could perceive."

> "I must go to him. I must learn from him."

[Context: Corhyn has left the Roundtable Hold and traveled to the Altus Plateau in search of Goldmask. He can be found near the bridge where Goldmask stands, gazing eternally at the Erdtree.]

> "..."

Corhyn is the only one who can interpret these gestures. Goldmask wears a golden mask and stands in contemplation of the Erdtree.]

## Brother Corhyn — Traveling with Goldmask

### On the Erdtree-Gazing Hill

> "Goldmask has been standing upon that bridge for... I cannot say how long. Days. Weeks. Perhaps longer. He stares at the Erdtree. And his fingers move."

> "His gestures... I believe they are a form of communication. A language of the Fingers themselves. I have devoted myself to understanding them."

> "He speaks of imperfection. Of a flaw within the Golden Order. A flaw at its very foundation."

[Context: Corhyn begins translating Goldmask's finger-signs. The revelation shakes him — the Golden Order, which he believed immutable, has a fundamental flaw.]

### On the Road to Leyndell

> "Goldmask's insight grows deeper by the day. He sees what I cannot. A flaw in the Order. A flaw born of... the gods themselves."

> "The Golden Order was not always as it is. It was changed. Altered. By the hand of one who should have been its greatest guardian."

> "But who? Who would dare alter the Golden Order?"

[Context: Corhyn is disturbed by Goldmask's revelations. The "flaw" relates to the gods — specifically, Radagon and Marika — tampering with the Order.]

## The Law of Regression — Leyndell, the Erdtree Sanctuary

### Before the Statue

> (At the Erdtree Sanctuary in Leyndell, there is a statue of Radagon. If the player casts the incantation "Law of Regression" before this statue, the statue transforms — revealing the truth that Radagon IS Marika. They are one being.)

[Context: This is one of the game's most significant lore revelations. The incantation "Law of Regression" reveals hidden truths — in this case, that the statue of Radagon was hiding Marika within it. Radagon and Marika are two aspects of the same being.]

### Corhyn's Reaction (After the Revelation)

> "The statue... it changed. Radagon is... Marika? They are one and the same?"

> "No... that cannot be. The Golden Order... the fundamentals... If Radagon is Marika, then the Order itself was built upon..."

> "Goldmask! What does this mean? What are your fingers saying?"

[Context: Corhyn is shattered by the revelation. His entire worldview — that the Golden Order is immutable and perfect — crumbles.]

---

## Goldmask's Response — After the Radagon/Marika Revelation

> "... ... ..."

> (Goldmask's fingers move with new urgency. Corhyn translates:)

> "He says... 'The Golden Order was imperfect because its gods were imperfect. Radagon and Marika — two wills in one body — brought contradiction to the Order. The flaw is not in the law, but in the lawgiver.'"

> "He says... 'What is needed is not a new Order, but a perfect Order. An Order purged of the caprice of the gods. Where the law stands absolute, beyond the reach of any god's will.'"

[Context: Goldmask's solution to the flaw in the Golden Order is not to abandon it but to perfect it — removing the influence of fickle gods and making the law itself supreme. This leads to the Mending Rune of Perfect Order.]

## Brother Corhyn — Crisis of Faith

### On the Bridge, After the Revelation

> "Radagon is Marika... The two that we worshipped as separate divine beings... are one."

> "Then the Golden Order... was it all a lie? A pretense by the gods? Were we all deceived?"

> "No. No, I refuse to believe that. The Order itself is still true. The fundamentals still hold. They must."

### Later, After Goldmask's Contemplation

> "Goldmask says the Order must be perfected. Purified of the gods' caprice. That the law must stand on its own, immutable and incorruptible."

> "Perhaps he is right. Perhaps this is the answer. Not to abandon the Order, but to transcend the gods who made it."

> "I had lost faith. But Goldmask has restored it. In a form I did not expect."

[Context: Corhyn's faith is broken and then rebuilt in a new form. He comes to accept Goldmask's vision of a perfected Order.]

> "The Erdtree burns. The Order crumbles. And yet Goldmask stands firm."

> "He says... the fire matters not. The law endures. Even if the Erdtree is ash, the Golden Order can be reforged. Made perfect."

## Goldmask — Death

> (Goldmask is eventually found dead, sitting in his contemplative pose, near the Erdtree. His body is lifeless but his posture unchanged — as though he simply stopped living while deep in thought.)

> (The Mending Rune of Perfect Order can be found on or near his body.)

[Context: Goldmask dies having completed his work. The Mending Rune of Perfect Order is his legacy. It can be used at the Elden Ring to create an ending where the Golden Order is restored but purged of divine caprice — a rule of law, not of gods.]

> "Goldmask... He is gone. But his work endures."

> "The Mending Rune of Perfect Order. His final gift. A rune to purge the Golden Order of the gods' imperfection."

> "I have followed him to the end. And I am not sorry for it."

> "I am done. There is nothing left for me."

[Context: In some quest states, Corhyn does not achieve resolution and instead falls into complete despair. He may turn hostile or simply vanish.]

## Notes on Goldmask and Corhyn's Lore Significance

- Goldmask never speaks a single word. His entire characterization comes through Corhyn's interpretation of his finger-gestures. This is a deliberate design choice — his wisdom is beyond language.
- The Law of Regression is an incantation that returns things to their original form. It requires high Intelligence — unusual for a faith-based incantation. This represents the intersection of reason and faith.
- The revelation that Radagon IS Marika is the central mystery of Elden Ring's lore. Two beings in one body — Radagon who sought to repair the Elden Ring after Marika shattered it. They are in conflict with themselves.
- Goldmask's solution — the Mending Rune of Perfect Order — removes the gods' ability to alter the Golden Order on a whim. It is arguably the most conservative ending: the Order is preserved but made incorruptible.
- Corhyn's journey mirrors many religious crises: unquestioning faith, discovery of hypocrisy at the top, crisis, and then either a reformed faith or total abandonment.
- The "caprice of the gods" that Goldmask identifies is Marika's decision to shatter the Elden Ring. Had the gods not been able to alter the Order, the Shattering would not have occurred.
- Goldmask's death in his contemplative pose suggests he achieved a kind of enlightenment — or exhausted himself reaching for it.
