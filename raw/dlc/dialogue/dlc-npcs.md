# DLC NPC Dialogue — Lore-Critical Lines

---

## Needle Knight Leda

### On Miquella's Purpose
> "We follow in Miquella's footsteps. He has entered the Land of Shadow
> to claim a new godhood — one built upon compassion rather than order.
> All of us have been touched by his kindness. We march at his side
> because we believe. Because we must."

### On Miquella's Charm (If Questioned)
> "Yes, I know what you would say. That Miquella's charm is a kind of compulsion.
> That we follow him not of our own will, but because he has made us love him.
> I have considered this. And I say — does it matter?
> If the outcome is a kinder world, does the means of devotion matter?"

### On the Other Followers
> "Each of us came to Miquella for our own reasons.
> Hornsent seeks justice for his people. Thiollier mourns St. Trina.
> Ansbach serves out of old loyalty to the house of Mohg.
> Freyja follows for honor. Moore... Moore simply believes.
> And I? I am his needle. I will cut away anything that threatens his ascent."

### Turning Against the Tarnished (Late Game)
> "You would stand against him? Against Miquella?
> Then you are a threat to the new age, and I will remove you
> as I have removed every other obstacle in his path.
> Do not mistake my devotion for blindness. I see clearly.
> I see that you must die."

### On Killing Former Allies
> "Hornsent wavered. Thiollier questioned. They had to be dealt with.
> Miquella's dream cannot afford doubt. A single crack in faith
> and the whole edifice crumbles. I am sorry for them.
> But I am sorrier still for anyone who forces my hand."

---

## Hornsent

### On Marika's Sins
> "You want to know what Marika did? What your precious Queen of the Erdtree did
> to the people of this land? She came with fire and sword.
> Messmer — her son, her hidden weapon — led the slaughter.
> My people, the hornsent, worshipped at the Shaman Towers.
> We had our own rites, our own gods. Marika declared them blasphemy.
> She burned our villages. She killed our shamans. She took our children
> and sealed them in jars — living prisons of flesh — for her experiments.
> The jar warriors you see in the Lands Between? Those are our children.
> Our stolen children, packed into clay vessels and made to fight.
> And when she was done, she hid this land away
> so no one would ever know what she had done."

### On the Jar Folk
> "The jars. You have seen them, yes? Living vessels, stuffed with flesh.
> In the Lands Between they are curiosities — loyal, simple, almost endearing.
> But here? Here we remember what they are. The hornsent children
> were taken from their families and compressed into those clay prisons.
> Marika's followers called it a sacred rite. We call it what it was:
> an atrocity."

### On Miquella
> "I followed Miquella because he promised justice.
> He said he would build a new order — one that would not repeat
> his mother's cruelties. He said the hornsent would be remembered.
> But now... I see him shedding himself, piece by piece.
> The charm. The love. The duty. What remains of the one who promised us justice?
> What kind of god discards compassion on the road to godhood?"

### Breaking Faith
> "I can follow him no further. A god who sheds his love is no god of mine.
> Miquella has become the very thing he claimed to oppose —
> a power that bends others to its will without regard for their suffering.
> If Leda comes for me, so be it. I would rather die with my convictions
> than live as a puppet in a kind god's empire."

---

## Thiollier

### On St. Trina
> "St. Trina... she was gentle. She brought sleep to the suffering,
> peace to the restless. I was her most devoted follower.
> I would have done anything for her. Anything.
> And now I learn she was Miquella all along.
> The sleep she offered — was it kindness, or control?
> Was I a follower, or merely another soul
> lulled into compliance by a demigod's dream?"

### On Miquella Shedding His Love
> "He discarded his love for St. Trina's followers. For us. For me.
> At a cross in the Shadow Keep, he simply... let it go.
> As though we were weight to be shed.
> I felt it — the moment his love left him.
> A coldness, where once there was warmth.
> I still love St. Trina. But St. Trina no longer loves me.
> She never did. She was never real."

### On Poison and Devotion
> "They say poison is the recourse of the desperate and the devoted.
> I am both. If St. Trina is dead — if she was never truly alive —
> then let me serve the memory of her.
> Let my poisons be the last gift of a faithful dreamer."

### If Aided Against Leda
> "Thank you. Leda would have killed me for my doubts.
> Perhaps she is right that doubt is dangerous.
> But I would rather doubt than worship blindly.
> Miquella cast us aside. I will not pretend otherwise."

## Ansbach

### On Mohg and Miquella
> "I served Lord Mohg. Yes, the Lord of Blood — reviled, accursed, an omen.
> But Mohg loved Miquella, after his own fashion. He stole the boy from the Haligtree,
> kept him in his cocoon beneath the Mohgwyn Palace, dreaming of raising him
> to godhood so they might rule together.
> Miquella did not resist. Some say he was sleeping, helpless.
> But I wonder now — was it all part of Miquella's plan?
> Did he allow himself to be taken, knowing Mohg's obsession
> would serve as a stepping stone to the Land of Shadow?"

### On Miquella's True Nature
> "I came here to understand what Miquella truly is.
> Not the Kind. Not the golden child. Not St. Trina.
> The thing beneath all those masks.
> And I think I see it now. Miquella is an architect of devotion.
> He builds love the way his mother built the Golden Order—
> as a structure of control. His charm is not incidental.
> It is his primary weapon.
> Every follower, every ally, every soul who adores him—
> we are all instruments. Mohg was an instrument.
> Radahn is an instrument. Even you, Tarnished, were meant to be one."

### On the Final Confrontation
> "If you mean to stop Miquella, you will face Radahn—
> or what remains of him. The starscourge has been hollowed out
> and filled with Miquella's will. He is a puppet, a vessel,
> a lord in name only. And Miquella rides upon his back
> like a child upon a steed, guiding him with strings of gold.
> It is the cruelest thing I have ever seen.
> And I have served the Lord of Blood."

## Moore

### On Simple Faith
> "Miquella is kind. Miquella is good. That's all I know.
> The others talk about charm and manipulation and free will.
> I don't understand any of that. I just know that
> when Miquella looked at me, I felt like I mattered.
> No one ever made me feel that way before.
> If that's a spell, then I don't want it broken."

### On Following Despite Everything
> "They say he's shedding himself. His love, his charm, his body.
> I don't care. Even if he becomes something I can't understand,
> I'll follow him. Because the Miquella who was kind to me—
> that Miquella was real, even if only for a moment.
> And a moment of real kindness is more than most gods offer."

## Freyja

### On Radahn
> "I followed Radahn once. The real Radahn — the starscourge,
> the lion of the battlefield, the man who held back the stars
> so that Ranni's fate would not befall the world.
> He was the greatest warrior I have ever known.
> And now Miquella has taken his body and made it walk again.
> That is not my general. That is a corpse on strings.
> I came to the Land of Shadow to honor Radahn's memory.
> But there is no honor in what Miquella has done to him."

### On Duty vs. Conscience
> "Leda says I should stay the course. That Miquella's new age
> is bigger than any one warrior's legacy.
> But I am a knight, not a zealot. My oath was to the living Radahn,
> not to the puppet Miquella has fashioned from his remains.
> When the time comes, I will stand against this travesty.
> Radahn deserves that much."

## Dryleaf Dane

### On the Martial Arts of the Land of Shadow
> "The dryleaf arts were born here, in the Land of Shadow.
> Before Marika's invasion, the people of this land had their own ways—
> their own warriors, their own disciplines.
> The dryleaf style is a legacy of the shamans' bodyguards,
> who fought without weapons, using only their hands and their faith.
> Marika tried to erase it. She failed."

### On Miquella
> "I have no quarrel with Miquella, nor any great devotion.
> I came to this land to preserve the old ways,
> not to crown a new god. If Miquella's ascent threatens
> the dryleaf legacy, I will oppose him.
> If not, I will watch from a distance.
> The affairs of gods are not the affairs of martial artists."

## Sir Igon

### On Bayle the Dread
> "BAYLE! That accursed wyrm! That defiler of dragonkind!
> He tore the wing from the great dragon, from our lord—
> the ancient dragon who ruled these peaks before Marika came!
> Bayle is no true dragon. He is a traitor, a beast of pure destruction.
> I have sworn to hunt him to the ends of the earth,
> through death itself if I must!
> Tarnished — if you face Bayle upon the Jagged Peak,
> summon me! SUMMON ME! I will not rest until that wyrm is dead!"

### On the Ancient Dragons of the Land of Shadow
> "Before Marika, before the Erdtree, the dragons ruled these lands.
> Not the wretched magma wyrms or the mindless beasts you see now—
> true dragons, ancient and wise, lords of storm and stone.
> Bayle's betrayal shattered their dominion.
> And then Marika came and finished what Bayle began.
> The dragons of the shadow land were driven to the Jagged Peak,
> the last refuge of a dying age."

## Count Ymir, Mother of Fingers

### On Metyr
> "The Mother of Fingers awaits in the cathedral below.
> Metyr — she who gave birth to the Two Fingers and the Three Fingers alike.
> A vassal of the Greater Will, sent to this world to guide its inhabitants
> toward the proper order. But the Greater Will is fickle,
> and when Metyr had served her purpose, she was abandoned—
> sealed in the cathedral beneath the finger ruins,
> her body withered, her mind wandering in the abyss between stars."

### On the Greater Will
> "The Greater Will does not love. It does not hate. It simply... ordains.
> It sent the Elden Beast to carry the Elden Ring.
> It sent Metyr to plant the seeds of the Fingers.
> And when these servants were no longer needed,
> it moved on without a backward glance.
> Marika understood this. It is why she shattered the Ring—
> not out of grief alone, but out of rebellion against a god
> that saw her people as instruments."

### On the Finger Ruins
> "These ruins predate the Erdtree. They predate Marika.
> The Fingers were here first—planted by Metyr in an age before memory.
> The Two Fingers guided. The Three Fingers burned.
> Both served the Greater Will, though in different capacities.
> The ruins are a testament to the first order of this world,
> the scaffolding upon which Marika built her golden tyranny."

## Needle Knight Leda — Final Confrontation Dialogue

### Before the Final Boss
> "You've come this far, Tarnished. But Miquella has already ascended.
> Radahn carries him upon his shoulders, a god upon a lord.
> You cannot stop what has already begun.
> The new age dawns. And if you will not kneel before it,
> you will be ground beneath its heel.
> This is not cruelty. This is necessity.
> Every age demands sacrifice. You are simply the last."
