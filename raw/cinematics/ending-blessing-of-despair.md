# Ending: Blessing of Despair

## The Dung Eater's Mending Rune

> (No unique narration. The visual is the darkest and most disturbing of the mending rune endings.)

The Tarnished repairs the Elden Ring using the Mending Rune of the Fell Curse, derived from the Dung Eater's Seedbed Curses. The ring is mended with a pattern that incorporates the Omen curse into its fundamental structure. The Erdtree's light takes on a sickly, cursed quality.

## Visual Description

The throne room scene is visually similar to other mending rune endings but with a distinctly disturbing quality. The light is wrong, tinged with the grey-brown of the Omen curse. The Elden Ring's repair patterns resemble the horn-like growths of the Omen. The Tarnished sits on the throne, but the atmosphere is oppressive rather than triumphant.

The Erdtree itself appears subtly corrupted, its golden light mixed with something darker. The overall visual impression is of a world where something fundamental has been poisoned at the source.

## Lore Implications

The Blessing of Despair is the most morally transgressive ending in the base game. The Dung Eater's curse transforms every being in the Lands Between into Omen: horned, tailed, and cursed. By incorporating this curse into the Elden Ring itself, the Tarnished has made Omen-hood the default state of all life.

The Dung Eater's motivation (revealed through his questline and armor descriptions) stems from a specific logic: the Omen are persecuted because they are different. If everyone is an Omen, the persecution ends. Equality through universal damnation. "If grace is denied to some, let it be denied to all."

This ending can be read multiple ways:

**As horror:** The Tarnished has cursed the entire world with a painful, disfiguring condition. Every person born in the Lands Between will bear horns, tails, and the physical burden of Omen-hood. The Dung Eater was a serial killer and defiler; his vision for the world is an extension of his pathology.

**As dark justice:** The Golden Order persecuted the Omen as cursed abominations. Mohg and Morgott, twin sons of Marika, were cast into sewers because of their Omen features. The Blessing of Despair eliminates the basis for this persecution by making the "curse" universal. It is impossible to oppress Omen when everyone is one.

**As commentary:** This is the ending that most directly confronts the Golden Order's bigotry. By making the most stigmatized condition universal, it forces the world to reckon with what it did to the Omen. The "blessing" in "Blessing of Despair" is unironic from the Dung Eater's perspective: he considers this a gift.

The DLC (Shadow of the Erdtree) expands on cursed and rejected peoples, adding further context to this ending's implications. The horned and tailed beings who were shunned by the Erdtree's order are given cosmic validation: their features are no longer marks of shame but the universal condition.

This ending requires the player to actively assist the Dung Eater, one of the most repugnant NPCs in the game. The player must collect Seedbed Curses (found on defiled corpses) and feed them to the Dung Eater's physical body in the sewers. The process is deliberately unpleasant, ensuring that the player cannot arrive at this ending accidentally. You must choose it, and you must work for it.
