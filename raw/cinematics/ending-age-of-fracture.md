# Ending: Age of Fracture

## The Default Ending

> (No unique narration. The ending uses the base game's visual and musical cues without additional spoken text beyond what is shown.)

The Tarnished approaches the shattered Elden Ring within the Erdtree after defeating the Elden Beast. The fragments of the ring float in the air, broken and scattered. The Tarnished reaches out and reassembles the ring, but without any Mending Rune applied, the repair is imperfect. The ring is restored but remains fractured, its cracks still visible.

The Tarnished sits upon the throne. The camera pulls back to show the Erdtree, still standing but damaged, its light dim and uncertain. The world continues, but the fundamental fracture in reality (the Shattering) has been addressed only superficially. The ring holds, but barely.

## Visual Description

The closing shot shows the Tarnished on the throne, the Erdtree in the background. The golden light is muted compared to the opening cinematic's depiction of the tree. The sky is overcast. The Erdtree's canopy is thinned. Nothing is healed; things are merely held together.

Marika/Radagon's body hangs in the Erdtree above, crucified on the rune arc. The Tarnished becomes Elden Lord not by earning a god's blessing but by defeating everything in the way and sitting in the empty chair. The age that follows is the Age of Fracture: an era where the old order is nominally restored but structurally compromised, the cracks papered over rather than mended.

## Lore Implications

The Age of Fracture is the "nothing changes" ending. The Golden Order persists but in a weakened, imperfect form. The Erdtree still stands. Grace still functions (presumably). The fundamental problems (the Outer Gods' influence, the Omen's persecution, the Albinaurics' exclusion, death's corruption) are unaddressed. The Tarnished has become lord of a broken system and chosen to keep it running as-is.

This ending can be read as pragmatic (the world needs stability, not revolution), cynical (the Tarnished fought through all of this just to maintain the status quo), or as a reflection of the player who engaged with the game's systems without engaging with its themes. The default ending requires no quests, no relationships, no understanding of the world's problems. You simply win and sit down.
