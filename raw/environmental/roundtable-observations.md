# Roundtable Hold — Environmental Observations

## The Hold's Nature (Exists Outside the World)

The Roundtable Hold is not a physical location within the Lands Between. It exists in an extradimensional space, accessible only via teleportation from Sites of Grace. The player is brought here by Melina early in the game and can fast-travel to it from anywhere. The Hold appears to be a grand manor house with multiple rooms, a central round table, a balcony, and a basement, but it has no exterior: every window shows only featureless golden light or void.

The Hold is a construct of the Two Fingers (or the Greater Will) designed as a gathering place for Tarnished champions pursuing the goal of becoming Elden Lord. Its existence outside the world means it is neutral ground, insulated from the conflicts of the Lands Between. No combat is possible in the main areas (attacking NPCs results in an invader, Mad Tongue Alberich, being summoned to punish the aggressor).

The round table at its center is a deliberate reference to Arthurian legend: knights gathered around a table where no seat is higher than another, implying equality of purpose. However, the table is empty. No meeting is ever held. The Tarnished who gather here do not cooperate; they pursue individual agendas, compete, and sometimes kill each other. The round table as a symbol of failed unity is reinforced by Gideon Ofnir's eventual betrayal and the general atmosphere of distrust.

A physical version of the Roundtable Hold exists within Leyndell (the Fortified Manor), confirming that the extradimensional Hold is a copy of a real building. This real version is damaged and empty, and within it the player can find items that mirror the Hold's layout. The existence of both versions raises questions: was the extradimensional Hold copied from the physical one, or vice versa? Did the Tarnished once meet in the real capital, and was the extradimensional copy created when they were exiled?

[Context: The hub area accessible from any Site of Grace, used throughout the game as the central NPC gathering point.]

## The Basement and the Mad Tarnished

Below the main floor of the Roundtable Hold, accessible via a staircase, is a basement area. Here, the player encounters D's brother (a catatonic figure lying on the floor), and, most significantly, the Dung Eater's cell. The Dung Eater is imprisoned in a locked room, seated in a chair, wearing his cursed Omen Mask. He speaks of defiling corpses to prevent their return to the Erdtree, spreading a curse that denies grace to the dead and their descendants.

The Dung Eater's presence in the Roundtable Hold is deeply unsettling. This is supposed to be a sanctuary for Tarnished champions, yet it houses a serial defiler whose explicit goal is to curse as many people as possible. That the other inhabitants tolerate his presence (he is locked up, but no one has killed him, despite his obvious threat) suggests either that they are bound by rules that prevent it, or that the Hold's inhabitants are more morally complex than they appear.

The Dung Eater represents the darkest possible interpretation of the Tarnished condition. If the Tarnished are those rejected by grace and recalled to fight for a new order, the Dung Eater asks: what if I use that second chance to ensure no one ever has grace again? His Blessing of Despair ending (if the player uses his Mending Rune) curses the entire world with Omen-hood, turning the greatest stigma in the Golden Order into a universal condition. It is equality through shared damnation.

Blackguard Big Boggart's questline and the consumption of Seedbed Curses all connect to the Dung Eater's presence in the basement, creating a web of cruelty that operates just beneath the Hold's surface, much like the sewers beneath Leyndell.

[Context: The basement level of the Roundtable Hold, below the main gathering area.]

## The Two Fingers Chamber

At the far end of the Roundtable Hold's upper corridor, behind a set of doors, is the chamber of the Two Fingers. The Two Fingers is a massive, severed hand (specifically, two enormous fingers) that twitches and gestures in cryptic motions. The Finger Reader Enia interprets its movements and communicates its will to the Tarnished. The chamber is spare and dimly lit, with the Two Fingers resting on a table.

The Two Fingers serves as the Greater Will's emissary, the direct link between the Erdtree's cosmic patron and the Tarnished who serve it. Its appearance is deeply uncanny: it is literally two fingers of a giant hand, severed at the knuckle, moving independently with apparent intelligence. It has no face, no voice, and communicates through movement that only Enia can interpret. The player has no way to verify Enia's translations.

As the game progresses, the Two Fingers becomes increasingly unresponsive. After certain events (particularly the burning of the Erdtree), the Two Fingers enters a dormant state, twitching but no longer communicating. Enia describes it as "trying to contact the Greater Will," a process that may take thousands of years. The Greater Will's communication link has broken, and the Two Fingers, the ultimate authority for the Tarnished, has nothing to say.

This silence is one of the game's most important environmental storytelling moments. The god behind the gods has gone quiet. The Two Fingers, which directed the entire Tarnished program (recall Grace, send champions, restore the Elden Ring), is a broken phone line. Whether the Greater Will has abandoned the Lands Between, has been blocked, or simply operates on timescales incompatible with mortal urgency is never resolved.

The Three Fingers beneath Leyndell are the dark mirror of this entity: where the Two Fingers represent order and guidance (even when that guidance goes silent), the Three Fingers represent the dissolution of all things through the Frenzied Flame. Together, the five fingers once formed a single hand. Their separation represents the fundamental schism in the cosmos of Elden Ring.

[Context: The rear chamber of the Roundtable Hold, accessible from the upper corridor past the blacksmith Hewg and the spirit tuner Roderika.]

## The Blacksmith Hewg

Hewg, the blacksmith in the Roundtable Hold, is chained to his workstation. He is a prisoner, bound to the Hold by enchantment and tasked with smithing weapons. He cannot leave. His chain is visible, and he references his imprisonment when spoken to. He was given a single purpose: to forge a weapon capable of killing a god.

Hewg's imprisonment raises uncomfortable questions about the Roundtable Hold as a sanctuary. It is a gathering place for champions, but at least one of its residents is a slave. Hewg's purpose (god-killing weapon) implies that whoever established the Hold anticipated that the Tarnished would eventually need to fight a divine being, which is exactly what happens in the final boss encounter against Radagon/Elden Beast.

At the end of the game, when the Hold begins to crumble (its extradimensional space failing as the Erdtree burns), Hewg loses his memory. He no longer remembers the player but continues smithing, fulfilling his purpose even without the capacity to understand why. His final state is a machine: working without meaning, bound without knowing he is bound.

[Context: The main floor of the Roundtable Hold, at the blacksmith's station near the central table.]

## The Roundtable Hold NPCs and Social Dynamics

The Hold collects a diverse group of Tarnished, each with their own agenda. Gideon Ofnir (the All-Knowing) operates as the de facto leader, gathering information on all demigods. Diallos seeks glory. Corhyn studies the Golden Order's fundamentals. D hunts Those Who Live in Death. Each NPC occupies a specific room or position, and their movements through the game reflect their questlines' progress.

The social dynamic of the Hold is one of polite mutual distrust. NPCs share space but rarely collaborate. Gideon hoards information. D's zealotry leads to his death at Fia's hands (Fia, a Deathbed Companion working against D's anti-death theology, kills him in the Hold itself, one of the only acts of violence in the sanctuary). The veneer of cooperation hides competing and often incompatible agendas.

The Roundtable Hold functions as a microcosm of the Lands Between's political landscape: multiple factions with irreconcilable goals forced into proximity by a shared physical space, held together only by the (increasingly absent) authority of the Two Fingers.
