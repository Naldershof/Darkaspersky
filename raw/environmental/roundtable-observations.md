# Roundtable Hold — Environmental Observations

## Existing Outside Reality

The Roundtable Hold is not a place in the physical Lands Between. There is no location on the map where the Hold exists. The player teleports there via Melina or through Sites of Grace, arriving in a self-contained interior space with no exterior. Looking out windows reveals only golden fog. There are no doors to the outside. The Hold exists in a pocket dimension, an interstitial space between the physical world and the Erdtree's influence.

This non-physical existence raises fundamental questions about the Hold's nature. It was created (or is sustained) by the Two Fingers as a gathering place for Tarnished, meaning it is a construct of the Greater Will's power. Every Tarnished who rests here, who levels up, who interacts with its NPCs, does so in a space that is entirely within the Greater Will's domain. The safety it provides is also, implicitly, surveillance. The Tarnished are gathered, guided, and observed.

The Hold's disconnection from physical space also means it exists outside of time's normal flow, or at least operates on different rules. NPCs appear and disappear as their questlines progress, and the Hold seems to accommodate them regardless of the logical timeline. Multiple Tarnished from different eras or parallel journeys could theoretically coexist here.

[Context: The Roundtable Hold, the hub area accessed through the map menu or via Melina's transport. No physical location on the game map.]

## Mirrored in Leyndell

A physical version of the Roundtable Hold exists in Leyndell. The Fortified Manor, located in the capital city, is architecturally identical to the Hold: the same layout, the same rooms, the same balcony. But the Fortified Manor is empty of NPCs, its rooms populated by enemies instead. Items and lore notes found here suggest this was the original Roundtable Hold, a real place in the real world, before it was somehow duplicated or projected into the pocket dimension the player knows.

The implication is that the Hold the Tarnished use is a copy — a spiritual projection of a building that once existed physically in the capital. The original was abandoned (or repurposed as part of Leyndell's defenses), and the copy persists outside reality, maintained by the Two Fingers' power. This raises the question: is the Hold the Tarnished use a genuine sanctuary, or is it a simulation? A cage made comfortable enough that its inhabitants don't notice the bars?

The Fortified Manor's hostile inhabitants and the loot found there (including the Cipher Pata, a faith weapon formed from incantation) suggest the original Hold's fall was violent. Whatever happened to empty the real Roundtable Hold and necessitate its spectral replacement is unrecorded but implied to have been traumatic.

[Context: The Fortified Manor in Leyndell, Royal Capital. Architecturally identical to the Roundtable Hold.]

## The Two Fingers' Chamber

In the back of the Hold, past the main hall, a chamber houses the Two Fingers: a massive, ancient hand (specifically two enormous fingers) that serves as the Greater Will's emissary. The Finger Reader Enia interprets the Two Fingers' slow, deliberate gestures, translating their guidance for the Tarnished.

The Two Fingers' communication is agonizingly slow. When consulted about the Erdtree's rejection (the thorn barrier), the Two Fingers must "commune with the Greater Will," a process that takes an indeterminate but very long time. Enia remarks on this delay with thinly veiled frustration. The implication is clear: the Greater Will is either unwilling to respond, unable to respond, or so distant that communication takes eons.

This delay is devastating for the game's theological framework. The Tarnished are guided by an emissary that cannot reach its god in any meaningful timeframe. The Two Fingers become a symbol of failed communication — an intermediary whose principal has gone silent. Whether the Greater Will has abandoned the Lands Between, is imprisoned, or simply does not care, the Two Fingers' inability to provide timely guidance means the Tarnished must act on their own.

Eventually, the Two Fingers are found dead or dying in the chamber, curled and desiccated. Their death (or total inactivity) removes even the pretense of divine guidance.

[Context: The rear chamber of the Roundtable Hold, where the Two Fingers sit on a cloth-covered table.]

## Hewg's Chains

Master Hewg, the blacksmith, is chained to his post in the Hold. Literal chains bind him to the area around his anvil. He cannot leave. He smiths for the Tarnished because he has no choice — he is a prisoner, not a servant.

Hewg's stated desire is to smith a weapon capable of killing a god. He was given this task (or gave it to himself) and was bound to the Hold to see it through. His chains are never explained in detail, but the implication is that either the Two Fingers or Marika herself bound him here to ensure the Tarnished would always have a capable blacksmith. His imprisonment is a utility function: the Tarnished need weapons, so a weaponsmith was chained in their hub.

Hewg's memory deteriorates over the course of the game. He begins to forget the player, forget his purpose, forget everything except the act of smithing. By the end, he is a hollow figure, hammering at the anvil out of habit rather than will. His chains have become redundant — he no longer needs to be restrained because he no longer has enough self to flee.

Roderika, who becomes his spirit-tuning student, is the closest thing to genuine human connection Hewg experiences. His gruff protectiveness of her is one of the Hold's few moments of warmth. When the Hold is burning (in certain endings), Hewg refuses to leave, choosing to continue smithing as the world ends.

[Context: The smithing station in the Roundtable Hold, where Hewg works. Located to the left of the main hall.]

## The Basement and the Mad Tarnished

Below the main hall, down a set of stairs, lies a basement area that contains a hostile NPC: a mad Tarnished who attacks on sight. This invader (Mad Tongue Alberich) uses death sorceries and fights aggressively in a room littered with broken furniture and signs of violence.

The basement's hostility within a supposed safe zone is jarring and deliberate. The Roundtable Hold is governed by a rule against violence (enforced by the Hold's magic, which weakens aggressors), but the basement appears to be outside this protection. Alberich's presence suggests that not all Tarnished gathered by the Two Fingers remained sane or cooperative. Some went mad, and they were pushed downstairs rather than expelled — contained within the Hold's pocket dimension because there was nowhere else to put them.

The basement also connects thematically to Leyndell's sewers and the Haligtree's lower rot zones: every sanctuary in Elden Ring has a basement where the unpleasant truths are stored. The Hold's basement is small and its single mad occupant is easily dispatched, but the principle is the same. Even in a non-physical space created by divine will, there is a downstairs where things go wrong.

[Context: The lower level of the Roundtable Hold, accessible via stairs from the main hall.]

## The Balcony Jump

Near the main hall, a balcony overlooks a lower floor. In most circumstances, the balcony is simply a scenic viewpoint. However, the player can jump from this balcony to reach a lower area containing the entrance to the basement and, more significantly, an item on a body far below.

This jump is one of the game's subtle environmental puzzles. The Hold discourages violence through magical enforcement, and its layout seems designed to prevent exploration beyond the intended areas. The balcony jump subverts this — it is a player-initiated act of transgression, breaking the Hold's intended boundaries through physical action (jumping) rather than combat.

The body at the bottom of the jump holds the Coded Sword, a faith weapon whose description references the Golden Order's "proper death." Finding it requires literally leaping from the Hold's safe upper areas into its hidden lower ones. The metaphor is consistent: to find truth in the Lands Between, you must be willing to fall.

[Context: The balcony area of the Roundtable Hold, near the hallway leading to the Two Fingers' chamber.]

## The NPCs as a Microcosm

The Roundtable Hold's roster of NPCs functions as a compressed version of the Lands Between's social dynamics. Gideon Ofnir studies and manipulates. Diallos dithers with noble intentions. Fia secretly serves death. D hunts the undead. Rogier investigates forbidden knowledge. Corhyn follows orthodox faith. Ensha lurks silently until triggered into violence.

As the game progresses, the Hold empties. NPCs leave for their questlines, die, go mad, or simply vanish. The Hold's population shrinks as the world's crisis deepens, mirroring the Lands Between's broader depopulation. By the endgame, the Hold is nearly empty — Hewg hammers alone, the Two Fingers are silent or dead, and the golden fog outside the windows feels less like protection and more like a shroud.

The Hold begins as a place of community and ends as a place of isolation. This arc mirrors the Tarnished's journey: they arrive seeking guidance and allies, and they end alone, facing the Elden Ring with no one left to advise them. The Hold's emptiness is the game's quiet answer to its central question: in the end, every decision about what the world becomes rests with you, and no one is left to tell you what to choose.
