# Liurnia of the Lakes — Environmental Observations

## Academy of Raya Lucaria

The Academy of Raya Lucaria sits on a massive island in the center of Liurnia's lake, sealed behind a glintstone barrier that can only be passed with an Academy Glintstone Key. The seal itself is a statement: the Academy has locked itself away from the world. After the Shattering, the scholars rejected the outside entirely, turning inward.

Inside, the Academy is a sprawling complex of lecture halls, libraries, courtyards, and waterwheel-powered mechanisms. Glintstone sorcerers patrol every corridor. The architecture features celestial motifs (stars, moons, and constellations carved into stone), reflecting the Academy's foundational connection to the stars and the cosmic understanding that predates the Erdtree. The massive waterwheel that powers the Academy's mechanisms is a marvel of engineering, suggesting that sorcery and technology are intertwined in Liurnian culture.

The scholars encountered as enemies are in various stages of transformation. Some have glintstone growths emerging from their bodies, particularly their heads. This is the endpoint of glintstone study taken to extremes: the sorcerers literally become crystallized, their flesh converting to the same material they study. The glintstone crowns worn by senior sorcerers are not merely hats; they appear fused to the skulls beneath. This transformation mirrors a broader theme in Elden Ring where the pursuit of power literally changes the body.

The graveyard outside the academy contains tombstones for students, and spirit ashes throughout describe scholars who died in magical experiments or academic rivalries. The Academy is not merely a school; it is a place of intense and often fatal competition.

[Context: Central Liurnia, the massive island structure dominating the lake. The Academy is the legacy dungeon of the region.]

## The Debate Parlor and Rennala

Rennala, Queen of the Full Moon, is found in the Academy's grand library (the Debate Parlor in its second phase). She sits on the floor, clutching a glowing amber egg, surrounded by her juvenile scholars who crawl on the floor and ceiling, chanting and singing. The room is filled with scattered books, overturned furniture, and candelabras. The scholars' song and their protective behavior toward Rennala has an eerie, lullaby-like quality.

Rennala is no longer in command of the Academy. The scholars who surround her appear to be tending to her in a motherly way, but they also keep her contained. She has retreated into grief and obsession with rebirth after Radagon left her to become Marika's consort. The amber egg she holds is a gift from Radagon, and she uses it to endlessly attempt rebirth of her children (or perhaps to rebirth something she has lost).

In the second phase of the fight, Rennala's power is revealed as an illusion. She herself is frail and passive; the true threat comes from the spectral projection of Rennala at full power, summoned by the egg. This spectral Rennala is how she was before her decline, a devastating full-moon sorceress. The contrast between the broken woman on the floor and the magnificent specter she once was is the entire story of Liurnia's decline told in a single boss encounter.

After the fight, Rennala remains alive (unique among shardbearers), sitting peacefully with her egg, offering the player the rebirth mechanic. She is not an enemy; she is a grieving mother.

[Context: The grand library at the top of the Academy of Raya Lucaria.]

## Carian Manor

Carian Manor sits at the northern edge of Liurnia, atop a cliff, and serves as the seat of the Carian royal family. The manor is defended by massive spectral hands that burst from the ground, grasping at intruders. These hands are a Carian defensive enchantment, and their sheer number and aggression indicate a family that expected and feared assault.

Inside, the manor is opulent but decaying. Carian knight armor stands in rows, but the knights themselves are largely absent or have become hostile spirits. The manor's gardens are overgrown and infested with giant spiders (actually transformed Carian servants, as suggested by the finger spider enemies and the Carian study hall). Pidia, a Carian servant, hides in a sealed room accessible only from above, terrified of the puppet soldiers that patrol the manor.

Preceptor Miriam and other spectral defenders haunt the Carian Study Hall, the bridge-tower connecting to the Divine Tower of Liurnia. The study hall is inverted when Ranni's questline progresses, literally turning the architecture upside down to reveal a hidden path. This inversion is both a mechanical puzzle and a metaphorical statement about the Carian family's relationship with conventional power structures.

Royal Knight Loretta guards the manor's innermost courtyard, a spectral knight still loyal to the family. Her presence as a lingering guardian, fighting long after the family has scattered, speaks to the Carian knights' legendary devotion. In reality, Loretta has gone to the Haligtree to find a home for the Albinaurics, and what remains here is only her spirit echo.

[Context: Northwestern Liurnia, the elevated manor complex. Gateway to the Three Sisters area where Ranni's Rise is located.]

## The Three Sisters and Ranni's Rise

Beyond Carian Manor, three towers stand on a cliff plateau: Ranni's Rise, Renna's Rise, and Seluvis's Rise. Ranni's Rise is the tallest and is where Ranni the Witch holds court with her followers (Blaidd, Iji, and Seluvis). The area is cold, wind-swept, and overlooking Liurnia from a great height. The towers are architecturally distinct from the manor, appearing older and more austere.

Ranni occupies the top of her tower in the form of a doll (her true body having been discarded when she shed her Empyrean flesh). The presence of a miniature Ranni doll at the top of a lonely tower, attended by a half-wolf warrior, a giant blacksmith, and a scheming puppeteer, creates an image of a deposed princess operating an insurgency from exile. The doll form itself is significant: Ranni chose to inhabit a witch's body made by the snow witch who taught her, rejecting her divine flesh entirely.

Seluvis's Rise contains a secret puppet chamber beneath it, hidden behind an illusory floor, where he keeps human-turned-puppet victims including a copy of Sellen. This hidden atrocity operating within Ranni's inner circle demonstrates that even the "rebel" faction has darkness within it.

[Context: The plateau beyond Carian Manor in far northwestern Liurnia.]

## Divine Towers of Liurnia

The Divine Tower of Liurnia is accessible via the Carian Study Hall and requires inverting the hall using a special item from Ranni's questline. The tower itself is unique among divine towers because it contains Ranni's discarded body rather than a mechanism to activate a Great Rune.

Ranni's corpse is found at the top, slumped in a chair, bearing the wound of the blade of death. This is the physical evidence of the Night of the Black Knives: Ranni orchestrated the assassination of Godwyn's soul using the Rune of Death, and simultaneously used a fragment of that same rune to kill her own body, severing herself from the Erdtree's influence. The body is intact but clearly dead, preserved in an almost mummified state. The positioning at the top of her family's divine tower feels deliberate: she placed her mortality at the highest point of Carian power and walked away.

[Context: The Divine Tower accessible via the Carian Study Hall bridge in western Liurnia.]

## The Albinaurics and the Village of the Albinaurics

The Village of the Albinaurics lies beneath a cliff in southwestern Liurnia, half-submerged in the swampy shallows. The first-generation Albinaurics here are largely slaughtered, their bodies scattered across the village. Some survive in hiding. The second-generation Albinaurics (the rotund, frog-like figures) are found throughout Liurnia serving as laborers and soldiers for various factions.

The Albinaurics are artificial beings created (likely by the Nox of the eternal cities or related groups) who are not blessed by the Erdtree and cannot receive grace. First-generation Albinaurics have functional human-like bodies but their legs eventually fail them, leaving them immobile. Second-generation Albinaurics are cruder creations, seemingly mass-produced.

The massacre at the village was carried out by Gideon Ofnir's forces (specifically the Omenkiller) in search of the right half of the Haligtree Secret Medallion, which Albus (disguised as a pot) carries. The slaughter of an entire village of marginalized beings to obtain a key to the Haligtree reflects the ruthless pragmatism of the Roundtable Hold's leadership and the expendability of those outside the Erdtree's grace.

Latenna, a first-generation Albinauric found nearby, is one of the last survivors. Her wolf companion lies dead, and she herself cannot walk. Her quest to reach the Haligtree and bring the Albinaurics' birthright (a giant Albinauric woman at the Apostate Derelict in the Consecrated Snowfield) to Miquella's refuge is one of the game's most poignant storylines about seeking a homeland.

[Context: Southwestern Liurnia, beneath the cliffside south of the Academy. A hidden village accessible via a cave.]

## The Carian Royal Family's Decline

The environmental storytelling across all of Liurnia paints a picture of a once-great dynasty in terminal decline. The Carian royal family (Queen Rennala, King Consort Radagon, and their children Ranni, Radahn, and Rykard) once ruled both through sorcerous might and political alliance with the Golden Order. When Radagon left Rennala to become Marika's consort, the political alliance shattered.

The Academy, which once served the Carians, rebelled and sealed itself. Carian Manor is infested with spider-transformed servants. The Three Sisters towers are isolated outposts. Ranni abandoned her body. Radahn went mad in Caelid. Rykard fed himself to a serpent. Rennala sits broken in her enemies' library.

The physical decay of every Carian-associated location reinforces this narrative. Nowhere in Liurnia is there a functioning, healthy institution. The lake itself is grey and desolate, dotted with ruins and crawling with lobsters and lesser creatures. The beauty that Liurnia once possessed (implied by the spectacular architecture, the magical infrastructure, the extensive libraries) has curdled into melancholy.

The Cuckoo Knight armor and the various Academy-related items describe a military and academic complex that turned on its royal sponsors. The Academy's refusal to acknowledge Rennala's authority even as she sits physically within their building is perhaps the most pointed symbol of this decline.

[Context: General observation applicable across all of Liurnia of the Lakes.]

## The Ruins and Risen Architecture

Liurnia's lake is dotted with partially submerged ruins and the remains of structures that suggest the water level was once much lower or the land has sunk. Churches, walls, and towers protrude from the shallow lake at odd angles. The Laskyar Ruins, Rose Church, and other sites stand in ankle-deep water, their foundations eroded.

This flooding may be connected to the Shattering's physical impact on the landscape or to a longer-term decline predating even Marika's reign. The Academy's island position, elevated above the waters, makes it a literal ivory tower looking down on the drowned remains of the civilization it was meant to serve.

The presence of lobsters (aggressive, oversized crustaceans) throughout the lake suggests an ecosystem that has adapted to or been warped by the changed conditions. They are disproportionately dangerous compared to their apparent position in the food chain, a recurring environmental joke in the community but also an indicator of how magic-saturated environments produce aberrant wildlife.

[Context: The lake floor and shallows throughout Liurnia, particularly visible when traveling between landmarks.]
