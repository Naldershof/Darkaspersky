# Fia, Deathbed Companion

> Fia embraces champions to steal their dying warmth, hoarding vitality in service of a single goal: to birth a rune that restores Destined Death to the Elden Ring and gives Those Who Live in Death a rightful place in the world.

## Overview

Fia is found in a bedchamber at the Roundtable Hold, offering her embrace to any Tarnished who will accept it. That embrace is a transaction -- the player receives a Baldachin's Blessing but loses a fraction of their maximum HP. She is a Deathbed Companion, a role that existed within the Golden Order to comfort dying champions. Fia has repurposed this role: she collects vitality not for the Order but for Those Who Live in Death.

Her questline takes her from the Roundtable Hold to the Deeproot Depths, where Godwyn the Golden's soulless body festers at the roots of the Erdtree. There, she lies with the Death-Prince and births the Mending Rune of the Death-Prince -- a rune that, when used, restores the principle of Destined Death to the Elden Ring.

## Lore Details

**The Deathbed Companion role.** In the Golden Order, Deathbed Companions lay with champions before their deaths, absorbing their residual life force. Fia has turned this sanctioned role into a weapon against the Order's central doctrine: the prohibition of death.

**D, Hunter of the Dead.** D is Fia's ideological opposite. He hunts Those Who Live in Death as abominations, enforcing the Golden Order's decree. Fia gives the player a Weathered Dagger to return to D -- a setup. When D confronts her, she kills him with the accumulated vitality of every champion she has embraced. D's twin brother later avenges him by killing Fia in her sleep.

**Godwyn the Death-Prince.** Godwyn was the first demigod slain during the Night of the Black Knives. His soul was killed, but his body survived -- a half-death that created Deathroot and spawned Those Who Live in Death. His corpse grows into the Erdtree's roots, a cancer at the heart of the Golden Order. Fia seeks to complete his death, to give him the ending the Black Knives denied.

**Lichdragon Fortissax.** An ancient dragon who loved Godwyn and entered his death dream to fight the corruption consuming him. Fortissax failed and became a Lichdragon -- trapped in the dream, spreading Deathblight. The player must defeat Fortissax to obtain the cursemark needed for Fia's Mending Rune.

## Connections & Analysis

**Fia's ending is the most "natural" resolution.** The Golden Order's foundational act was the removal of Destined Death from the Elden Ring. This made demigods immortal but also created an unnatural stasis -- nothing could truly die, and those caught between life and death became persecuted outcasts. Fia's Mending Rune reverses this single change. The [Age of Duskborn](../endings/age-of-duskborn.md) does not replace the Golden Order or destroy it; it simply restores the cycle of life and death. Of all the endings, it changes the least while fixing the most fundamental problem.

**The murder of D is morally complex.** Fia kills D not out of malice but out of necessity -- she needs the accumulated vitality and the confrontation forces her hand. D is not wrong to hunt the undead by the Golden Order's laws, and Fia is not wrong to seek death's restoration. The game refuses to adjudicate between them.

**Fia's questline intersects with [Rogier's](rogier.md).** Rogier discovers Godwyn's corpse beneath Stormveil Castle -- a smaller manifestation of the same body Fia seeks in the Deeproot Depths. Rogier contracts death-blight from his investigation and dies, his research passing to the player. His work is the entry point; Fia's quest is the conclusion.

**Those Who Live in Death are the Golden Order's original sin.** By sealing away Destined Death, Marika created a world where death was incomplete. The undead are not aberrations -- they are symptoms of a broken system. The Golden Order then persecutes them for existing, compounding injustice with cruelty. Fia sees this clearly.

**The cycle of vengeance.** Fia kills D. D's brother kills Fia. The Mending Rune persists regardless. The personal vendetta is rendered meaningless by the cosmic scale of what Fia achieved -- her work outlives her, and her killer's revenge changes nothing about the outcome.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "Was Fia always working toward this goal, or did she discover it?"
    Her dialogue suggests a long-held purpose, but it is unclear whether she was born a Deathbed Companion with this specific mission or whether she chose it after encountering Those Who Live in Death.

!!! question "What is the Weathered Dagger?"
    The dagger Fia gives the player to return to D may be a Black Knife or a weapon touched by Destined Death. Its origins are never explained.

!!! question "Does the Age of Duskborn truly solve the problem?"
    Restoring Destined Death means demigods can die. But the Erdtree's corruption from Godwyn's body has already spread. Can the damage be undone, or only halted?
