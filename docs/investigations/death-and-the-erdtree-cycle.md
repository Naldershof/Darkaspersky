# Death and the Erdtree Cycle — What Broke and Why It Matters

> Death in the Lands Between is not a philosophical abstraction — it is a mechanical system. Marika broke it by removing Destined Death from the Elden Ring. Everything that followed — Godwyn's growing corpse, the Deathroot, Those Who Live in Death, the undying horror of the Shattering — is a consequence of that single act. Understanding the death cycle is the key to understanding why the world is ruined and which ending can fix it.

## The Question

What happens when a being dies in the Lands Between? The game presents multiple death-related systems — the Erdtree's cycle, the Rune of Death, the Deathroot, spirit summoning, Those Who Live in Death — that seem contradictory until mapped as stages of a single broken process. By cross-referencing every death-related source in the wiki, we can reconstruct how death *should* work, how it *currently* works, and what each ending does about it.

## The Original Cycle (Before Marika's Intervention)

### The Pre-Erdtree Death Cycle

Before the [Greater Will](../lore-concepts/the-greater-will.md) arrived, death followed different patterns:

- **The Ancestral Followers** (Siofra River) worship through spirit-animal rituals. Their dead become spirit ancestors, visible as ghostly deer and other creatures. Death is not an ending but a transformation into a spiritual ecology.
- **The Nox civilization** practiced death rituals connected to the stars. Their Eternal Cities sit among the roots of the [Greattree](erdtree-greattree-identity.md), suggesting an older root-network handled the dead.
- **The Death Rite Birds** "served the original form of the Erdtree, before the establishment of the Golden Order." They carried the dead in a cycle that predates the Erdtree's golden age.
- **Placidusax's era** had its own divine order and presumably its own death cycle, now lost.

### The Erdtree Death Cycle (Golden Order Era)

Under the [Golden Order](../lore-concepts/the-golden-order.md), death was systematized:

1. A being dies
2. The soul returns to the [Erdtree](../lore-concepts/the-erdtree.md) via its roots
3. The Erdtree absorbs and recycles the soul
4. The soul is eventually returned to the world in new life

This cycle is described in Golden Order incantations and the Erdtree's Favor talisman. It's a closed loop: the Erdtree is both graveyard and womb. Grace guides living beings, and the root system guides dead ones.

**The Rune of Death's role:** [Destined Death](../lore-concepts/destined-death.md) was originally part of the Elden Ring — one rune among many. It governed the *finality* of death. With the Rune in place, death was complete and irreversible: souls went to the Erdtree and stayed dissolved. Death was an ending, not a state.

## What Marika Changed

[Marika](../demigods/marika-radagon.md) extracted the Rune of Death from the Elden Ring and bound it within [Maliketh's](../locations/farum-azula.md) Black Blade. This act had immediate and far-reaching consequences:

**What she intended (probably):** Without Destined Death, demigods and gods cannot truly die. Marika made herself and her children immortal. A god who cannot die cannot be replaced — this may have been Marika's insurance against the Greater Will discarding her for a new vessel.

**What actually happened:** Removing death from the cosmic equation didn't just protect the divine. It *broke the entire death cycle for everything alive*. Without finality, the system clogs:

- Souls return to the Erdtree but cannot fully dissolve
- Bodies persist beyond their time
- The boundary between life and death becomes porous
- Beings that should be dead linger in an undead state

## The Consequences

### Those Who Live in Death

The most visible consequence. These beings — skeletons, revenants, spectral warriors — are souls that failed to complete the death cycle. They returned to the Erdtree's roots, couldn't dissolve (because the finality mechanism is gone), and were pushed back out in corrupted forms.

[D, Hunter of the Dead](../npcs/rogier.md) hunts them as abominations against the Golden Order. [Fia](../npcs/fia.md) protects them as victims of Marika's tampering. Both are correct: they are abominations *created by the system that condemns them*.

### The Godwyn Problem

[Godwyn the Golden](../lore-concepts/destined-death.md) was killed on the Night of the Black Knives — but only his soul died. His body persisted. This created the worst possible outcome: a soulless divine body still connected to the Erdtree's root system, growing like a cancer through the underground.

Godwyn's face appears beneath Stormveil Castle. His body spreads through Deeproot Depths. The Deathroot growing from his corpse corrupts everything it touches — [Gurranq/Maliketh](../locations/farum-azula.md) is driven mad consuming it.

Godwyn's half-death is the proof that Marika's removal of Destined Death didn't just pause death — it *corrupted the infrastructure of death itself*. When a fragment of Destined Death was finally used (stolen from Maliketh by Ranni's conspirators), it produced not clean death but a grotesque half-state that poisons the entire root network.

### The Deathroot

Deathroot grows from Godwyn's corpse and spreads through the roots connecting all major areas. It is, functionally, death-corruption flowing through the Erdtree's circulatory system. Every Minor Erdtree, every Site of Grace, every root-connected location is potentially contaminated.

Gurranq/Maliketh consumes Deathroot in what appears to be penance — the beast who guards Destined Death is trying to clean up the mess that Destined Death's removal caused. He consumes the corruption, but it drives him mad. Even the guardian of death cannot contain the consequences.

### The Tibia Mariners

Skeletal boatmen found in swamps and waterways, summoning the undead. They are the Lands Between's version of Charon — ferrymen of the dead. But they're ferrying souls with nowhere to go. The death cycle's destination (full dissolution in the Erdtree) no longer works, so the mariners endlessly carry the dead in circles.

### Spirit Summoning

The player can summon spirits of the dead to fight using Spirit Ashes. [Roderika](../npcs/rogier.md) can "tune" these spirits, strengthening them. The entire spirit-summoning mechanic is possible *because* death is broken — the dead persist in a usable form rather than dissolving. The Tarnished's most powerful tool is a direct product of the death cycle's failure.

## Cross-Reference Analysis

### The Three Death Systems

Cross-referencing all sources reveals three overlapping death systems:

| System | Era | How It Works | Status |
|--------|-----|-------------|--------|
| **Ancestral/Stellar** | Pre-Erdtree | Dead become spirit-animals, guided by starlight | Still functioning in Siofra/Ainsel (isolated from the Erdtree) |
| **Greattree roots** | Pre-Golden Order | Dead dissolve into root network, nourish the cycle | Corrupted by Godwyn's corpse; roots now spread Deathroot |
| **Erdtree/Golden Order** | Golden Order | Dead return to the Erdtree via Grace, dissolve, are reborn | Broken by Destined Death's removal; souls cannot fully dissolve |

The underground civilizations (Siofra, Ainsel) still have functioning death cycles because they're disconnected from the Erdtree. The Ancestral Followers' spirits are at peace — they transformed cleanly into spiritual forms. Above ground, where the Erdtree governs, death is broken.

### Why Marika's Action Was Catastrophic

The Rune of Death wasn't just about killing things. It governed the *finality* of every process in the Elden Ring's jurisdiction. Without it:

- The dead accumulate rather than cycling
- Diseases (like the Scarlet Rot) can spread without the natural check of death
- Demigods become functionally immortal, enabling the Shattering to become an eternal stalemate
- The Erdtree's root system clogs with un-dissolved souls, eventually producing Deathroot when Godwyn's half-death catalyzes the corruption

Every major problem in the game — the undead, the rot's spread, the Shattering's stalemate, the Deathroot, Godwyn's growing corpse — is downstream of this single act.

### Which Ending Fixes It?

| Ending | Does It Fix Death? |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Age of Fracture** | No. The Ring is mended as-is — Destined Death remains removed. |
| **Age of Order** | No. Perfects the Golden Order's structure but doesn't restore the removed rune. |
| **Age of Duskborn** | **Yes.** Fia's Mending Rune of the Death-Prince weaves Destined Death back into the Ring. The death cycle is restored. |
| **Age of Stars** | Unclear. Removes the Erdtree's influence entirely. Death may function differently under the Dark Moon, but the game doesn't specify. |
| **Blessing of Despair** | No. Changes who is cursed, not how death works. |
| **Lord of Frenzied Flame** | Technically yes — everything dies when everything burns. Not a fix; an amputation. |

Only the Age of Duskborn directly addresses the death cycle's corruption. Fia's rune doesn't just restore death — it legitimizes Those Who Live in Death, acknowledging them as products of a broken system rather than sinners.

## Proposed Model of Death in the Lands Between

Synthesizing all evidence:

```
ORIGINAL (Pre-Erdtree):
  Being dies → soul enters root network → dissolves → nourishes new life
  (Rune of Death provides finality; process is clean and complete)

GOLDEN ORDER (Post-Marika's intervention):
  Being dies → soul enters Erdtree roots → CANNOT dissolve (no Rune of Death)
  → soul accumulates → eventually pushed back out as undead/spirit
  → Those Who Live in Death, spirit ashes, revenants

POST-GODWYN (After the Night of Black Knives):
  All of the above PLUS:
  → Godwyn's soulless body grows through roots
  → Deathroot spreads corruption through the system
  → Even the accumulation process fails — death becomes actively toxic

AGE OF DUSKBORN (Fia's ending):
  Rune of Death restored → finality returns → death cycle resumes
  → Those Who Live in Death are acknowledged, not persecuted
  → Godwyn's corruption can potentially be addressed
  → The system heals
```

## Verdict

**Marika's removal of Destined Death is the original sin of the Elden Ring's world.** Every horror in the game — the undead, the Deathroot, the Shattering's stalemate, the impossibility of peace — traces back to this single act. She broke the most fundamental natural process to make herself immortal, and the consequences corrupted everything.

The Age of Duskborn is the only ending that directly restores the broken cycle. It doesn't fix everything (the Omen persecution, the Golden Order's political crimes, the Land of Shadow), but it fixes the foundational *metaphysical* problem that generated most of the world's suffering. Fia's sacrifice — embracing the corpse of the death that went wrong to create a rune that makes death go right — is the most symbolically appropriate resolution in the game.

## Remaining Gaps

!!! question "Did Marika know removing Destined Death would cause all this?"
    If she foresaw the consequences, her act was even more calculated — she deliberately created a broken world to engineer the conditions for its eventual destruction. If she didn't foresee it, she is a god who didn't understand the system she governed.

!!! question "Can Godwyn's corruption be reversed?"
    Even in the Age of Duskborn, Godwyn's soulless body persists in the root system. Restoring Destined Death might allow his body to finally die — or it might be too deeply embedded. The Deathroot may be permanent damage.

!!! question "What happens to spirit summoning if death is fixed?"
    If the dead dissolve properly, spirit ashes should cease to function. The player's most powerful tool is a symptom of the disease they're trying to cure.

!!! question "Was there a death cycle before the Greater Will arrived?"
    The Ancestral Followers and Death Rite Birds suggest yes — an older, possibly healthier cycle that didn't require the Erdtree. The Age of Duskborn restores death within the Erdtree system, but the pre-Erdtree cycle may have been better. This connects to the [Erdtree/Greattree investigation](erdtree-greattree-identity.md).
