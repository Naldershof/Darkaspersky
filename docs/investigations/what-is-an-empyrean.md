# What Is an Empyrean?

> An Empyrean is not merely a powerful demigod — it is a body prepared for godhood, selected by the Two Fingers, paired with a shadow designed to destroy it if it disobeys, and ultimately intended to replace Marika as the vessel of the Elden Ring. The Empyrean system is not divine favor. It is a succession mechanism built on surveillance and control.

## The Question

"Empyrean" is one of Elden Ring's most frequently used and least explained terms. Three characters are confirmed Empyreans: [Malenia](../demigods/malenia.md), [Miquella](../demigods/miquella.md), and [Ranni](../demigods/ranni.md). [Marika](../demigods/marika-radagon.md) is implied to have been one. The [Gloam-Eyed Queen](../lore-concepts/the-gloam-eyed-queen.md) is described as one in the Godslayer's Seal. But what does the title actually mean? What makes someone an Empyrean? And why does the system that chooses them also prepare to kill them?

## The Confirmed Empyreans

### Marika — The Sitting God

Marika is the vessel of the Elden Ring, the god-queen of the Golden Order. She was chosen by the [Greater Will](../lore-concepts/the-greater-will.md) (via the Two Fingers) as its mortal vessel. Her shadow is [Maliketh](../locations/farum-azula.md) — the Black Blade, a beast clergyman bound to guard Destined Death. Marika is what an Empyrean *becomes* if the process succeeds: a god fused with the Elden Ring, governing reality itself.

### Ranni — The Defector

[Ranni](../demigods/ranni.md) is the daughter of Rennala and Radagon (who is Marika). She was chosen by the Two Fingers and given [Blaidd](../npcs/blaidd.md) as her shadow. She rejected the role utterly — killing her own Empyrean body on the Night of the Black Knives, transferring her soul to a doll, and pursuing the [Age of Stars](../endings/age-of-stars.md). Ranni proves Empyreans can refuse.

### Malenia — The Unwilling Vessel

[Malenia](../demigods/malenia.md) is the daughter of Marika and Radagon (a self-union). She is cursed with the Scarlet Rot from birth — chosen not only by the Two Fingers as an Empyrean but also claimed by the [Outer God of Rot](../lore-concepts/outer-god-of-rot.md) as its vessel. Her shadow is unknown (possibly the Cleanrot Knights serve a similar function). Malenia demonstrates the Empyrean's vulnerability: if an Outer God other than the Greater Will claims the vessel, the succession plan is corrupted.

### Miquella — The Self-Made God

[Miquella](../demigods/miquella.md), Malenia's twin, is also a child of the Marika self-union. His Empyrean nature is complicated by his "unalloyed" quality — he resists Outer God influence, making him the only Empyrean who could potentially serve as a truly independent god. The DLC reveals he chose to [shed his humanity](../dlc/miquella-journey.md) to ascend on his own terms, bypassing the Two Fingers entirely.

### The Gloam-Eyed Queen — The Predecessor

The Godslayer's Seal describes the Gloam-Eyed Queen as an Empyrean who wielded [Destined Death](../lore-concepts/destined-death.md). She turned her power against the Greater Will's order, leading the [Godskin Apostles](../lore-concepts/the-gloam-eyed-queen.md) in a crusade against the gods. Maliketh defeated her. She is the proof that Empyreans have rebelled before Ranni — and that the shadow system exists specifically to prevent it from succeeding.

## Cross-Reference Analysis

### What Makes Someone Empyrean?

The game never gives a single definition. But cross-referencing all five cases reveals a pattern:

**Common traits:**
- Born of divine or semi-divine lineage (children of Marika, or chosen from among the Numen)
- Selected by the Two Fingers specifically
- Assigned a shadow (a bestowed companion who is also a kill-switch)
- Possess some form of exceptional power or quality
- Female-bodied or androgynous (Malenia, Ranni, the Gloam-Eyed Queen are female; Miquella is eternally childlike and androgynous; Marika can become Radagon)

**The gender pattern is striking.** [Gideon Ofnir](../npcs/rogier.md) states that Empyreans are "chosen by the Two Fingers from among the demigods," but the game's confirmed Empyreans suggest a narrower selection: specifically, beings born of a single god (Malenia and Miquella are children of Marika's self-union) or beings with unique divine potential. The emphasis on female/androgynous bodies may connect to the vessel metaphor — the Empyrean is a *container* for the Elden Ring, and the game consistently frames them in terms of receptivity rather than agency.

### The Shadow System — Surveillance as Succession

Every Empyrean gets a shadow. This is not a gift. Cross-referencing Blaidd's and Maliketh's fates reveals the system's true nature:

| Shadow | Empyrean | What Happened |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| Maliketh | Marika | Marika betrayed the Greater Will; Maliketh guards Destined Death in exile at Farum Azula, "betrayed" by "she" |
| Blaidd | Ranni | Ranni defied the Two Fingers; Blaidd went mad, driven to attack the Empyrean he loved |
| Unknown | Malenia | Malenia was claimed by the Rot God; no confirmed shadow, but the Cleanrot Knights' absolute loyalty may serve this function |
| Unknown | Miquella | Miquella shed his humanity; no confirmed shadow (possibly Leda fills an informal version of this role) |
| Unknown | Gloam-Eyed Queen | The Queen attacked the Greater Will; defeated by Maliketh (Marika's shadow, not her own — possibly because her own shadow had already failed) |

**The pattern:** Shadows are programmed to serve the Greater Will, not the Empyrean. When the Empyrean acts within the Greater Will's plan, the shadow is a loyal protector. When the Empyrean rebels, the shadow becomes the weapon that destroys them. This is not a bug in the system — it's the system's entire purpose. The [Two Fingers](../lore-concepts/the-fingers.md) don't trust their own chosen candidates.

### Why Does the Greater Will Distrust Its Own Candidates?

This is the investigation's central paradox. The Greater Will selects Empyreans as potential gods — then embeds kill-switches in their closest companions. Why?

**Theory: The Greater Will has been betrayed before.**

The timeline supports this:

1. The Gloam-Eyed Queen was an Empyrean who turned Destined Death against the gods
2. After her defeat, the system was tightened — Marika's shadow (Maliketh) was given Destined Death to guard specifically because the previous Empyrean had weaponized it
3. Ranni still found a way to steal Destined Death and rebel
4. Miquella bypassed the system entirely by shedding his Empyrean nature

Each generation of Empyreans finds a new way to rebel, and each time the system adds a new safeguard that the next generation circumvents. The Empyrean system is an arms race between the Greater Will's need for mortal vessels and mortal vessels' tendency to develop their own will.

### Empyrean as Vessel — The Elden Ring Connection

The crucial insight: an Empyrean is not just a powerful being. It is a body *capable of holding the Elden Ring*. Marika is "the vessel" — her body contains the Ring, and when she shatters it, the shards scatter to the demigods. The Empyrean designation marks a being whose body could physically contain and govern the metaphysical law of reality.

This explains why the Greater Will cares about succession. If Marika is destroyed or rebels, the Ring needs a new vessel. The Empyreans are backup containers. The shadows exist to ensure the backup containers don't develop independent ambitions before they're needed.

It also explains Miquella's DLC strategy. To become a god *without* the Greater Will's permission, he needed to transform his body into something that could hold divine power independently. That's why he shed his human qualities at each cross — he was rebuilding himself as a vessel, but for his own divinity rather than the Greater Will's Ring.

### The Outer God Problem

Multiple Empyreans are claimed by Outer Gods other than the Greater Will:

- Malenia → Outer God of Rot
- Ranni → The Dark Moon
- The Gloam-Eyed Queen → possibly connected to a death-associated Outer God
- Miquella → none (his "unalloyed" nature resists Outer God influence)

This creates a competition. The Greater Will selects Empyreans as its vessels, but other Outer Gods recognize the same potential and try to claim those vessels for themselves. Malenia was supposed to be the Greater Will's backup; instead, the Rot God turned her into its own weapon. Ranni was supposed to be a successor to Marika; instead, the Dark Moon offered her an alternative.

**Miquella's unique position:** His resistance to Outer God influence makes him the "purest" Empyrean — the only one who could serve as a vessel without being corrupted by a competing cosmic force. This is likely why both the Two Fingers and Mohg (via the Formless Mother) were so interested in him: he's the only unclaimed vessel. And it's why his DLC arc is so significant — he's the only Empyrean who could potentially ascend *independently* of any Outer God, becoming something entirely new.

### Marika as the Template

If we reconstruct Marika's history through the Empyrean lens:

1. Marika was chosen as an Empyrean from among the Numen (a people from outside the Lands Between)
2. She was assigned Maliketh as her shadow
3. She was made the vessel of the Elden Ring — the Empyrean system's "success case"
4. She ruled as god-queen for an age
5. She grew disillusioned and rebelled — shattering the very Ring she was meant to contain
6. Her shadow (Maliketh) was unable to prevent it because she had already exiled him to Farum Azula
7. The Greater Will punished her by crucifying her within the Erdtree

Marika's entire arc is the story of what happens when the Empyrean system works as intended — and then fails. She was the perfect vessel until she wasn't. Every subsequent Empyrean (Ranni, Malenia, Miquella, the Gloam-Eyed Queen) represents a different failure mode of the same system.

## Verdict

**An Empyrean is a body prepared to be a god — specifically, a vessel capable of containing the Elden Ring or an equivalent divine power.** The term describes a biological and metaphysical *capacity*, not a social role or honor. The Two Fingers select candidates based on this capacity, assign shadows to monitor them, and maintain them as backup vessels in case the current god (Marika) fails.

The system is inherently unstable because the same qualities that make someone capable of holding divine power — exceptional will, divine lineage, cosmic potential — also make them capable of refusing to serve. Every Empyrean in the game's history has either rebelled (Ranni, the Gloam-Eyed Queen, Marika herself), been corrupted by a rival Outer God (Malenia), or attempted independent ascension (Miquella). The Greater Will's succession system has a 100% failure rate.

This is not accidental. Miyazaki designed the Empyrean system as a microcosm of the game's central theme: **systems of control inevitably produce the forces that destroy them.** The Greater Will creates gods who rebel against it. The Golden Order persecutes groups who become its nemeses. The Elden Ring, which governs reality, is shattered by its own keeper. The Empyrean system is the most intimate expression of this pattern — divine power, by its nature, cannot be controlled.

## Remaining Gaps

!!! question "Were there other Empyreans we don't know about?"
    The game's history spans ages. If the Greater Will has been selecting Empyreans since before the Erdtree age (Placidusax served a different god), there may be ancient Empyreans whose stories are entirely lost. The Gloam-Eyed Queen is almost one of these — she survives only in item descriptions.

!!! question "What is the biological mechanism?"
    What physically distinguishes an Empyrean's body from a normal demigod's? Miquella's DLC transformation — shedding qualities at each cross to rebuild himself as a divine vessel — suggests Empyrean status involves a specific *configuration* of body and soul, not just raw power. Godrick is a demigod who grafts others' strength; he could never hold the Ring because his body is fundamentally wrong for it.

!!! question "Can an Empyrean be created artificially?"
    The Eternal Cities created the Mimic Tear — an artificial being that copies the player. The Nox were destroyed for "heresy," possibly including attempts to forge their own Empyrean. If Empyrean status is a physical capacity, it might be replicable — which the Greater Will would consider an existential threat to its monopoly on godhood.

!!! question "Is the player character an Empyrean?"
    The Tarnished can contain Great Runes, can be chosen by Melina, and can sit on the Elden Throne. In the Age of Stars ending, they become Ranni's consort — functionally a god's partner. Are they an Empyrean who was never officially designated? Or does becoming Elden Lord require a different kind of vessel than the Empyrean system provides?

!!! question "What does the death of the Two Fingers mean for the system?"
    Late in the game, the Two Fingers at the Roundtable Hold die. If they are the mechanism by which Empyreans are chosen, their death means no new Empyreans can be selected. The succession system is permanently broken. Every ending occurs in a world where the Greater Will's method of choosing gods has ceased to function.
