# The Moral Weight of Every Ending

> No ending is good. Every ending is a wager — a bet that the specific harms it causes are worth the specific goods it enables. The question isn't "which ending is right?" but "which set of consequences can you live with?"

## The Question

Elden Ring presents six endings, each reconfiguring the Elden Ring — the metaphysical law governing reality. Miyazaki has stated no ending is canonical or "correct." But that doesn't mean they're morally equivalent. Each ending has identifiable victims, beneficiaries, and costs. By cross-referencing every NPC questline, item description, and environmental detail in the wiki, we can weigh each ending's moral ledger with more precision than any single playthrough allows.

## The Moral Framework

The Lands Between has one central injustice: the [Golden Order](../lore-concepts/the-golden-order.md) excludes, persecutes, or kills anything that doesn't fit its definition of sacred. The Omen are imprisoned. The merchants were buried alive. The Albinaurics are hunted. Those Who Live in Death are exterminated. The Eternal Cities were destroyed. The [Land of Shadow](../dlc/land-of-shadow.md) was sealed with its entire population.

Every ending can be judged by how it responds to this injustice — and what new injustices it creates.

---

## Age of Fracture — The Complacent Ending

**What happens:** The Tarnished mends the Elden Ring without modification and becomes Elden Lord. The Golden Order is restored as it was before the Shattering.

**Who benefits:** Those already served by the Golden Order — the faithful, the powerful, the "normal." Stability returns. The wars end.

**Who suffers:** Everyone the Order persecuted before. The Omen remain cursed. Those Who Live in Death remain hunted. The merchants' genocide goes unaddressed. The [Land of Shadow](../dlc/land-of-shadow.md) stays sealed. [Destined Death](../lore-concepts/destined-death.md) remains removed — nothing truly dies, and the undying horror continues.

**The moral problem:** This ending changes nothing. It restores the system that produced every atrocity in the game. A Tarnished who fought through the entire Lands Between, witnessed the Omen in the sewers, saw what happened to [Godwyn](../lore-concepts/destined-death.md), learned about the merchants' fate — and then chooses to put everything back the way it was. It's not evil. It's indifference.

**Miyazaki's framing:** The ending cinematic is the most muted. No music swell, no dramatic transformation. The Tarnished simply sits on the throne. Even the game seems unimpressed.

**Verdict:** The safest ending. The least courageous. A stable system is not a just system.

---

## Age of Order — The Authoritarian Ending

**What happens:** [Goldmask's](../npcs/goldmask.md) Mending Rune of Perfect Order removes the flaw that allowed [Marika](../demigods/marika-radagon.md) to shatter the Ring. The Golden Order becomes self-consistent, governed by fixed law rather than divine caprice.

**Who benefits:** Anyone who suffered from the Golden Order's inconsistency — the contradictions, the arbitrary cruelty, the gods' personal vendettas. A perfect system, by definition, cannot be corrupted from within.

**Who suffers:** Anyone the Golden Order's *content* (not just its contradictions) oppresses. The Omen curse wasn't a contradiction — it was doctrine. The persecution of Those Who Live in Death wasn't a bug — it was a feature. Perfecting the Order's *structure* without changing its *substance* means persecuting the same groups more efficiently.

**The moral problem:** Goldmask discovered that "Radagon is Marika" — the Golden Order's god is a contradiction, two wills in one body. His Mending Rune resolves that contradiction. But Goldmask never questioned the Order's *values*, only its *governance*. He fixed the management while leaving the policy untouched. [Brother Corhyn](../npcs/goldmask.md) breaks down because Goldmask's discovery destroys his faith, but Goldmask doesn't care — truth is more important than comfort.

**The deeper irony:** By making the Golden Order immutable, this ending may be the most authoritarian. A flawed-but-flexible system can evolve. A perfect-and-rigid system cannot. Goldmask accidentally created the most oppressive possible outcome — a theocracy that can never be reformed because it has no flaws to reform.

**Verdict:** The intellectual's ending. Solves governance, ignores justice. Perfecting a cage is not the same as opening it.

## Age of Duskborn — The Naturalist Ending

**What happens:** [Fia's](../npcs/fia.md) Mending Rune of the Death-Prince restores [Destined Death](../lore-concepts/destined-death.md) to the Elden Ring. True death returns to the world. Those Who Live in Death are no longer abominations — they are part of the natural cycle.

**Who benefits:** Everyone. Marika's original sin was removing death from the world. Without death, nothing truly ends — Godwyn's corpse spreads Deathroot through the roots, spirits linger without rest, the undying accumulate. Restoring death is restoring the most fundamental natural process.

**Who suffers:** The demigods and powerful who benefited from immortality. Those Who Live in Death are *legitimized* but also now truly mortal. And Fia herself — she dies in the process, embracing Godwyn's corpse to create the rune.

**The moral argument:** This ending addresses the root cause rather than the symptoms. The Golden Order's persecutions were downstream of Marika removing death — if nothing can truly die, everything that *should* die becomes a problem to be managed. Restoring death doesn't fix the persecution directly, but it removes the metaphysical distortion that generated many of the problems.

**What it doesn't fix:** The Omen curse, the merchants' genocide, the Land of Shadow — these are political and cultural crimes, not consequences of removing death. This ending can't undo them.

**The Rogier connection:** [Rogier](../npcs/rogier.md) investigated the Black Knives and died from Deathroot. His research — passed to Fia — is what enables this ending. His death has meaning: it's the investigative thread that leads to the most restorative rune. The game rewards curiosity with the ending that most directly addresses the world's deepest wound.

**Verdict:** The most "natural" ending. Accepts the fundamental truth that death is necessary. Doesn't fix everything, but fixes the thing Marika should never have broken.

---

## Lord of Frenzied Flame — The Annihilator's Ending

**What happens:** The Tarnished becomes the Lord of Frenzied Flame. The Erdtree burns. The Lands Between begin to dissolve. All distinctions — life, death, order, chaos — melt back into the primordial One Great.

**Who benefits:** In theory, no one and everyone. The [Frenzied Flame](../lore-concepts/the-frenzied-flame.md) theology holds that separation itself is suffering. If all things return to oneness, suffering ends because there are no longer individual beings to suffer. The [merchants](../lore-concepts/the-frenzied-flame.md) who were buried alive and generated the Frenzied Flame through their agony might find peace in the dissolution they catalyzed.

**Who suffers:** Every living thing. This ending is omnicide. Every NPC the player helped, every questline they completed, every small act of kindness — rendered meaningless as the world melts.

**The moral case FOR:** The Golden Order created the conditions for every atrocity in the game. The Erdtree is built on [genocide](../dlc/land-of-shadow.md). The Outer Gods treat mortals as pawns. Every "reform" ending keeps some version of this structure. The Frenzied Flame is the only ending that destroys the game board entirely — no more Outer Gods, no more divine hierarchies, no more systems of persecution, because there are no more *systems*.

**The moral case AGAINST:** Destroying everything because you can't fix it is the ultimate failure of imagination. The merchants who generated the Frenzied Flame didn't *choose* this — their agony was weaponized by a cosmic force. Using their suffering to justify annihilating the world adds exploitation on top of their original persecution.

**Melina's response:** She survives this ending *specifically* to condemn it. She opens her [Gloam-Eyed](../lore-concepts/the-gloam-eyed-queen.md) eye and vows to deliver "destined death" to the Lord of Frenzied Flame. The game's most mysterious character breaks her usual ambiguity to say, clearly and directly: *this was wrong*.

**Verdict:** The most honest ending about the depth of the problem. The least honest about whether the solution is proportionate. Compassionate nihilism is still nihilism.

## Age of Stars — The Revolutionary's Ending

**What happens:** [Ranni](../demigods/ranni.md) becomes the new god. The Tarnished becomes her consort. The [Dark Moon](../lore-concepts/the-dark-moon.md) replaces the Erdtree. The [Greater Will](../lore-concepts/the-greater-will.md) is expelled from the Lands Between. A "thousand-year voyage" under the stars begins.

**Who benefits:** Everyone oppressed by the Greater Will's order — potentially. The Omen, the Eternal Cities, Those Who Live in Death, the Albinaurics — all were persecuted by a system Ranni dismantles. The [Nox civilization](../locations/siofra-ainsel-river.md) worshipped the Dark Moon; Ranni's ending vindicates their heresy.

**Who suffers:** Those who depended on the Golden Order's stability. Ranni's ending removes the known cosmic order and replaces it with... what? The "thousand-year voyage" is deliberately vague. Freedom from the Greater Will is also freedom from its protections. And Ranni's path required [Blaidd's](../npcs/blaidd.md) death, [Iji's](../npcs/ranni-questline.md) death, and possibly the sacrifice of everyone who served her.

**The critical question:** Is the Dark Moon different from the Greater Will, or just newer? Every Outer God operates through a proxy — the Greater Will through Marika, the Rot God through [Malenia](../demigods/malenia.md), the [Formless Mother](../lore-concepts/the-formless-mother.md) through [Mohg](../demigods/mohg.md). The Dark Moon operates through Ranni. Ranni insists she's choosing freedom, but she may be choosing a different leash.

**Ranni's own words:** She promises to take the Elden Ring "far away, where it can do no harm," removing it from the lives of mortals. This is the most explicitly anti-theocratic ending — not reforming divine governance but removing it. Mortals will live under stars, not under a tree.

**The body count:** Ranni's path requires more NPC deaths than any other ending except the Frenzied Flame. Blaidd goes mad because the Two Fingers programmed him to. Iji burns himself alive. The Black Knife assassins (Marika's people) enabled the Night of the Black Knives on Ranni's orders. Ranni is a liberator, but liberation in Elden Ring is not bloodless.

**Verdict:** The most radical ending. Genuinely removes the oppressive system rather than reforming it. But trades known tyranny for unknown cosmic influence, and Ranni's path is paved with the bodies of those who loved her.

## Blessing of Despair — The Curse-Spreader's Ending

**What happens:** The [Dung Eater's](../npcs/dungeater.md) Mending Rune of the Fell Curse rewrites the Elden Ring so that all living beings carry the Omen curse. Everyone grows horns, tails, and bestial features. Everyone becomes what [Morgott](../demigods/morgott.md) and [Mohg](../demigods/mohg.md) were persecuted for being.

**Who benefits:** This is the hardest ending to defend. In theory: the Omen are no longer cursed because *everyone* is Omen. You can't persecute a minority for a trait the majority shares. The [Omen-Hornsent-Crucible investigation](omen-hornsent-crucible.md) established that the Omen carry the Crucible's sacred heritage. If everyone carries it, the Crucible is universal again — the Golden Order's erasure of the primordial form is reversed.

**Who suffers:** Everyone, involuntarily. The Omen curse is not a gift — it's painful, deforming, and socially devastating. The Dung Eater spreads it through violation — the Seedbed Curses are created by defiling corpses. The *method* is monstrous regardless of the outcome.

**The Dung Eater question:** Is he a prophet or a monster? Both. He is the most reviled figure in the game — a serial defiler whose victims are cursed to carry the Omen affliction across generations. But his Mending Rune does something no other rune does: it changes *everyone* equally. Age of Order perfects the hierarchy. Age of Duskborn restores a process. Age of Stars replaces the ruler. The Blessing of Despair abolishes the distinction between blessed and cursed by making everyone cursed.

**The uncomfortable parallel:** The Golden Order persecuted the Omen by calling their sacred features a curse. The Dung Eater's solution is to make the "curse" universal, forcing the world to accept what it once rejected. This is the most violent form of equality — not elevating the oppressed but lowering everyone to the same status. It's liberation through mutual damnation.

**Verdict:** The ugliest ending. The only ending whose architect is genuinely evil. And yet — it's the only ending that directly addresses the Omen persecution, the game's most sustained injustice. A monstrous answer to a monstrous problem.

## The DLC Context — Miquella's Failed Ascension

The DLC doesn't add a new ending, but it recontextualizes all of them. [Miquella](../demigods/miquella.md) attempted to become a god of "absolute compassion" — a god so loving that suffering would be impossible under his rule. To do this, he [shed his charm, his love, his youth, his memories](../dlc/miquella-journey.md). The result: a hollow vessel pursuing perfection through self-annihilation.

**The lesson for every ending:** Even good intentions, taken to their logical extreme, produce horror. Goldmask's perfect order becomes a cage. Ranni's freedom becomes isolation. Fia's natural death becomes mortality. The Frenzied Flame's compassion for suffering becomes annihilation of everything, including the sufferers. Miquella proves that the *purity* of an ideal is what makes it dangerous — a god with no compromises is a god with no humanity.

## The Verdict: Ranking by Moral Weight

If forced to rank, weighing harm minimized against harm caused:

| Rank | Ending | Reasoning |
|------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **Age of Duskborn** | Addresses the deepest systemic wound (removal of death) without creating new cosmic tyranny. Fia's self-sacrifice is voluntary and informed. The most restorative. |
| 2 | **Age of Stars** | Removes the oppressive system rather than reforming it. High body count along the way, and the Dark Moon is an unknown variable. Bold but risky. |
| 3 | **Age of Fracture** | Does no additional harm but fixes nothing. The moral weight of inaction when you have the power to act. |
| 4 | **Age of Order** | Fixes governance but perfects an oppressive system. The most insidious ending because it *looks* wise but may be the most authoritarian. |
| 5 | **Blessing of Despair** | Addresses the Omen persecution specifically but through universal, involuntary affliction. The ends may justify the means, but the means are genuinely monstrous. |
| 6 | **Lord of Frenzied Flame** | Addresses every problem by destroying everything. Proportionality is a virtue; this ending has none. Even Melina says you're wrong. |

**The honest answer:** There is no right ending. Each addresses real problems and creates real harm. The ranking above privileges restoration over revolution, voluntary sacrifice over imposed change, and proportionate response over total annihilation. A different moral framework would produce a different ranking.

Miyazaki designed it this way on purpose.
