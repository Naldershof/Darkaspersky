# Investigation: The Age Before the Erdtree -- Placidusax's Fled God

> Before the Erdtree, before Marika, before the Golden Order, a different god ruled the Lands Between through the Dragonlord Placidusax -- and that god's unexplained departure may have been the event that made everything else possible.

## The Question

The Remembrance of the Dragonlord states plainly: *"The Dragonlord whose seat lies at the heart of the storm beyond time is said to have been Elden Lord in the age before the Erdtree. Once his god was fled, the lord continued to await its return."* Placidusax's Ruin incantation reinforces this: *"The Dragonlord Placidusax once served as Elden Lord, before the Erdtree's age. In that lost era, his god had fled."*

Two words carry the weight of an entire cosmological epoch: **"was fled."** Not "was killed," not "was overthrown," not "departed." Fled. Something caused a god to run. What was it? And what did the world look like before it left?

## What We Know About Placidusax

Placidusax is found in the deepest, oldest layer of [Crumbling Farum Azula](../locations/farum-azula.md) -- a hidden arena reached by lying down and being transported backward in time. He originally possessed four or five heads (his model shows two intact and three severed neck stumps), each a mark of his status as Dragonlord. The loss of heads over the eons of waiting suggests a slow physical erosion, as if his vigil is literally diminishing him.

His lightning is red -- distinct from both the golden lightning of Ancient Dragons who allied with the Erdtree and the blue lightning of storms. This unique coloration marks his power as older and categorically different from anything in the current age. The Beastmen of Farum Azula served him and the dragons as a pre-human civilization with their own clergy, rituals, and social hierarchy connected to the [Crucible](../lore-concepts/the-erdtree.md).

The critical detail: Placidusax held the title of **Elden Lord**. This implies a governing order -- an Elden Ring or its equivalent -- existed before the [Greater Will](../lore-concepts/the-greater-will.md) sent the Elden Beast. The title is not honorary. It denotes the consort of a god who holds the fundamental law of reality.

## What We Know About the Pre-Erdtree Age

The pre-Erdtree age was not a void. It was a functioning cosmic order with its own structures:

- **The Crucible** -- the primordial wellspring of life where all forms were mixed together. Horns, tails, wings, and other traits the Golden Order would later call impure were simply the natural state of existence. The Crucible predates the [Erdtree](../lore-concepts/the-erdtree.md) and may predate the Greater Will's influence entirely.
- **The Beastmen** -- a pre-human civilization that served the dragons. Their beast clergy rituals, architecture, and social organization prove that intelligent civilization existed before the Erdtree age. They are the janitors of a ruin, priests of an absent god.
- **The Greattree** -- references to a tree that predated the Erdtree, whose roots ran deep into the earth and whose cycle returned the dead to the ground rather than burning them. The Erdtree may have parasitically co-opted or replaced this older organism.
- **The Eternal Cities** -- [Nokron and Nokstella](../locations/mohgwyn-palace.md) were cast underground by the Greater Will for the sin of attempting to create a Lord of Night. Their star-worship, lunar theology, and creation of artificial life (Silver Tears, Mimic Tears) suggest a sophisticated civilization that operated outside the Golden Order. Were they contemporaries of Placidusax's age, or remnants who survived the transition?
- **Dragon Communion** -- the ancient practice of consuming dragon hearts to gain their power. The dragons carry a primordial divine force from the pre-Erdtree age, accessible to those willing to take it by violence.

## The Fled God -- Candidate Analysis

If Placidusax served a god before the Greater Will's arrival, who was it?

**Candidate 1: The Greater Will itself.** The simplest reading. The Greater Will was Placidusax's god, grew dissatisfied with the dragon order, withdrew, and later returned with a new plan -- the Elden Beast, the Erdtree, Marika. This makes the Greater Will a serial patron that discards servants. Problem: the Elden Stars incantation says the Elden Beast *"descended upon the Lands Between, carrying with it the Elden Ring,"* implying the Greater Will's influence **arrived** -- it was not already there. If the Greater Will had been present under Placidusax, it would not need to send a new vassal.

**Candidate 2: An unnamed, unique Outer God.** A deity with no other trace in the current game. It ruled through Placidusax, and when it fled, it left a vacuum the Greater Will eventually filled. This is the most conservative interpretation -- it demands the least speculation -- but it explains nothing about why the god fled.

**Candidate 3: The Fell God of the Fire Giants.** The Fire Giant Remembrance describes a "fell god" whose flame once threatened the Erdtree. Marika cursed the Giants to guard this flame after their defeat. This is a known pre-Erdtree deity with real power. However, the Fell God appears to have been imprisoned, not fled -- and its domain (fire, destruction) does not align with the dragon civilization's storm-and-lightning character.

**Candidate 4: A primordial force connected to the Crucible.** Not an Outer God in the conventional sense, but the animating principle of the Crucible itself -- the raw, undifferentiated life-force that preceded all order. It "fled" when the Greater Will imposed differentiation and structure. This reading treats the fled god not as a being but as a cosmic state that was displaced.

## Cross-Reference Analysis

This is where the evidence converges on a critical conclusion.

The [Elden Ring is modular](../lore-concepts/the-elden-ring.md) -- runes can be added and removed. If Placidusax was Elden Lord, there was a Ring (or Ring-equivalent) under his god. The Greater Will did not invent the concept of cosmic governance; it installed its own version over a pre-existing framework. This means the fled god had its own configuration of reality, its own runes, its own rules.

The Elden Beast *"descended upon the Lands Between"* carrying the Ring. The word "descended" implies it came from outside. The Greater Will's influence was not native to the Lands Between. It arrived, and it arrived **after** something else left. The sequence matters: the fled god's departure created the opening. The Greater Will exploited it.

The Eternal Cities were destroyed for heresy -- specifically, for trying to create an alternative Lord who would rule under stars rather than golden light. The Fingerslayer Blade, designed to kill the Two Fingers, proves they were not simply heretics but active rebels against the Greater Will's regime. If the Nox remembered or practiced a pre-Greater Will theology, their "heresy" was not innovation but **memory**. They remembered the old order and tried to restore it.

The Beastmen, the Crucible, the Greattree, the Eternal Cities, Dragon Communion -- all of these pre-Erdtree elements share one trait: the Greater Will suppressed, displaced, or demonized every one of them. This is not the behavior of an entity that filled a vacuum. This is the behavior of a conqueror erasing the previous regime.

## Proposed Timeline of the Ages

1. **The Age of the Crucible.** The primordial state. Life is undifferentiated. The Greattree grows. The Crucible's power suffuses the world. A god -- Placidusax's god -- governs through a proto-Elden Ring, with the Dragonlord as its Elden Lord and the Beastmen as its civilization.
2. **The Fled God departs.** The god flees. The cause is unrecorded, but the Greater Will's arrival is the most compelling explanation. Whether the Greater Will drove it out through direct conflict or whether the fled god retreated from an approaching superior force, the result is the same: a vacancy in cosmic governance.
3. **Placidusax enters his vigil.** The Dragonlord refuses to accept his god's departure. Farum Azula begins its temporal collapse. The Beastmen continue their rituals in a crumbling temple, serving a power that no longer answers.
4. **The Greater Will sends the Elden Beast.** The Beast descends carrying the Elden Ring -- the Greater Will's version of cosmic law. It plants itself in the Lands Between, and the Erdtree grows (or the Greattree is transformed).
5. **The Erdtree age begins.** Marika is chosen as vessel. The Golden Order is established. The Nox are cast underground for heresy. The Giants are subjugated. The dragons are conquered. Every remnant of the prior age is suppressed.
6. **The Shattering.** Marika breaks the Ring, shattering the very order the Greater Will installed. The cycle of cosmic governance destabilizes again.

## Verdict

**The Greater Will drove out Placidusax's god.** The evidence points to displacement, not voluntary departure. The word "fled" implies fear or urgency -- gods do not casually leave. The Greater Will's systematic suppression of every pre-Erdtree element (the Crucible, the Beastmen, the Nox, the Giants, the dragons) is consistent with a conqueror eliminating traces of a prior regime, not an inheritor respecting what came before. The Elden Beast "descended" carrying a new order, which implies the old order needed to be removed first.

Placidusax's god did not simply leave. It was outcompeted, overpowered, or threatened by a stronger cosmic entity that wanted the Lands Between for itself. The Greater Will is not the Lands Between's original patron. It is an occupying force, and everything the Golden Order calls sacred -- the Erdtree, the Ring, the grace system -- is the infrastructure of occupation built over the ruins of what came before.

This reframes the entire game. Every ending, from [Ranni's Age of Stars](../endings/age-of-stars.md) to the [Lord of Frenzied Flame](../endings/lord-of-frenzied-flame.md), is not a rebellion against the natural order. It is a continuation of the same pattern: cosmic powers struggling over the Lands Between, installing and removing their systems, while the Dragonlord waits at the bottom of a crumbling ruin for a god that was driven away long ago.

## Remaining Gaps

- **The fled god has no name, no description, and no confirmed identity.** Every candidate is inference. The game may be deliberately withholding this to preserve the mystery of what came before.
- **The relationship between the Crucible and the fled god is unclear.** Was the Crucible the fled god's creation, or did the fled god govern a world where the Crucible already existed?
- **The Eternal Cities' timeline is uncertain.** Were Nokron and Nokstella part of Placidusax's age, the transitional period, or the early Erdtree age? Their star-worship could place them in any of these.
- **Whether the Elden Ring existed under the fled god** or whether "Elden Lord" is a retroactive title applied to Placidusax using Erdtree-age terminology remains unresolved.
- **The fled god's current location.** It fled, but fled where? Is it dead, dormant in another world, or waiting -- like Placidusax -- for conditions to change?
