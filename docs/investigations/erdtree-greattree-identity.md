# Investigation: The Erdtree and the Greattree — Transformation or Parasitism?

> The Erdtree is the Greattree -- colonized, repurposed, and gilded by the Greater Will's power, its original death-cycle severed and replaced with a system of spiritual extraction that the Golden Order calls grace.

## The Question

The Erdtree dominates the Lands Between as the seat of the Elden Ring, the engine of the Golden Order, and the destination of every faithful soul upon death. But item descriptions reference an older organism -- the Greattree -- whose roots still exist underground, distinct from the Erdtree. Meanwhile, Crucible Knight armor describes the Crucible as "the primordial form of the Erdtree." Are these the same organism at different stages of life, or did the Greater Will plant a parasite on top of an older, independent tree?

The stakes are enormous. If the Erdtree naturally evolved from the Greattree, the Golden Order has a legitimate claim to succession -- it governs through the same tree that always governed, merely in a new form. But if the Greater Will colonized the Greattree and repurposed it, then the Golden Order is a foreign occupation wearing the skin of something it killed. The Erdtree's golden light becomes not a blessing but a brand of ownership.

## Evidence for Transformation (Same Tree)

The strongest evidence for continuity comes from the Crucible Knight armor sets. The Crucible Tree Set states: *"The Crucible is the primordial form of the Erdtree. It is said that the Crucible existed before the establishment of the Golden Order."* The Crucible Axe Set adds that Crucible Knights *"were champions of the Erdtree in a bygone era, when its primordial form was still a common sight."* The language is unambiguous: the Crucible is an earlier form of the Erdtree, not a separate entity. If the Crucible and the Erdtree are the same organism, and the Crucible predates the Greater Will's influence, then the Erdtree evolved naturally from something older.

The Crucible Knot Talisman reinforces this: *"In the beginning, everything was in a state of perfect wholeness. An age of abundance, where everything existed in an unalloyed state."* This describes the Crucible era as one of unified, primordial life -- horns, tails, wings, all mixed together. The Erdtree grew from this. The Omen, born with Crucible traits, are living proof that the Erdtree's body remembers what it was before the Golden Order sanitized it.

Death Rite Birds also confirm continuity: they *"served the original form of the Erdtree, before the establishment of the Golden Order."* The tree existed before the Order. Its servants predate the golden light. The Erdtree is older than the system that now claims it. Critically, the Death Rite Birds performed funerary rites -- they oversaw the dead's return to the earth. This was the Greattree's cycle, and the birds served it. When the Golden Order took over the tree and replaced the old death-cycle with burning, the Death Rite Birds became heretical -- servants of a function the tree itself had once performed.

The Ancestral Follower spirit ash reinforces the point from below: they are *"relics of a civilization that predates the Erdtree."* Yet they worship beneath a tree's root system. That tree was the Greattree. The fact that it is called both the Erdtree's precursor and a separate entity by different sources is itself evidence of colonization -- the new name overwrites the old, but inconsistently, because the old organism is still physically present.

## Evidence for Parasitism (Different Entity)

Root Resin provides the single most damning piece of evidence: *"Resin taken from the roots of the Greattree. Not the Erdtree, but the primordial form."* This directly separates the two. The Greattree's roots are physically distinct from the Erdtree's roots, existing underground in a recognizable, different form. If the Erdtree simply evolved from the Greattree, why would the game distinguish their root systems?

The Tibia Mariner and Deathroot descriptions reinforce the separation. Deathroot spreads through the underground root network from Godwyn's corpse in Deeproot Depths, and it travels through what are described as the Greattree's roots, not the Erdtree's. The corruption moves through the older system. The Erdtree's golden roots extend downward too, but they interface with the Greattree's roots rather than being identical to them -- two root networks, intertwined but distinguishable.

The Elden Beast, described as *"a vassal beast, sent to the Lands Between long ago by the Greater Will,"* is the Erdtree's core. It *"became the living embodiment of the Elden Ring itself, dwelling within the Erdtree as its guardian."* The Elden Stars incantation adds that the beast *"descended upon the Lands Between, carrying with it the Elden Ring."* Something arrived from outside. Something was installed. The Erdtree's golden power has a foreign origin.

## The Root System Evidence

Deeproot Depths is the critical location. At the lowest point of the underground, where the root systems converge, Godwyn's soulless corpse has fused with the roots and expanded into a massive tumor spreading Deathroot through the network. The roots in Deeproot Depths are dark, diseased, and ancient -- nothing like the golden roots visible on the surface. These are Greattree roots. The Erdtree's golden influence fades the deeper you descend, revealing the older organism beneath. The game's visual design reinforces this: the transition from golden trunk to dark roots is gradual, like peeling gilding off wood. The deeper you go, the more the original material shows through.

The Elden Stars incantation, found in Deeproot Depths itself, describes the Elden Beast as having *"descended upon the Lands Between, carrying with it the Elden Ring."* That this description exists physically at the junction point between the Erdtree's golden roots and the Greattree's dark roots is suggestive -- it marks the place where the foreign power meets the native organism.

The underground civilizations corroborate this. The Ancestral Followers in Siofra River worship nature spirits under a false starlit sky, predating the Erdtree entirely. The Nox of Nokron and Nokstella built cities among these deep roots and worshipped the moon and stars -- not the Erdtree, not the Golden Order. These civilizations lived alongside the Greattree's root system before the Erdtree existed. The roots they built among are the Greattree's roots, and those roots are still there, still structurally intact, still a separate layer beneath the golden veneer above.

The Minor Erdtrees scattered across the surface are also relevant here. Each is guarded by an Erdtree Avatar -- a tree-spirit construct that attacks all who approach, without distinction between friend and foe. These satellite trees distribute Golden Seeds and Sacred Tears, functioning as nodes in the Greater Will's power network. Are they shoots of the original Greattree, co-opted like the main trunk? Or are they new growths planted by the Greater Will's influence? Their mechanical, aggressive guardians suggest the latter -- they behave less like natural outgrowths and more like installed infrastructure, distribution points for a system that was imposed on the landscape rather than grown from it.

## Cross-Reference Analysis

This is where the picture comes together. Consider the timeline implied by all sources combined:

**Stage 1 -- The Crucible/Greattree era.** A primordial tree exists, embodying the Crucible -- a state where all life is mixed, undifferentiated, abundant. Death Rite Birds serve it. The Crucible Knights champion it. The underground civilizations build among its roots. The dead return to the earth through the root system. Life and death cycle naturally.

**Stage 2 -- The Greater Will's intervention.** The Elden Beast descends, carrying the Elden Ring. It does not plant a new tree. It enters the existing tree. The Elden Beast *dwells within* the Erdtree -- the language of inhabitation, not creation. The golden light suffuses the tree, transforming its visible form. The Crucible's primordial energy is overwritten by the Elden Ring's ordered power. The tree is renamed: no longer the Greattree, now the Erdtree. The surface changes. The roots do not.

**Stage 3 -- The Golden Order.** Marika, empowered by the Greater Will, formalizes the new system. The Erdtree burns the dead instead of returning them to the earth, absorbing their essence. Crucible traits (horns, tails, wings) are reclassified as impurities -- the Omen are persecuted for bearing the tree's own original characteristics. The underground civilizations that worshipped the Greattree's natural cycle are cast down or destroyed. The Nox are banished for attempting to create a Lord of Night -- an alternative to the Elden Lord. The Greattree's death-cycle is severed and replaced with the Erdtree's burning-cycle.

**Stage 4 -- The cracks appear.** Godwyn's half-death infects the root system with Deathroot. The corruption travels through the Greattree's roots, not the Erdtree's golden network, because the old roots still function as the tree's true vascular system. The Erdtree's golden surface is cosmetic; the Greattree's roots are structural. When the system fails, it fails along the old lines. Marika's removal of Destined Death compounds the damage -- the Greattree's cycle required true death to function, and without it, the root system chokes on souls that have nowhere to go.

**Stage 5 -- The Erdtree seals itself.** After the Shattering, the Erdtree blocks entry with thorns, rejecting even Morgott, its most devoted defender. This behavior is revealing. The thorns are the Erdtree's response -- meaning the Greater Will's response, channeled through the tree it controls. The Greattree never rejected anyone; its Crucible-era cycle accepted all life, all death, all forms. The thorns are the parasite's immune response, not the host's.

The Haligtree and the Scadutree provide the final confirmation. Miquella grew the Haligtree independently, using his own unalloyed gold rather than the Greater Will's power. The DLC's Revered Spirit Ash confirms that the Land of Shadow remembers *"the age before the Erdtree -- when death was governed by the Crucible's cycle and the shadow was not yet severed from the light."* The Scadutree stands in the Land of Shadow as *"a pale reflection of the Erdtree itself -- an echo of grace that Marika left behind."* Both prove that trees are vessels -- organisms that can be claimed, powered, and directed by different forces. The Greattree was such a vessel. The Greater Will claimed it.

Dragonlord Placidusax adds a temporal anchor: he *"is said to have been Elden Lord in the age before the Erdtree. Once his god was fled, the lord continued to await its return."* The Erdtree did not exist during Placidusax's reign. But the Greattree almost certainly did -- the Crucible's age of abundance had to precede both the dragons' era and the Greater Will's arrival. The Greattree was the Lands Between's native sacred tree. The Greater Will was an interloper that arrived after Placidusax's god departed, found an existing tree, and claimed it.

## Proposed Theory

The Erdtree and the Greattree are the same organism. The Erdtree is what the Greattree became after the Greater Will's Elden Beast entered it and the Elden Ring's power transformed its surface expression. The golden light, the burning of the dead, the distribution of grace -- these are the Greater Will's modifications, overlaid on an older living structure whose root system remains intact underground. The Greattree was not destroyed. It was colonized.

This is neither pure transformation nor pure parasitism. It is closer to what happens when a parasitic organism so thoroughly integrates with its host that the two become inseparable -- but the host's original structure persists beneath the parasite's modifications. The Erdtree's trunk is golden; the Greattree's roots are dark. The Erdtree burns the dead; the Greattree returned them to the earth. The surface changed. The foundation did not.

The apparent contradiction in the sources dissolves under this reading. The Crucible Knight armor says the Crucible is the Erdtree's primordial form -- true, because it is the same organism. Root Resin says the Greattree is "not the Erdtree" -- also true, because the Greattree's root system retains its original character, untouched by the golden transformation above. Both statements are accurate. They describe different parts of the same colonized body. The Erdtree is the name for the golden canopy and trunk. The Greattree is the name for the dark roots below. They are the same tree, but only the top half has been claimed.

## Verdict

**The Erdtree is the Greattree, colonized by the Greater Will -- a living organism repurposed as a control mechanism.** The Crucible Knight descriptions confirm biological continuity. The Root Resin and Deeproot Depths confirm structural distinction between the old roots and the new golden overlay. The Elden Beast's arrival explains the mechanism: a foreign entity entered an existing tree, installed the Elden Ring, and transformed the tree's function from a natural death-cycle into a system of spiritual extraction. The Golden Order is not an evolution of the Greattree's original faith. It is a hostile takeover wearing the tree's body as legitimacy.

The confidence level is high. No single piece of evidence contradicts this reading. The only uncertainty is whether the Greater Will's colonization was violent (forcibly overwriting the Greattree's nature) or cooperative (the tree accepting a new tenant). Given that the Golden Order subsequently persecuted everything associated with the tree's original state -- the Crucible traits, the Death Rite Birds, the underground civilizations, the natural death-cycle -- the relationship reads as coercive rather than symbiotic.

## Implications

This verdict reframes the entire game. The Golden Order's claim to divine authority rests on the Erdtree, but the Erdtree is a stolen body. The Omen are persecuted for bearing the tree's own original traits -- horns, tails, and wings that are aspects of the Crucible, the tree's own primordial form. The Omenkiller surgeons who cut these traits from living children are, unknowingly, performing a kind of botanical pruning on behalf of the parasite -- removing the host's original features to better fit the new occupant's aesthetic. The underground civilizations were destroyed for remembering what the tree used to be. The Nox were banished for attempting to create an alternative to the Elden Lord -- a Lord of Night who would serve the stars, not the golden light. Their Fingerslayer Blade, designed to kill the Two Fingers, was an act of resistance against the colonizing power.

Morgott's tragedy becomes almost unbearable under this reading. He died defending a tree that was, in its deepest roots, the same organism whose original nature would have accepted him -- horns and all. The Crucible did not distinguish. The Greattree did not reject. Only the Erdtree -- the colonized version, the golden mask over the old face -- branded Morgott's body as accursed.

Every ending in the game involves the player reaching the Erdtree's core and reconfiguring the Elden Ring. In light of this investigation, that act is not merely political. It is an encounter with the parasite at the heart of the host. The Elden Beast is not the tree's natural guardian -- it is the thing that took the tree over. Killing it does not destroy the tree. It liberates the Greattree from the power that colonized it. What grows after depends on which Mending Rune the player installs -- but the tree itself, the ancient organism rooted in Deeproot Depths, endures.

Fia's Age of Duskborn ending gains particular resonance. By restoring Destined Death to the Elden Ring, Fia reintroduces true mortality -- the very principle the Greattree's original cycle depended on. The dead can return to the earth. The Death Rite Birds' function is restored. This ending does not merely fix the Golden Order. It reverses the Greater Will's most fundamental modification: the severing of the natural death-cycle. The Duskborn ending is, in this reading, the closest the player can come to restoring the Greattree's original purpose while keeping the Elden Ring intact.

The Lord of Frenzied Flame ending represents the opposite extreme. The Frenzied Flame would melt all distinctions back into the One Great -- obliterating not just the Erdtree's golden overlay but the Greattree itself, the roots, the underground, everything. It is not liberation. It is annihilation of host and parasite alike.
