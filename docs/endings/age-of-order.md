# Age of Order

> Goldmask's ending. The Elden Ring is mended with the Mending Rune of Perfect Order, purging the Golden Order of divine caprice. The law becomes absolute, immutable, and incorruptible -- beyond the reach of any god's will, including Marika's.

## Overview

The Age of Order is achieved by obtaining the Mending Rune of Perfect Order from [Goldmask's](../npcs/goldmask.md) body after completing his questline, then applying it when mending the Elden Ring. The result is a Golden Order stripped of its most dangerous feature: the ability of the gods to alter it on a whim.

[Goldmask](../npcs/goldmask.md) identified the core flaw in the Golden Order: it was governed by beings -- [Marika and Radagon](../demigods/marika-radagon.md) -- whose internal contradictions made them unreliable. Marika shattered the Ring. Radagon tried to repair it. Two wills in one body, pulling in opposite directions. The Mending Rune of Perfect Order removes this variable. The law governs itself, requiring no divine steward to maintain or interpret it.

## Lore Details

**The rune's description.** "The current imperfection of the Golden Order is the result of the fickleness of the gods, no better than the madness of men. The Mending Rune of Perfect Order is the answer to that madness -- an Order immutable, perfected, and eternal." This language frames the gods' ability to choose as a defect, and rigidity as a virtue.

**What changes.** The Golden Order continues, but no god can alter its rules. The Shattering becomes impossible because the conditions that enabled it -- divine agency over the Ring -- no longer exist. The Two Fingers presumably retain their role as interpreters, but there is nothing left to interpret; the law is complete.

**What does not change.** The rune addresses the gods' caprice but does not address the Order's content. If the Golden Order already persecutes Omens, suppresses death, and marginalizes pre-Erdtree civilizations, those injustices become permanent. Perfect Order is not the same as just order.

## Connections & Analysis

**The most conservative ending.** Every other ending modifies what the Golden Order is. The Age of Order modifies how it functions -- locking it in place so that it can never be changed again. This is reform in the narrowest sense: fixing the mechanism while preserving the machine. It prevents future Shatterings but also prevents future reformations. The cage is perfected, not opened.

**Goldmask diagnoses correctly but prescribes dangerously.** He is right that the Shattering was caused by divine contradiction. He is right that an order vulnerable to its gods' whims is unstable. But his solution -- making the Order immutable -- assumes the Order is already correct in its current form. It is not. Destined Death is still sealed. The Omens are still persecuted. Those Who Live in Death are still condemned. Perfecting the system's stability while ignoring its injustice is a profoundly conservative act.

**Contrast with the Age of Duskborn.** [Fia's ending](age-of-duskborn.md) also reforms the Golden Order rather than replacing it, but it changes the Order's content (restoring death) rather than its governance. Fia fixes what the Order does; Goldmask fixes how the Order is maintained. Together they represent the two dimensions of reform: substance and structure.

**Contrast with the Age of Stars.** [Ranni's ending](age-of-stars.md) is the polar opposite. Where Goldmask perfects the Golden Order, Ranni abolishes it entirely. Where Goldmask removes divine agency from the system, Ranni removes the system from the world. The Age of Order is a world governed by perfect law. The Age of Stars is a world governed by distant mystery.

**The Radagon/Marika paradox.** Goldmask's rune is a response to the revelation that Radagon is Marika. Two beings in one body, with conflicting desires, governing a system that demands unity. His solution is to make the system independent of its governors. But this raises a question: if the gods are removed from the equation, what is the Golden Order for? An impersonal law without a divine purpose is indistinguishable from natural law. Goldmask may have accidentally secularized the faith.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "Does Perfect Order eliminate all Outer God influence?"
    The rune removes the gods' ability to alter the Order. But does this apply only to Marika, or to all cosmic entities? If the Greater Will itself cannot modify the Order, the Erdtree becomes a self-sustaining system with no external oversight.

!!! question "Is immutability a virtue?"
    Goldmask treats stability as the highest good. But the world changes. New threats emerge. An Order that cannot adapt to the [Frenzied Flame](lord-of-frenzied-flame.md), the Scarlet Rot, or future cosmic incursions is an Order waiting to be overwhelmed by something it cannot accommodate.

!!! question "What happens to worship?"
    If the gods no longer govern the Order, does prayer become meaningless? The Golden Order is a theocracy; removing the gods from governance while keeping the structure creates a theocracy without a theos.
