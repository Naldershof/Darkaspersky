# Endings of the Lands Between

> Each ending represents a different answer to the same question: what should the Elden Ring -- the governing law of reality -- become? The Tarnished does not merely choose a ruler; they choose the metaphysical rules by which all life will be governed.

## Overview

Elden Ring has six primary endings and one DLC-connected variant. Each is determined by the Mending Rune (or equivalent catalyst) the player brings to the shattered Elden Ring after defeating the final boss. Three endings use Mending Runes obtained from NPC questlines. Two bypass the Mending Rune system entirely (the Frenzied Flame and the Age of Stars). One is the default, requiring nothing. The DLC adds context to a seventh variant.

The endings are not ranked by the game. No ending is presented as canonical, correct, or superior. Each carries its own costs and contradictions, and the game's refusal to judge is itself a statement: the Lands Between's future is a genuine choice, not a puzzle with a right answer.

## Thematic Spectrum

The endings can be arranged on a spectrum from preservation to annihilation:

1. **Age of Order** -- Preserves and perfects the Golden Order
2. **Age of Fracture** -- Preserves the Golden Order as-is
3. **Age of Duskborn** -- Reforms the Golden Order by restoring death
4. **Age of Stars** -- Replaces the Golden Order entirely
5. **Blessing of Despair** -- Defiles the Golden Order from within
6. **Lord of Frenzied Flame** -- Destroys everything

Each step away from the center represents a more radical break with the existing system. The DLC's Miquella context adds a perpendicular axis: even well-intentioned gods are dangerous.
