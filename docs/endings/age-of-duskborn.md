# Age of Duskborn

> Fia's ending. The Mending Rune of the Death-Prince restores Destined Death to the Elden Ring, undoing Marika's original sin of sealing death away. Those Who Live in Death are no longer abominations -- they are part of the natural order. Life and death exist in balance for the first time since the Golden Order began.

## Overview

The Age of Duskborn is achieved by obtaining the Mending Rune of the Death-Prince from [Fia's](../npcs/fia.md) questline and applying it when mending the Elden Ring. Fia lies with Godwyn's corpse in the Deeproot Depths, uses the accumulated vitality of every champion she embraced, and births a rune that restores the concept of death to the fundamental law of reality.

The result is a world where death functions. Beings can die completely. Godwyn's half-death -- and the corruption it spawned -- is resolved. Those Who Live in Death are no longer treated as aberrations because death is no longer aberrant. The Erdtree presumably continues to function, but its relationship to death is restored to something more natural.

## Lore Details

**The rune's description.** "The Golden Order was founded upon the separation of life from death. The Death-Prince would undo that separation, allowing those who live in death to find a rightful place." This is an explicit indictment of the Golden Order's founding act: the removal of Destined Death.

**What changes.** Destined Death is restored to the Elden Ring. This means: demigods can die. The Erdtree's cycle of absorbing the dead and recycling them may change or end. Deathroot ceases to spread (its cause -- Godwyn's incomplete death -- is resolved). The persecution of the undead loses its theological justification.

**What does not change.** The Golden Order presumably continues in some form. The Greater Will's influence through the Two Fingers persists. The broader structure of divine authority remains. This ending reforms the Order's relationship to death without dismantling the Order itself.

## Connections & Analysis

**The most "natural" ending.** Of all the endings, the Age of Duskborn makes the smallest structural change with the greatest philosophical impact. It does not replace the Golden Order, perfect it, defile it, or destroy it. It simply restores a single element that should never have been removed: death. Every other ending changes what the Lands Between is. The Age of Duskborn changes what was done to the Lands Between, returning it to a state closer to nature.

**Marika's original sin, undone.** Marika ordered Maliketh to seal away Destined Death, making the demigods immortal. This act is the root cause of nearly every catastrophe in the game: Godwyn's half-death (which spawned Deathroot and Those Who Live in Death), the Night of the Black Knives (which required stolen Death), the Shattering (which was made possible by immortal demigods who could not resolve their conflicts through mortality), and the persecution of the undead (who exist only because death was incomplete). Fia's rune addresses all of these consequences at their source.

**The death of persecution.** The Golden Order hunts Those Who Live in Death through agents like D, Hunter of the Dead. This persecution is not incidental -- it is doctrinal. The Order declares that death should not exist, and therefore the undead are blasphemous. By restoring death to the Order, Fia removes the theological basis for this persecution. D's crusade becomes meaningless not because the undead are vindicated but because the category "undead" ceases to exist as an aberration.

**Contrast with the Age of Fracture.** The [default ending](age-of-fracture.md) preserves everything, including the sealed Death. The Age of Duskborn changes one thing -- but that one thing cascades through every other problem. This contrast highlights how the game structures its moral choices: the simplest change can be the most transformative.

**Contrast with the Frenzied Flame.** The [Lord of Frenzied Flame](lord-of-frenzied-flame.md) ending also resolves the world's suffering, but through total annihilation. The Age of Duskborn resolves suffering through acceptance -- death is not something to fear or prevent, but something to integrate. Fia and the Three Fingers both oppose the Golden Order's relationship to death, but Fia seeks coexistence where the Frenzied Flame seeks erasure.

**Rogier's legacy.** [Rogier](../npcs/rogier.md) investigated Godwyn's corpse and died of death-blight -- a victim of the very problem Fia's rune solves. His research led the player to Ranni and, indirectly, to Fia. In a sense, Rogier's death is redeemed by the Age of Duskborn: the corruption that killed him is resolved by the rune his investigation helped make possible.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "Can Godwyn's corruption be reversed?"
    The Mending Rune restores Destined Death going forward. But Godwyn's body has already fused with the Erdtree's roots, and Deathroot has already spread across the continent. Is the damage already done, or does restoring Death retroactively heal what was corrupted?

!!! question "What happens to the demigods?"
    If Destined Death is restored, the remaining demigods become mortal. Malenia, Mohg, Rykard -- any who survived -- can now die. This is a profound shift in the balance of power.

!!! question "Is the Erdtree's cycle broken?"
    The Erdtree absorbs the dead and recycles their souls. If death is restored as a natural process, does the Erdtree's role change? Does it become a monument rather than a mechanism?
