# The Golden Order

> The religious, metaphysical, and political system imposed upon the Lands Between by Queen Marika and the Greater Will -- a framework of divine law that governed reality itself, enforced through faith, incantation, and violence.

## Overview

The Golden Order is not merely a religion. It is the operating system of reality in the Lands Between: the rules that govern life, death, causality, and grace all flow from the [Elden Ring](the-elden-ring.md), which the Golden Order interprets and enforces. At its peak, the Order was maintained by a dual structure -- [Marika](../demigods/marika-radagon.md) as its divine vessel and the [Two Fingers](the-fingers.md) as the [Greater Will](the-greater-will.md)'s intermediaries. Its tenets were expressed through Golden Order incantations, its will enforced by the Erdtree's knights and the Crucible Knights, and its dead consumed by the [Erdtree](the-erdtree.md)'s flames.

But the Golden Order's greatest virtue -- its totalizing coherence -- was also its deepest flaw. Everything that did not conform was deemed impure and suppressed: the Omen were imprisoned, the merchants were buried alive, the Nox civilizations were cast underground, the Land of Shadow was hidden from history, and [Destined Death](destined-death.md) itself was removed from the cycle. The Order's collapse during [the Shattering](the-shattering.md) was not merely a political crisis but a revelation: the cracks had been there all along.

## Lore Details

### The Founding of the Order

The Golden Order was established when the [Greater Will](the-greater-will.md) sent the Elden Beast to the Lands Between, carrying the Elden Ring. Queen Marika was chosen as the vessel for the Ring, becoming its human embodiment. The Erdtree grew around the Ring's power, supplanting the older Crucible and the faiths that preceded it. [Godfrey](../demigods/godfrey.md), a fearsome warrior chieftain known as Hoarah Loux, suppressed his berserker nature to become the first Elden Lord -- Marika's consort and the Order's military arm[^1].

Together, Marika and Godfrey waged wars of conquest. They defeated the Giants of the Mountaintops, whose Flame of Ruin threatened the Erdtree. They subdued the storm lords at Stormveil. They drove the dragons from their ancient seat (Dragonlord Placidusax was Elden Lord in the age before the Erdtree[^2]). And in the [Land of Shadow](../dlc/land-of-shadow.md), Marika's hidden son [Messmer](../dlc/messmer.md) subjugated the Hornsent, allowing the Erdtree to be planted over their civilization.

### The Tenets of the Order

The Golden Order's theology holds several core principles:

1. **The Erdtree is supreme.** All life comes from it and returns to it. The dead are burned by the Erdtree's roots, their souls absorbed into the golden cycle.
2. **Grace is granted by the Erdtree** (or the Greater Will through it). Those who bear Grace are favored; those who lose it are cast out.
3. **The Elden Ring defines reality.** Its runes establish the laws of the world. When runes are added or removed, reality itself changes.
4. **Destined Death was removed** from the Ring by Marika, who sealed it within Maliketh's Black Blade. This made the demigods functionally immortal and removed true death from the world.
5. **Impurity must be excised.** Anything that recalls the pre-Erdtree world -- Crucible traits (horns, tails), underground faiths, rival cosmic powers -- is heretical.

These tenets are encoded in Golden Order incantations, which function as both prayers and physics. The incantations literally reshape reality according to the Order's principles, demonstrating that in the Lands Between, theology and natural law are the same thing.

### The Order's Hypocrisy

The Golden Order preaches unity and grace but practices systematic persecution:

- **The Omen:** Children born with horns -- a trait linked to the ancient Crucible -- are surgically de-horned or imprisoned in the Subterranean Shunning-Grounds beneath Leyndell. Both [Morgott](../demigods/morgott.md) and [Mohg](../demigods/mohg.md), twin sons of Marika herself, were imprisoned as Omen. Morgott's devotion to the Erdtree despite this cruelty is one of the most tragic arcs in the game; Mohg's turn to the [Formless Mother](the-formless-mother.md) is entirely comprehensible.
- **The Merchants:** An entire people buried alive beneath Leyndell. Their collective agony gave birth to the [Frenzied Flame](the-frenzied-flame.md) -- the cosmic force that seeks to melt all existence back into the One Great. The Golden Order created its own antithesis.
- **Those Who Live in Death:** When Marika removed Destined Death, those who should have died but could not became abominations in the Order's eyes. They are hunted by the Golden Order's agents despite being victims of the Order's own policy.
- **The Land of Shadow:** An entire civilization conquered and erased from history. The Hornsent's faith, culture, and people were suppressed so thoroughly that most inhabitants of the Lands Between do not even know the Land of Shadow exists[^3].

### Radagon and Marika: The Dual Pillars

[Radagon](../demigods/marika-radagon.md) and Marika are the same being -- two aspects sharing one body. Yet their relationship to the Golden Order differs profoundly. Radagon is described as the "leal hound of the Golden Order," a faithful enforcer who tried to repair the Elden Ring after Marika shattered it[^4]. Marika, by contrast, grew to question and ultimately destroy the system she had built.

Their duality embodies the Golden Order's internal contradiction: the system was maintained by a being that was both its architect and its saboteur. Marika's words at the Church of Pilgrimage -- *"I declare mine intent, to search the depths of the Golden Order. Through understanding of the proper way, our faith, our Golden Order, shall be made whole again"*[^5] -- can be read as either reformist zeal or the first signs of the rebellion that would culminate in the Shattering.

## Connections & Analysis

### The Golden Order and Every Ending

Each ending in the game represents a different critique of the Golden Order:

- **[Age of Fracture](../endings/age-of-fracture.md):** The simplest restoration -- the Ring is mended, the Order continues, but weakened by fracture lines.
- **[Age of Order](../endings/age-of-order.md) (Goldmask):** The Order's imperfection is the fickleness of the gods. Remove divine caprice, and the Order becomes "immutable, perfected, and eternal"[^6]. This is a technocratic fix that addresses the symptom (unstable gods) rather than the disease (the Order's fundamental injustices).
- **[Age of Duskborn](../endings/age-of-duskborn.md) (Fia):** Restore Destined Death to the Ring, allowing true death and giving Those Who Live in Death a rightful place. This directly reverses one of the Order's founding decisions.
- **[Blessing of Despair](../endings/blessing-of-despair.md) (Dung Eater):** Spread the Omen curse to all. If everyone is cursed, no one can be persecuted for it. A horrifying egalitarianism.
- **[Age of Stars](../endings/age-of-stars.md) (Ranni):** Abandon the Golden Order entirely. Replace it with an order of stars and moon, kept "far from the earth"[^7] -- an order that does not micromanage mortal existence.
- **[Lord of Frenzied Flame](../endings/lord-of-frenzied-flame.md):** Destroy everything. Return all to the One Great. The most extreme rejection possible.

### Goldmask's Paradox

[Goldmask](../npcs/goldmask.md) is the Golden Order's most devoted scholar, and yet his conclusion -- that the gods are the problem -- is devastating to the Order's legitimacy. His Mending Rune of Perfect Order seeks to remove divine fickleness, but in doing so, it implicitly admits that the Golden Order as designed by Marika and enforced by the Greater Will was fundamentally flawed. Goldmask's "perfect" order would strip agency from the very gods who created it.

### The Order's Self-Destruction

The Golden Order created every force that would eventually destroy it. By persecuting the Omen, it drove Mohg to the Formless Mother. By burying the merchants alive, it birthed the Frenzied Flame. By removing Destined Death, it created Those Who Live in Death and corrupted the Erdtree's roots. By conquering the Hornsent, it created the hidden history that would draw Miquella into the Land of Shadow. The Order's insistence on purity generated the very impurities it most feared.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "Was the Golden Order ever good, or was it always a system of control?"
    **Interpretation A:** The early Golden Order brought genuine peace and prosperity to the Lands Between. The wars of conquest were necessary to end the chaotic age of dragons and giants. The corruption came later, as Marika's decisions (sealing Destined Death, persecuting the Omen) introduced distortions.
    **Interpretation B:** The Order was always imperial and coercive. The "peace" it brought was the peace of subjugation. The Crucible's inclusive cycle was healthier, and the Golden Order's sanitized version was a degradation from the start.
    **Gap:** Almost no sources describe the Lands Between before the Golden Order in detail. The Dragonlord Placidusax awaited a "fled god," suggesting even the pre-Order era had its own cosmic patron that abandoned it.

!!! question "What is the relationship between Golden Order incantations and reality?"
    **Interpretation A:** The incantations are powered by the Elden Ring and literally enforce the Order's rules on reality. When the Ring is shattered, the incantations weaken.
    **Interpretation B:** The incantations tap into a deeper truth that the Order merely codified. The power exists independently; the Order just structured access to it.
    **Gap:** The game does not clearly distinguish between the Order as metaphysical law and the Order as institutional religion.
