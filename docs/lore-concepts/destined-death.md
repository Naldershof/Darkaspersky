# Destined Death

> The Rune of Death -- the principle of mortality itself, removed from the Elden Ring by Queen Marika and sealed within Maliketh's Black Blade. Its absence created a world where nothing truly dies, and its theft triggered the most consequential night in the Lands Between's history.

## Overview

Destined Death is not merely a concept but a literal rune -- a component of the [Elden Ring](the-elden-ring.md) that once governed the principle of true, final death. Queen [Marika](../demigods/marika-radagon.md) removed this rune from the Ring and entrusted it to her shadow-bound beast, [Maliketh](../locations/farum-azula.md), who sealed it within his Black Blade. With Destined Death removed, the demigods became functionally immortal, and the natural cycle of death was broken.

This decision had catastrophic consequences. The dead could not truly die, leading to the emergence of Those Who Live in Death. The [Erdtree](the-erdtree.md) burned the dead but could not process them properly without the rune. And when [Ranni](../demigods/ranni.md) stole a fragment of Destined Death to orchestrate the Night of the Black Knives, the resulting "half-deaths" corrupted the Erdtree's roots with Deathroot and set in motion the events that would lead to [the Shattering](the-shattering.md).

## Lore Details

### Marika's Decision: Sealing Death Away

Marika removed the Rune of Death from the Elden Ring and gave it to Maliketh for safekeeping. The Remembrance of the Black Blade describes Maliketh as *"the shadowbound beast given to Queen Marika. He alone was bestowed the Destined Death rune, sealed within his black blade. As Marika's shadow, he was the gravest of the empyrean assassins."*[^1]

The stated motivation was to protect her demigod children from death. But this act rewrote the fundamental rules of the world. Without Destined Death, the Erdtree's cycle of absorption could not function properly. Souls were trapped in a half-state between life and death. The golden equilibrium the [Golden Order](the-golden-order.md) promised was built on the suppression of an essential natural law.

Maliketh's subsequent behavior reveals the weight of this charge. After the Rune of Death was partially stolen during the Night of the Black Knives, Maliketh *"sealed away the blade so that it might never be stolen again"*[^1], retreating to [Crumbling Farum Azula](../locations/farum-azula.md) -- a decaying temple suspended outside of time. His guilt and paranoia are evident: he failed his one purpose, and the consequences were world-shattering.

### The Night of the Black Knives

[Ranni](../demigods/ranni.md) orchestrated the theft of a fragment of Destined Death from Maliketh. She provided this fragment to the Black Knife Assassins, who imbued their blades with its power. On a single night, they struck at Godwyn the Golden, Marika's firstborn son.

But the death was imperfect. The stolen fragment was incomplete, and the resulting deaths were "half-deaths":

- **Godwyn** suffered a death of the soul while his body lived on. His soulless body sank into the Erdtree's roots, becoming a source of Deathroot and the patron of Those Who Live in Death. His face -- slack, fishlike, monstrous -- can be found at the base of the Erdtree in the Deeproot Depths, still growing.
- **Ranni** suffered a death of the body while her soul survived. She escaped into a doll's body crafted by the snow witch, freeing herself from the [Two Fingers](the-fingers.md)' control[^2].

These complementary half-deaths suggest they were linked -- perhaps ritually required to occur simultaneously. The stolen Rune of Death was split between two targets, ensuring neither received the full force of true death.

### The Consequences: Deathroot and Those Who Live in Death

With Godwyn's soulless body fused into the Erdtree's roots, Deathroot began to spread through the root system like a cancer. Deathroot corrupts those who consume it (as Gurranq/Maliketh does in his anguish) and creates environments of death-taint.

Those Who Live in Death -- skeletons, revenants, death-birds, and other undead -- are the most visible consequence of Destined Death's removal. They cannot truly die because death itself is sealed away. The Golden Order declares them abominations and hunts them, but this is deeply hypocritical: it was the Order's own decision to remove Destined Death that created the undead in the first place.

[Rogier](../npcs/rogier.md), who investigates the bloodstain beneath Stormveil Castle, discovers the Deathroot contamination and becomes afflicted with death-taint as a result. His obsession with understanding "what lies below Stormveil" reflects the broader truth: death's corruption lies beneath the entire system, rotting it from within.

### Lichdragon Fortissax: Fighting Death From Within

Lichdragon Fortissax provides a poignant illustration of Destined Death's corrupting power. *"Fortissax was an ancient dragon and a familiar to the hero Godwyn the Golden. When Godwyn was first marked by Destined Death, the ancient dragon fought the Death within him for the longest time, but was at last broken by it -- becoming the Lichdragon and keeper of Deathblight."*[^3]

Even an ancient dragon could not contain the infection. Fortissax's transformation into an undead dragon demonstrates that Destined Death, once unleashed improperly, corrupts everything it touches. The friendship between Fortissax and Godwyn -- a dragon and a demigod, allies in the Golden Order -- was destroyed by the very rune the Order had tried to suppress.

## Connections & Analysis

### The Paradox of Immortality

Marika's removal of Destined Death was meant to protect her children, but it created a world of suffering. Without true death:

- The dead cannot rest, leading to Those Who Live in Death.
- The Erdtree's cycle is broken, leading to root corruption.
- The demigods became stagnant, unable to die and therefore unable to truly change.
- The Tarnished themselves are undying -- they return from death at Sites of Grace, which is both their greatest strength and a symptom of the broken cycle.

The irony is that Marika's attempt to protect her children from death led directly to the death of Godwyn -- the one death the system was designed to prevent. And because Destined Death was improperly applied (only a fragment, only a half-death), Godwyn's "death" was worse than true death would have been.

### Fia's Ending: Restoring the Cycle

[Fia](../npcs/fia.md), the Deathbed Companion, sought to mend the Elden Ring by returning Destined Death to it. Her Mending Rune of the Death-Prince states: *"The Golden Order was founded upon the separation of life from death. The Death-Prince would undo that separation, allowing those who live in death to find a rightful place."*[^4]

The [Age of Duskborn](../endings/age-of-duskborn.md) is the most targeted critique of the Golden Order's founding decision. It does not reject the Order wholesale (like Ranni's ending) or destroy everything (like the Frenzied Flame). It identifies the single most catastrophic policy -- the removal of Destined Death -- and reverses it. In this ending, death returns to the world, the undead find peace, and the cycle is restored.

### Destined Death and Melina

[Melina](../demigods/melina.md)'s connection to Destined Death is strongly implied but never confirmed. In the Lord of Frenzied Flame ending, she appears with her left eye open -- burning with dark flame -- and declares: *"Lord of Frenzied Flame... I will deliver thee thy destined death."*[^5] The Gloam-Eyed Queen, a figure from before the current age, wielded Destined Death and had the power to slay gods. Melina's dark eye, her connection to Marika, and her promise to deliver Destined Death all suggest she may be the Gloam-Eyed Queen or her successor.

### The Black Knives and Marika's Homeland

The Black Knife Assassins are Numen women, from the same mysterious homeland as Marika. Their involvement in the Night of the Black Knives raises the question of whether Marika herself facilitated the theft of Destined Death from her own guardian. If so, then the Night of the Black Knives, the death of Godwyn, and the Shattering were all part of a single plan to dismantle the Golden Order from within.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "Why did Marika remove Destined Death from the Elden Ring?"
    **Interpretation A (Love):** Marika wanted to protect her children from death. She acted as a mother, not a strategist.
    **Interpretation B (Control):** Removing death gave Marika leverage over her children and the world. Immortal demigods are still subject to their immortal mother.
    **Interpretation C (Rebellion's first step):** Removing Destined Death was the first stage of Marika's long rebellion against the Greater Will. By altering the Ring, she proved it could be altered -- and would later shatter it entirely.
    **Gap:** No source describes Marika's reasoning at the time of the decision.

!!! question "Was Godwyn specifically targeted, or was he collateral?"
    **Interpretation A:** Ranni needed a complementary death to free her soul from her body. Godwyn was chosen because his death would cause maximum disruption to the Golden Order.
    **Interpretation B:** Godwyn was targeted because Marika needed a loss profound enough to justify shattering the Ring. He was the sacrifice that made the Shattering possible.
    **Gap:** Whether Ranni chose Godwyn specifically or whether any demigod would have served is never stated.

!!! question "Can Destined Death be fully restored to the Elden Ring?"
    **Interpretation A:** Fia's ending accomplishes this, returning the Rune of Death to the Ring and restoring the natural cycle.
    **Interpretation B:** Fia's Mending Rune of the Death-Prince is not the original Rune of Death but a new construct. The original rune remains sealed in Maliketh's blade, which the player destroys. True Destined Death may be gone forever.
    **Gap:** The relationship between Maliketh's sealed rune, the stolen fragment, and Fia's Mending Rune is never explicitly defined.
