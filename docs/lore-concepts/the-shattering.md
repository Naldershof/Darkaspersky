# The Shattering

> The cataclysmic war that erupted among the demigods after Queen Marika shattered the Elden Ring, fracturing both the governing law of reality and the political order of the Lands Between.

## Overview

The Shattering refers to two intertwined events: the breaking of the [Elden Ring](the-elden-ring.md) by Queen Marika, and the subsequent war among her demigod children over the resulting shards -- the [Great Runes](the-great-runes.md). What had been a unified divine order governed by the [Golden Order](the-golden-order.md) collapsed into warring factions, each demigod claiming a fragment of the Ring and fighting to impose their vision on the Lands Between.

The war produced no victor. By the time the player arrives, the demigods have retreated into their strongholds, diminished and corrupted. The Erdtree is sealed behind thorns. The Tarnished are called back by Grace to do what the demigods could not: reassemble the Ring and become Elden Lord.

## Lore Details

### The Precipitating Event: The Night of the Black Knives

Before the Elden Ring was shattered, an event known as the Night of the Black Knives shook the foundations of the Golden Order. [Ranni the Witch](../demigods/ranni.md), daughter of Rennala and Radagon, orchestrated the theft of a fragment of the [Rune of Death](destined-death.md) from [Maliketh](../demigods/marika-radagon.md), Marika's shadow-bound beast and the keeper of Destined Death. Ranni provided this stolen fragment to the Black Knife Assassins -- Numen women, possibly from Marika's own homeland -- who used it to kill Godwyn the Golden, Marika's firstborn and most beloved son[^1].

Godwyn's death was unprecedented. The Rune of Death had been sealed away precisely to prevent the demigods from dying. But the stolen fragment created an imperfect death: Godwyn's soul was killed while his body lived on, becoming a grotesque, undead thing that seeped corruption into the Erdtree's roots. Simultaneously, Ranni's body was killed while her soul survived, escaping into a doll crafted by the snow witch of the Carian Manor.

This "half-death" corrupted the natural cycle and spawned Deathroot, Those Who Live in Death, and the Deathblight that plagues the Lands Between.

### Marika Shatters the Ring

In the aftermath of Godwyn's death, Marika shattered the Elden Ring. Her own words, preserved at the Queen's Bedchamber in Leyndell, confirm the act was deliberate: *"O Radagon, leal hound of the Golden Order. Thou'rt yet to become me, thou'rt yet to become a god. Let us both be shattered, mine other self."*[^2]

She also acknowledged the consequences: *"Then, a Loss. One which should not have been possible. An impossible Loss. But the Elden Ring, and I are one. If it shall shatter, then so shall I. And so it has."*[^2]

Since Marika and the Elden Ring were one, shattering it was an act of self-destruction. The Greater Will's response was swift: Marika was imprisoned within the Erdtree, her body crucified and broken, where she remained until the Tarnished arrived.

### The War Among the Demigods

With the Elden Ring broken, its shards -- the Great Runes -- were claimed by the demigods. War erupted as each sought to gather enough shards to restore the Ring under their own authority. The key combatants:

- **Godrick the Grafted** claimed the weakest Great Rune and retreated to Stormveil Castle, where he compensated for his weakness by grafting the limbs of the fallen onto his own body[^3]. He was universally despised, even by his own servants.
- **Radahn the Starscourge** held Caelid with the mightiest army. His Great Rune raised maximum HP, FP, and Stamina, reflecting his vast fortitude[^4]. His battle with Malenia would become the defining clash of the war.
- **Malenia, Blade of Miquella** fought on behalf of her twin brother [Miquella](../demigods/miquella.md). Undefeated until she clashed with Radahn in Caelid, where she unleashed the Scarlet Rot in desperation, destroying Radahn's mind and rendering all of Caelid a rotting wasteland[^5].
- **Morgott, the Omen King** defended Leyndell and the Erdtree. Despite being born an Omen -- cursed, shunned, and imprisoned by his own kin -- he was the last demigod to stand in the Erdtree's defense[^6].
- **Mohg, Lord of Blood** operated in secret, abducting Miquella and seeking to raise him to godhood through the power of the [Formless Mother](the-formless-mother.md)[^7].
- **Rykard, Lord of Blasphemy** fed himself and his Great Rune to the God-Devouring Serpent at Volcano Manor, becoming something beyond a demigod[^8].
- **Ranni** had already abandoned her claim, discarding her Great Rune and her Two Fingers to pursue the [Age of Stars](../endings/age-of-stars.md)[^9].

### The Stalemate

No demigod proved strong enough to claim all the Great Runes. The war ground to a halt. Radahn, driven mad by the Scarlet Rot, now wanders the deserts of Caelid eating corpses. Malenia slumbers at the foot of the Haligtree, waiting for Miquella who will never come. Godrick cowers in Stormveil. Morgott stands alone in the capital. The Lands Between is a frozen battlefield, its people suffering under broken governance and undying horrors.

## Connections & Analysis

### Marika's Deliberate Design

The Shattering was not a single rash act but the culmination of a long-planned rebellion against the [Greater Will](the-greater-will.md). Marika first stripped [Grace](grace-and-the-tarnished.md) from the Tarnished and exiled them, with the explicit plan that they would return after death: *"Then, after thy death, I will give back what I once claimed."*[^2] She then shattered the Ring, knowing the Tarnished would be called back to reassemble it. The implication is staggering: Marika engineered both the crisis and its solution, ensuring that the Tarnished -- not the Greater Will's chosen demigods -- would determine the new order.

This connects directly to [Ranni's parallel rebellion](../demigods/ranni.md). Both mother and daughter worked to undermine the Greater Will, but through different means: Marika from within the system, Ranni from without. The Night of the Black Knives may have been coordinated between them, though the evidence is circumstantial.

### The Shattering as Revelation

The war exposed what the Golden Order had suppressed. [Morgott's](../demigods/morgott.md) loyalty revealed the Order's hypocrisy -- its most devoted defender was an Omen it had condemned. Mohg's blood dynasty revealed the horrors hidden beneath the Subterranean Shunning-Grounds. Godrick's grafting laid bare how far the "golden lineage" had fallen. Each demigod's corruption is a mirror of a flaw in the Golden Order itself.

### Timeline Implications

The chronology matters: the Night of the Black Knives preceded the Shattering, and Godwyn's death appears to have been the trigger -- or at least the pretext -- for Marika's act. The question of whether Marika and Ranni coordinated their actions is one of the deepest unresolved questions in the lore (see below).

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "Did Marika shatter the Elden Ring deliberately, or in grief-stricken madness?"
    **Interpretation A (Deliberate):** Marika's own words confirm intent: "Let us both be shattered, mine other self." She had already engineered the Tarnished's exile and return, suggesting a long-term plan. The Shattering was the next phase.
    **Interpretation B (Grief):** Godwyn's death -- her firstborn, her most beloved -- may have pushed her past the breaking point. The Shattering was real but driven by maternal agony, not cold calculation.
    **Interpretation C (Both):** Marika may have always planned to shatter the Ring but used Godwyn's death as the catalyst. Grief and strategy are not mutually exclusive.
    **Gap:** No source definitively confirms whether the Night of the Black Knives was coordinated with Marika or occurred independently of her plans.

!!! question "Did Marika and Ranni coordinate the Night of the Black Knives?"
    **Interpretation A:** The Black Knife Assassins are Numen women -- from Marika's homeland. This connection suggests Marika may have facilitated Ranni's plot, or at least tacitly allowed it.
    **Interpretation B:** Ranni acted independently, stealing the Death Rune fragment through her own agents. Marika's reaction to Godwyn's death was genuine shock, which then motivated the Shattering.
    **Gap:** The relationship between Marika and Ranni before the Shattering is never directly described in any source.

!!! question "Why did the war produce no victor?"
    **Interpretation A:** The Great Runes are fundamentally incomplete without the full Ring, and no single demigod was strong enough to take all the others' shards by force.
    **Interpretation B:** Marika designed it this way -- the Shattering was meant to create a stalemate that only the returning Tarnished could break.
    **Gap:** Whether the stalemate is an accident of power balance or part of Marika's design remains ambiguous.
