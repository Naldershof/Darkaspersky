# The Dark Moon

> Ranni's patron -- the enigmatic cosmic entity behind the full moon of Raya Lucaria and the dark moon of the Carian royal house. Connected to the stars, fate, and the promise of an age free from the Greater Will's influence. The least understood of all the Outer Gods.

## Overview

The Dark Moon is the most mysterious cosmic power in Elden Ring. Unlike the [Greater Will](the-greater-will.md), which has institutions, agents, and a visible theology, or the [Frenzied Flame](the-frenzied-flame.md), which has a coherent philosophy of annihilation, the Dark Moon is defined primarily by what it is not. It is not the Greater Will. It is not the golden light of the Erdtree. It is cold, distant, dark, and it promises freedom from cosmic interference -- not by destroying the world, but by removing the gods from mortal affairs.

The Dark Moon's primary association is with [Ranni the Witch](../demigods/ranni.md) and the Carian royal family. [Rennala](../demigods/rennala.md) is the Queen of the Full Moon, and the Academy of Raya Lucaria studies glintstone sorcery derived from stellar and lunar power. But within the Carian tradition, there is a distinction between the full moon (associated with Rennala and the Academy) and the dark moon (associated with Ranni and the path she chooses). The dark moon is the deeper, hidden truth behind the bright surface.

## Lore Details

### The Full Moon and the Dark Moon

The Carian royal family and the Academy of Raya Lucaria both draw power from the moon and stars. Glintstone sorcery -- the magic of the Academy -- is derived from the cosmos: the glintstone itself is believed to be fragments of stars or the residue of stellar power. The full moon is Rennala's symbol, represented in her Queen of the Full Moon title and the Regal Sorcery she commands.

The dark moon is something different. The Dark Moon Ring -- the engagement ring Ranni gives to the player -- is described as *"a cold, dark blue, like the moon that guides the chill night"*[^1]. This is not the warm, bright full moon of Rennala's sorcery. It is a colder, more distant celestial body -- one associated with night, cold, and the unknown.

The distinction between full moon and dark moon may represent two aspects of the same celestial power: the visible, institutionalized magic of the Academy (full moon) versus the hidden, revolutionary power that Ranni channels (dark moon). Or they may be two separate entities entirely -- one a natural astronomical body, the other an Outer God wearing the moon's face.

### Ranni and the Dark Moon's Influence

[Ranni](../demigods/ranni.md) is the Dark Moon's primary agent in the Lands Between, though she would likely reject the word "agent." Her entire questline is built around escaping cosmic control -- she killed her own body to free herself from the [Two Fingers](the-fingers.md), she sought the Fingerslayer Blade to sever the Greater Will's hold, and her ending establishes an order of stars and moon that is kept *"far from the earth beneath our feet"*[^2].

Ranni's relationship with the Dark Moon appears to be one of patronage rather than servitude. She does not worship the Dark Moon the way the Golden Order worships the Greater Will. She does not perform rituals for it or enforce its will. Instead, the Dark Moon seems to have empowered her quest for independence, providing the cosmic backing she needed to challenge the Greater Will's monopoly.

At the Cathedral of Manus Celes, where Ranni's questline culminates, the player finds her original Empyrean body beneath the cathedral, beside the dead Two Fingers she killed. The player places the Dark Moon Ring on her finger, and she rises in a new form -- spectral, blue, crowned with a halo of dark moonlight. This transformation is the Dark Moon's investiture: Ranni becomes its vessel, or its partner, or perhaps simply someone it has chosen to support.

### The Age of Stars: The Dark Moon's Order

Ranni's ending establishes a new cosmic order:

*"Mine will be an order not of gold, but the stars and moon of the chill night. I would keep them far from the earth beneath our feet. As I have learned from my mistakes, the fingers and the greater will are not to be trusted."*[^2]

*"Here beginneth the chill night that encompasses all, reaching the great beyond. Into fear, doubt, and loneliness. As the path stretcheth into darkness."*[^3]

The Dark Moon's order is characterized by:

1. **Distance.** The new order will be kept far from the earth, not imposed upon it. This is the antithesis of the Greater Will's micromanagement through the Erdtree and the Two Fingers.
2. **Uncertainty.** "Fear, doubt, and loneliness" -- Ranni does not promise comfort or guidance. She offers freedom, and freedom includes the freedom to be afraid.
3. **Darkness.** Not darkness as evil, but darkness as the unknown -- the night sky, the stars, the vast cosmos beyond mortal comprehension.

This is the most philosophically nuanced ending in the game. It does not fix the Golden Order (like the Age of Order), restore a missing principle (like the Age of Duskborn), or destroy everything (like the Frenzied Flame). It removes cosmic oversight entirely, trusting mortals to find their own way in the dark.

### The Stars and Fate

Before the Shattering, [Radahn](../demigods/radahn.md) -- the Starscourge -- held the stars in place through his gravitational magic. This was not merely a display of strength; it was a strategic act that prevented the stars (and whatever power they carry) from moving. Ranni's questline requires the player to defeat Radahn, releasing the stars and allowing a star to crash into Limgrave, opening the path to Nokron.

The connection between the stars and fate is implied throughout the game. The Nox people of the Eternal Cities worshipped the stars and the dark moon. The Naturalborn of the Void -- *"a malformed star born from the void"* -- was connected to the fall of the Eternal Cities, which were *"cast underground"*[^4]. The Greater Will may have suppressed the Eternal Cities precisely because they revered the stars rather than the Erdtree, viewing stellar worship as a rival faith.

If the stars represent an alternative cosmic system -- one aligned with the Dark Moon rather than the Greater Will -- then Radahn's holding of the stars was an act of suppression on the Greater Will's behalf, and Ranni's release of them was an act of liberation.

### The Nox and the Eternal Cities

The Nox civilization, found in the underground cities of Nokron and Nokstella, worshipped the stars and the moon. Their civilization was *"cast underground"* by an unnamed power -- almost certainly the Greater Will, which punished them for seeking forbidden knowledge (the creation of the Fingerslayer Blade and possibly the attempted recreation of a dark lord).

The Nox are the Dark Moon's earliest known followers. Their civilization was destroyed for their allegiance, but their legacy persists: the Fingerslayer Blade they forged is essential to Ranni's quest, and the Mimic Tear (another Nox creation) is one of the game's most powerful tools. The Nox were defeated but not erased; their weapons and knowledge survive to serve the Dark Moon's champion.

## Connections & Analysis

### The Dark Moon as Anti-Greater Will

The Dark Moon's philosophical position is unique among the Outer Gods: it does not seek to replace the Greater Will's order with its own domination. It seeks to remove cosmic domination entirely. This makes it qualitatively different from the Frenzied Flame (which replaces order with annihilation), the [Rot God](outer-god-of-rot.md) (which replaces order with decay), or the [Formless Mother](the-formless-mother.md) (which wants access to the system through an Empyrean).

The Dark Moon's offer is liberation -- but liberation without comfort. Ranni's ending does not promise a golden age or a paradise. It promises night, cold, and the freedom that comes with being truly alone. This is an existentialist answer to a theological problem: if the gods are untrustworthy, the solution is not better gods but no gods at all.

### The Moon and the Erdtree: Rival Lights

The visual symbolism is striking: the Erdtree radiates golden light (day, warmth, certainty), while the Dark Moon casts silver-blue light (night, cold, mystery). These are rival cosmologies expressed through color and light:

| | Erdtree / Greater Will | Dark Moon |
|---|---|---|
| **Light** | Golden, warm | Silver-blue, cold |
| **Time** | Day, eternal noon | Night, the chill dark |
| **Promise** | Order, certainty, hierarchy | Freedom, uncertainty, solitude |
| **Relationship to mortals** | Governs from within | Watches from afar |
| **Death** | Consumed by the tree | Unknown |

### Rennala and Ranni: Mother and Daughter, Full Moon and Dark

[Rennala](../demigods/rennala.md) serves the full moon; Ranni serves the dark moon. The mother represents the institutionalized, academic understanding of lunar power; the daughter represents its revolutionary, hidden potential. Rennala's moon is studied at the Academy; Ranni's moon is pursued through rebellion and sacrifice.

The tragedy of Rennala -- abandoned by Radagon, driven to madness, endlessly rebirthing her students -- is also a tragedy of the full moon tradition: it was co-opted, abandoned, and left to decay. Ranni's dark moon path can be read as a correction -- a refusal to let the moon's power be tamed by institutions or claimed by the Golden Order through Radagon's marriage.

### The Least Understood Outer God

The Dark Moon is deliberately underdefined. The Greater Will has the Elden Beast, the Two Fingers, and the Erdtree. The Frenzied Flame has a theology, a history, and the Three Fingers. The Dark Moon has... the moon. And Ranni. And the vague allegiance of a destroyed underground civilization.

This ambiguity may be the point. The Dark Moon offers an order of unknowing -- an order where not everything is explained, catalogued, and controlled. Its mystery is not a failure of worldbuilding but a thematic statement: some things should remain beyond comprehension.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "Is the Dark Moon an Outer God, or something else?"
    **Interpretation A:** The Dark Moon is an Outer God comparable to the Greater Will, with its own cosmic agenda.
    **Interpretation B:** The Dark Moon is not an entity at all but a symbol -- a celestial body that represents freedom from cosmic influence, adopted by Ranni as her banner.
    **Interpretation C:** The Dark Moon is the residual power of Placidusax's fled god -- the cosmic power that ruled before the Greater Will and still lingers in the stars.
    **Gap:** No source confirms the Dark Moon's nature as an Outer God. It is associated with cosmic power but never explicitly categorized.

!!! question "Does Ranni serve the Dark Moon, or does the Dark Moon serve Ranni?"
    **Interpretation A:** Ranni is the Dark Moon's chosen, empowered by it to achieve its goals (removing the Greater Will).
    **Interpretation B:** Ranni uses the Dark Moon's power as a tool, maintaining her independence. She is no one's servant.
    **Interpretation C:** Their relationship is mutual -- a partnership between a mortal Empyrean and a cosmic power, each needing the other.
    **Gap:** Ranni never describes herself as serving the Dark Moon. Her language is always about her own will and her own order.

!!! question "What happens to the Lands Between under the Age of Stars?"
    **Interpretation A:** The Lands Between enters a period of freedom and self-determination, with cosmic influence removed. Mortals govern themselves.
    **Interpretation B:** The "chill night" is not liberation but abandonment. Without cosmic guidance, the Lands Between descends into chaos.
    **Interpretation C:** The Age of Stars is a transitional period -- the Greater Will's influence is removed, but eventually the Dark Moon (or another power) will fill the vacuum.
    **Gap:** Ranni's ending dialogue describes the beginning of the chill night but says nothing about what comes after. The long-term consequences are entirely unknown.
