# The Gloam-Eyed Queen

> The most dangerous enemy the Golden Order ever faced — a god-slayer who wielded Destined Death itself, led an army that literally skinned gods alive, and whose identity remains the game's deepest unsolved mystery.

## Overview

The Gloam-Eyed Queen is never seen in Elden Ring. She exists only through the items and incantations of her followers — the Godskin Apostles and Nobles — and through scattered references in weapon descriptions. Yet the evidence trail she left behind describes one of the most formidable figures in the game's history: a leader who wielded the power of [Destined Death](destined-death.md), forged it into the black flame, and led a cult whose explicit purpose was the killing and flaying of gods.

She was defeated by [Maliketh](../locations/farum-azula.md), Marika's shadow-bound beast, who stripped her of Destined Death and sealed it within himself. After her fall, the Godskin cult was scattered, its apostles left wandering the Lands Between as remnants of a failed crusade against the [Greater Will](the-greater-will.md).

But the game refuses to let her story end there. The evidence points insistently toward a living connection — one that leads directly to [Melina](../demigods/melina.md).

## The Evidence Chain

### What We Know For Certain

These facts are stated directly in item descriptions:

1. **She existed.** The Godslayer's Greatsword names her as "leader of the Godskin Apostles" and "sole survivor of the godskin cult."
2. **She wielded Destined Death.** The Godslayer's Seal calls her "the first to wield the power of Destined Death." Not merely a user — the *first* or *primary* vessel of this power.
3. **She created the black flame.** Multiple incantation descriptions state the black flame "was born of" or "bestowed by" the Gloam-Eyed Queen. The black flame is explicitly linked to Destined Death — when Maliketh sealed Destined Death, the black flame lost its god-slaying potency (Scouring Black Flame description).
4. **She led a crusade against the Greater Will.** The Black Flame Tornado description names "the Greater Will" as the Godskins' explicit enemy. This wasn't anti-Erdtree rebellion — it was anti-cosmic-order warfare.
5. **She was defeated by Maliketh.** He sealed the power of Destined Death, breaking the black flame's full potency and scattering the cult.
6. **She survived.** The Godslayer's Greatsword calls her the "sole survivor." She was not killed — she was defeated, stripped of power, and... then what?

### The Black Flame = Destined Death

This is the critical link the game establishes but never states in a single line. Reconstructing from multiple sources:

- The black flame "was born of the Gloam-Eyed Queen" (Black Flame description)
- The black flame "once burned so fiercely it could slay a god" (Scouring Black Flame)
- "Maliketh sealed the power of Destined Death, and the true power of the black flame was lost" (Scouring Black Flame)
- The Gloam-Eyed Queen was "the first to wield Destined Death" (Godslayer's Seal)

**Synthesis:** The Gloam-Eyed Queen channeled Destined Death into the form of the black flame. When Maliketh took Destined Death from her, the black flame was reduced to a shadow of its former power — still dangerous, but no longer capable of true deicide. The Godskin incantations the player finds are the weakened remnants.

### The Godskin Practice

The Godskins didn't just kill gods — they *skinned* them and wore the skin. This is not metaphorical:

- "Peeled the skin of the gods, claiming it for their own" (Godskin Peeler)
- "Their robes are made of skin peeled from their victims — those with divine blood" (Apostle Set)
- "The Nobles gorged on divine skin until they grew corpulent" (Noble Presence)
- "A swathe of cloth once used to wrap a newborn god" (Godskin Swaddling Cloth)

The Godskins practiced ritualized deicide: they weakened gods with the Black Flame Ritual, killed them with Destined Death-infused weapons, and then harvested their skin. The Windmill Village's dancing women and their flaying practices may be a surviving folk derivation of Godskin ritual.

### The Spiralhorn and the Crucible Connection

The Spiralhorn Shield states that the spiralhorn "was said to be beloved by the Gloam-Eyed Queen." Horns are a [Crucible](the-erdtree.md) attribute — the same primordial force that manifests in the [Omen](../demigods/morgott.md). This connects the Gloam-Eyed Queen to the pre-Golden Order age, the Crucible, and the bestial/primordial forces the Golden Order suppressed.

Maliketh himself is a beast clergyman of the Crucible tradition. The beast clergy worship the primordial Erdtree. That the Gloam-Eyed Queen also has Crucible connections suggests both she and Maliketh draw from the same ancient power source — but they are on opposite sides. Maliketh guards Destined Death *for* [Marika](../demigods/marika-radagon.md); the Queen wielded it *against* the divine order.

## Connections & Analysis

### The Melina Question

The game builds a case for Melina being the Gloam-Eyed Queen through accumulation rather than direct statement:

| Evidence | Gloam-Eyed Queen | Melina |
|----------|-------------------|--------|
| **Eye** | "Gloam-eyed" — an eye associated with twilight/dark flame | Left eye permanently closed; when opened (Frenzied Flame ending), burns with dark flame |
| **Destined Death** | "First to wield Destined Death" | Vows to "deliver thee thy destined death" in the Frenzied Flame ending |
| **Marika connection** | Unknown, but wielded power Marika controlled | Says she was "born at the foot of the Erdtree" with purpose given by her "mother" (Marika) |
| **Anti-divine mission** | Led crusade against the Greater Will | Offers to burn the Erdtree — the Greater Will's seat of power |
| **Defeat by Maliketh** | Defeated, stripped of power, survived | Exists in a diminished, bodiless state — consistent with someone stripped of power |
| **Fire/kindling** | Black flame (Destined Death's expression) | Offers herself as "kindling" to burn the Erdtree — fire that can destroy the divine |

The convergence is striking. If Melina is NOT the Gloam-Eyed Queen, the game has created an extraordinary number of false parallels.

**The strongest counter-argument:** Maliketh defeated the Gloam-Eyed Queen and sealed Destined Death. If Melina is the Queen, how does she still possess the ability to kindle the Erdtree's burning? Possible answers:
- Maliketh took the *Rune* of Death but couldn't fully strip the Queen's innate connection to it
- Marika reconstituted the defeated Queen into Melina, repurposing her for a different mission
- The power of Destined Death is not a single artifact but a force that can have multiple expressions

### The Marika Orchestration Theory

The deepest reading connects all three figures — Marika, the Gloam-Eyed Queen, and Melina — into a single scheme:

1. **Marika creates or empowers the Gloam-Eyed Queen** — possibly her own daughter, given the power of Destined Death as a weapon against the Greater Will
2. **The Queen's crusade threatens the Golden Order too visibly** — Marika has Maliketh defeat the Queen and seal Destined Death, removing the weapon from play but keeping it available
3. **The defeated Queen becomes Melina** — diminished, bodiless, her purpose redirected from crusade to kindling
4. **Marika engineers the Shattering** — creating the conditions where a Tarnished can reach the Forge and deploy Melina's kindling
5. **Melina burns the Erdtree** — the Gloam-Eyed Queen's original mission accomplished through indirection rather than direct assault

This reading makes the Gloam-Eyed Queen the prototype for Marika's rebellion — a first attempt that was too overt. Melina is the refined version: instead of a crusade, a single precise deployment of Destined Death at the right moment.

### The Godskin Placement Map

The Godskins' positions in the game world tell a story of strategic vigil:

- **Divine Towers** — Where Great Runes are activated (the Godskins guard approaches to divine power)
- **Windmill Village** — Where the flaying practice survives in folk ritual
- **Volcano Manor** — Near Rykard's anti-Erdtree rebellion (allies of convenience?)
- **Crumbling Farum Azula** — Near Maliketh himself (their nemesis and the seal on their power)

They are still, centuries after the Queen's defeat, positioned to threaten the Golden Order's infrastructure. They may be leaderless, but their institutional memory persists. If Melina IS the Queen, the Godskins are her abandoned army — and they don't know it.

### The Empyrean Question

Was the Gloam-Eyed Queen an Empyrean? The game never states this directly, but consider:

- She wielded Destined Death — a power of cosmic significance
- She threatened the Greater Will itself — suggesting power on a divine scale
- She survived Maliketh's assault — suggesting extraordinary resilience
- If she is Marika's daughter, she would be a candidate for Empyrean status (like Malenia, Miquella, Ranni)

The Two Fingers choose Empyreans. If the Queen was chosen but rebelled (like [Ranni](../demigods/ranni.md)), this would explain the Greater Will's response — sending Maliketh to crush the rebellion and seal the weapon.

### The Black Flame as Pre-Golden Order Power

The black flame opposes holy/golden damage. It scales with faith but produces dark fire rather than golden light. This inversion suggests the black flame is the Golden Order's shadow — the same power source expressed through negation rather than affirmation.

If the Crucible (the primordial Erdtree) is the source of all divine power, both the Golden Order's incantations and the black flame may derive from it. The Golden Order channels the Crucible through order and hierarchy; the black flame channels it through death and dissolution. The Gloam-Eyed Queen may represent an older, more primal expression of divine power — one the Golden Order sanitized and suppressed.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "Is the Gloam-Eyed Queen Melina, Ranni, Marika, or someone else entirely?"
    **Melina (strongest evidence):** The dark burning eye, Destined Death invocation, Marika's daughter, bodiless state consistent with a defeated/stripped entity, kindling as Destined Death deployment.
    **Ranni (weaker but present):** Ranni orchestrated the theft of Destined Death via the Black Knives. She killed her own body. She has one closed eye (though a different eye). But Ranni explicitly serves the Dark Moon, not the black flame tradition.
    **Marika (most radical):** If Marika is Radagon (two-in-one), could she also have been the Queen? This would make the Queen another persona — one that wielded Destined Death until Marika "defeated" her own alter-ego through Maliketh. Highly speculative but explains the inside knowledge needed to steal Destined Death later.
    **Someone else:** The game may deliberately leave the Queen unnamed to preserve mystery. She could be a figure from the pre-Erdtree age with no other in-game manifestation.

!!! question "What happened between the Queen's defeat and Melina's appearance?"
    If they are the same person, there is a gap: the Queen was defeated and stripped of Destined Death by Maliketh, then... what? Was she imprisoned? Did Marika take the defeated Queen and reshape her into Melina? Did she wander bodiless for an age before being given a new purpose? The gap is never addressed.

!!! question "Did the Godskins know about Melina?"
    If Melina is the Queen, did the wandering Godskins recognize her? Their continued vigil at divine sites suggests they await their leader's return. But they attack the player indiscriminately — they don't recognize or assist Melina. Either they don't know, or Melina's transformation left her unrecognizable.

!!! question "Why does the black flame oppose holy damage specifically?"
    Black Flame's Protection reduces incoming holy damage. If the black flame and the Golden Order's incantations both derive from the Erdtree/Crucible, why are they opposed? This suggests a fundamental schism in the nature of divine power — order vs. entropy, creation vs. death — that predates the Golden Order itself.

!!! question "What is the relationship between the Godskins and the DLC's Land of Shadow?"
    The [Land of Shadow](../dlc/land-of-shadow.md) contains pre-Golden Order civilizations and evidence of Marika's conquest. Were the Godskins active there? Did the Gloam-Eyed Queen originate from the Land of Shadow? The DLC doesn't directly address this, but the Godskins' ancient origins and Crucible connections suggest they predate the Golden Order's establishment.
