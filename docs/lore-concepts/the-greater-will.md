# The Greater Will

> The primary Outer God of the Lands Between -- the cosmic entity that sent the Elden Beast carrying the Elden Ring, established the Golden Order through Queen Marika, and governs through the Two Fingers. Whether it is benevolent, indifferent, or merely controlling is the game's deepest theological question.

## Overview

The Greater Will is the unseen architect of the Lands Between's current order. It is never encountered directly. It communicates (slowly, imperfectly) through the [Two Fingers](the-fingers.md), enacts its will through the [Elden Ring](the-elden-ring.md), and appointed Queen [Marika](../demigods/marika-radagon.md) as its vessel. The [Erdtree](the-erdtree.md) is its cathedral, the [Golden Order](the-golden-order.md) is its doctrine, and the Elden Beast is its vassal, the living embodiment of the Ring[^1].

Yet for all its apparent omnipotence, the Greater Will is strangely passive. It did not prevent Marika from removing [Destined Death](destined-death.md). It did not stop the Night of the Black Knives. It did not halt [the Shattering](the-shattering.md). Its Two Fingers fall into millennia-long deliberations when faced with crises. The Greater Will may be the most powerful entity in the game's cosmology, but it is also the most absent -- a god whose silence speaks louder than any commandment.

## Lore Details

### The Elden Beast: The Greater Will's Emissary

The Elden Remembrance provides the clearest description of the Greater Will's intervention in the Lands Between: *"The Elden Beast is a vassal beast, sent to the Lands Between long ago by the Greater Will. It became the living embodiment of the Elden Ring itself, dwelling within the Erdtree as its guardian."*[^1]

The Elden Beast is not the Greater Will. It is a servant -- a biological vessel designed to carry and embody the Elden Ring. When the player fights the Elden Beast in the game's final encounter, they are fighting the Greater Will's representative, not the Greater Will itself. The beast's arena is a vast, golden ocean within the Erdtree -- a space that feels less like a room and more like a pocket dimension, suggesting the Erdtree's interior is a threshold between the physical world and whatever realm the Greater Will inhabits.

The beast's attacks include golden projectiles, cosmic sword constructions, and ring-shaped traps -- all manifestations of the Elden Ring's power. Defeating it does not destroy the Greater Will; it merely removes the Greater Will's guardian, allowing the Tarnished to access and reconfigure the Ring.

### The Greater Will's Governance Model

The Greater Will governs through a hierarchy of intermediaries:

1. **The Greater Will** (cosmic, distant, possibly in a different plane of existence)
2. **The Two Fingers** (physical agents stationed throughout the Lands Between)
3. **The Finger Readers** (human interpreters of the Fingers' twitching)
4. **Marika** (the human vessel of the Elden Ring, empowered to shape reality)
5. **The Elden Beast** (the Ring's embodiment and final guardian)

This hierarchy creates multiple points of failure. The Two Fingers are slow. The Finger Readers may misinterpret. Marika proved disloyal. The Elden Beast can be killed. The Greater Will's system is robust in theory but brittle in practice, because it relies entirely on intermediaries that have their own wills, limitations, and potential for betrayal.

### The Greater Will Before Marika

A critical detail: the Greater Will is not the first cosmic power to govern the Lands Between. Dragonlord Placidusax *"is said to have been Elden Lord in the age before the Erdtree. Once his god was fled, the lord continued to await its return."*[^2]

Placidusax served a different god -- one that "fled" and never returned. The Greater Will appears to have filled the vacuum left by this departed deity. Whether the Greater Will drove the previous god away, or simply arrived after it left, is unknown. But the implication is clear: the Greater Will is not the Lands Between's native cosmic power. It is an interloper that installed its own system (the Elden Ring, the Erdtree, the Golden Order) over whatever preceded it.

### What Does the Greater Will Want?

This is the central question, and the game deliberately refuses to answer it clearly. Several possibilities:

**Order and stability.** The Greater Will wants a functioning reality governed by clear rules (the Elden Ring) and maintained by a faithful vessel (Marika). It does not care about individual mortal suffering so long as the system persists.

**Control and dominion.** The Greater Will wants to govern the Lands Between for its own purposes -- purposes that may not align with mortal flourishing. The Erdtree burns the dead and absorbs their essence; the Greater Will may be feeding on this cycle.

**Benign oversight.** The Greater Will is a distant but well-meaning guardian that established the Elden Ring as a gift, and the system only went wrong because of mortal failures (Marika's rebellion, the demigods' wars).

The game provides evidence for all three and conclusive proof of none.

## Connections & Analysis

### The Greater Will and Marika: A Relationship of Mutual Need

Marika was chosen by the Greater Will as its vessel, but the relationship was not purely hierarchical. Marika wielded the Elden Ring's power directly, reshaping reality by adding and removing runes. She removed Destined Death without the Greater Will stopping her. She exiled the Tarnished according to her own plan. She ultimately shattered the Ring.

Either the Greater Will could not stop Marika, or it chose not to. Both possibilities are significant:

- If it *could not*, then the Greater Will's power has limits. It can install a system but cannot prevent its chosen vessel from subverting it.
- If it *chose not to*, then the Greater Will may have its own reasons for allowing the Shattering -- perhaps the broken Ring serves some purpose the mortals cannot perceive.

The Greater Will's response to the Shattering was to imprison Marika within the Erdtree and call the Tarnished back to restore the Ring. This is a reactive, corrective measure, not a proactive one. The Greater Will cleans up the mess rather than preventing it.

### The Greater Will vs. Other Outer Gods

The Greater Will is the most powerful Outer God in the Lands Between, but it faces competition:

| Outer God | Domain | Agent | Goal |
|-----------|--------|-------|------|
| Greater Will | Order, golden light | Two Fingers, Marika | Maintain the Elden Ring's current configuration |
| [Frenzied Flame](the-frenzied-flame.md) | Chaos, annihilation | Three Fingers | Melt all back into the One Great |
| [Outer God of Rot](outer-god-of-rot.md) | Decay, consumption | Malenia (unwilling) | Consume all through the Scarlet Rot |
| [Formless Mother](the-formless-mother.md) | Blood, wounds | Mohg | Audience with an Empyrean |
| [Dark Moon](the-dark-moon.md) | Stars, cold, freedom | Ranni | Remove cosmic interference from the world |

The Frenzied Flame is its most direct opponent: where the Greater Will imposes differentiation and order, the Flame seeks to dissolve all distinctions. Hyetta's words frame this as a theological critique: *"The Greater Will made a mistake. Torment, despair, affliction... Every sin, every curse, every one, born of a mistake."*[^3] The Frenzied Flame claims that the Greater Will's act of separating the One Great into distinct beings was the original sin.

### Every Ending Diminishes the Greater Will

A remarkable fact: no ending in the game straightforwardly restores the Greater Will's authority.

- **Age of Fracture:** The Ring is restored but with fracture lines -- the order will never be as strong.
- **Age of Order (Goldmask):** Removes divine fickleness, implicitly stripping the gods (including the Greater Will's chosen) of agency.
- **Age of Duskborn (Fia):** Restores Destined Death, undoing Marika's decision but also challenging the Greater Will's approved configuration.
- **Blessing of Despair:** Spreads the Omen curse, defiling the Erdtree's blessing.
- **Age of Stars (Ranni):** Explicitly removes the Greater Will's influence: *"I would keep them far from the earth beneath our feet."*[^4]
- **Lord of Frenzied Flame:** Destroys everything.

In every case, the Tarnished -- the Greater Will's supposed tool -- acts in ways that undermine the Greater Will's control. If the Greater Will called the Tarnished back to restore its order, it made a catastrophic miscalculation. Or perhaps it did not call them back at all -- perhaps Marika did, and the Greater Will merely went along.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "Is the Greater Will benevolent, indifferent, or controlling?"
    **Interpretation A (Benevolent):** The Greater Will brought order to a chaotic world. The Elden Ring is a gift. The system went wrong due to mortal failures, not divine malice.
    **Interpretation B (Indifferent):** The Greater Will is a cosmic entity operating on scales incomprehensible to mortals. It does not care about individual suffering any more than gravity cares about what it crushes.
    **Interpretation C (Controlling):** The Greater Will is a parasite. The Erdtree burns the dead to feed it. The Golden Order is a system of spiritual extraction disguised as religion. Ranni and the Frenzied Flame both correctly identify it as an oppressor.
    **Gap:** The Greater Will never speaks, never explains itself, and is never directly encountered. Every claim about its nature comes through unreliable intermediaries.

!!! question "What was Placidusax's god, and why did it flee?"
    **Interpretation A:** The "fled god" was driven away by the Greater Will, which then installed its own system.
    **Interpretation B:** The fled god departed voluntarily, perhaps to another world. Placidusax waits in vain.
    **Interpretation C:** The fled god is still present -- perhaps the Frenzied Flame, the Rot God, or some other entity is its surviving form.
    **Gap:** No source identifies Placidusax's god or explains the circumstances of its departure.

!!! question "Did the Greater Will actually call the Tarnished back, or did Marika?"
    **Interpretation A:** The Greater Will activated the Sites of Grace to summon the Tarnished, using Marika's earlier exile as the mechanism.
    **Interpretation B:** Marika alone engineered both the exile and the return. The Greater Will had no role in calling the Tarnished back and may even oppose their interference.
    **Gap:** Marika's words at the Third Church of Marika suggest she planned the return ("I will give back what I once claimed"), but whether this plan required the Greater Will's cooperation is unclear.
