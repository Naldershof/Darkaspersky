# Grace & the Tarnished

> Grace is the guiding light of the Erdtree, and the Tarnished are those warriors who lost it, were exiled from the Lands Between, and were called back after death to restore the Elden Ring -- all according to Queen Marika's long-laid plan.

## Overview

Grace manifests as golden wisps of light emanating from Sites of Grace throughout the Lands Between. It guides the bearer toward the [Erdtree](the-erdtree.md) and, by extension, toward the [Elden Ring](the-elden-ring.md). To be "graced" is to be chosen -- favored by the Erdtree (or the [Greater Will](the-greater-will.md)) for a purpose. To be "Tarnished" is to have lost that grace: eyes dimmed, purpose removed, exiled to wage war in distant lands.

But the exile was not permanent. Queen [Marika](../demigods/marika-radagon.md) herself stripped the Tarnished of grace and sent them away, with the explicit intention that they would return after death. The Tarnished are not outcasts -- they are soldiers in Marika's war against the very order she created. Grace was given, taken, and returned as part of a plan that spans the entire timeline of the game.

## Lore Details

### What Grace Is

Grace appears mechanically as the golden light at Sites of Grace (rest points) and as trails of golden wisps that point toward the next objective. In lore terms, Grace is the Erdtree's favor -- a form of divine attention that guides the bearer and, critically, allows them to return from death. The Tarnished's ability to respawn at Sites of Grace is not a game mechanic abstracted from the story; it is the story. Grace literally prevents permanent death for those it touches.

The first thing the player sees upon emerging from the Stranded Graveyard is the Erdtree dominating the skyline, with Grace trails pointing toward Stormveil Castle[^1]. Grace establishes the game's fundamental loop: follow the light, fight, die, return, push forward. The golden wisps are the Erdtree's leash and the Tarnished's lifeline.

### The Tarnished: Origin and Exile

The Tarnished were once warriors of extraordinary valor who served [Godfrey](../demigods/godfrey.md), the first Elden Lord. After the war against the Giants, Marika dismissed them. Her words, preserved at the First Church of Marika, are explicit: *"Hark, brave warriors. Hark, my lord Godfrey. We commend your stance. But the battle is over. I bid you, warriors. Put down your arms. And rest in grace."*[^2]

But this dismissal was followed by something far more radical. At the Third Church of Marika, her words reveal the full plan: *"My Lord, and thy warriors. I divest each of thee of thy grace. With thine eyes dimmed, ye will be driven from the Lands Between. Ye will wage war in a land afar, where ye will live, and die."*[^3]

And then the crucial line: *"Then, after thy death, I will give back what I once claimed. Return to the Lands Between, and have thy deathbed companion."*[^3]

Marika did not merely exile the Tarnished. She programmed their return. The loss of Grace was temporary -- a delay, not a rejection. The Tarnished would die in foreign lands, and upon death, Grace would find them again, pulling them back to the Lands Between. The "deathbed companion" likely refers to the Finger Maiden assigned to each Tarnished (though the player's maiden died before the game begins, and [Melina](../demigods/melina.md) steps in as a substitute).

### Godfrey: The First Tarnished

[Godfrey](../demigods/godfrey.md) was the first to lose Grace and the first to be exiled. The Remembrance of Hoarah Loux describes how he *"suppressed the flame of his fighting spirit to become Godfrey, first Elden Lord"* and how *"when he was divested of grace and banished from the Lands Between, he discarded the crown and reverted to his warrior's nature"*[^4].

Godfrey's exile has a dual significance. First, it shows that Grace can be given and taken by Marika -- it is not inherent to a person but bestowed and revocable. Second, it establishes that the Tarnished retain their martial nature even without Grace. Godfrey became Hoarah Loux again, a warrior without divine purpose but not without strength. When he returns to the Lands Between, he is the final obstacle before the Erdtree, suggesting that Grace has called back not just the player but all the Tarnished -- including the most powerful among them.

### The Finger Maidens and the Roundtable Hold

Each returning Tarnished was meant to have a Finger Maiden -- an attendant who could channel Grace and help the Tarnished grow stronger (mechanically, this means leveling up). The player's Finger Maiden is found dead at the Chapel of Anticipation at the game's very start, leaving the player maiden-less until Melina offers to "play the role" of one.

Melina is not a true Finger Maiden. She says so explicitly. She is the daughter of Marika, born within the Erdtree, bodiless and spiritual[^5]. Her ability to serve as a maiden substitute -- channeling runes into strength -- suggests she has a unique connection to the Erdtree's power, possibly inherited from her mother.

The [Roundtable Hold](../locations/roundtable-hold.md) serves as a gathering place for Tarnished, guided by Grace and overseen by the Two Fingers. Its structure suggests it was purpose-built for the Tarnished's return: Hewg the blacksmith is chained there to forge god-slaying weapons, Roderika tunes spirit ashes, and Enia reads the Great Runes. Everything in the Hold exists to prepare the Tarnished for their ultimate purpose -- reaching the Erdtree and restoring the Ring.

### Why Were the Tarnished Called Back?

The in-game explanation is that Grace was returned because the Elden Ring was shattered and the Lands Between needed a new Elden Lord. But Marika's words reveal that she planned the Tarnished's return before the Shattering. She exiled them with the promise of return. She shattered the Ring knowing that the Tarnished would be the ones to pick up the pieces.

This means the Tarnished are not merely responders to a crisis. They are instruments of Marika's will. She engineered an army of undying warriors who, by definition, could not be permanently stopped by the demigods or the Golden Order's defenders. The Tarnished are Marika's weapon against her own system.

## Connections & Analysis

### Grace as Control

Grace guides, but it also constrains. The golden wisps point the Tarnished toward specific objectives -- first the demigods, then the Erdtree, then the Ring. A Tarnished who follows Grace is following a script written by the Greater Will (or Marika, or both). The Sites of Grace are checkpoints in a designed experience.

This raises a disturbing question: is the Tarnished a hero or a tool? The player has agency in choosing which ending to pursue, but the path to every ending follows the same Grace-lit road. Even Ranni's ending, which rejects the Golden Order, requires the Tarnished to first follow Grace to gather the Great Runes and defeat the Elden Beast. Freedom, in the Lands Between, begins only after the script is completed.

### The Dead Tarnished Who Came Before

Environmental storytelling reveals that many Tarnished returned before the player and failed. Dead bodies litter the approaches to every major boss. Piles of weapons and armor sit before Stormveil Castle's gates. The Stranded Graveyard through which the player arrives is a catacomb of stone coffins, each presumably containing a returned Tarnished who did not make it far[^1].

The player character is not the first Tarnished to follow Grace. They are merely the one who succeeds. This positions the player within a vast, ongoing process: Marika called back an army, and only one needs to win. The scale of attrition -- hundreds or thousands of dead Tarnished -- underscores that Marika's plan was ruthless. She did not send one champion; she sent a wave, knowing most would die.

### Melina's Purpose and Marika's Design

Melina states that her purpose was given to her by her mother inside the Erdtree *"for the purpose of a journey"*[^5]. But at the Forge of the Giants, she asserts: *"Now I act of my own volition. I have set my heart upon the world that I would have. Regardless of my mother's designs."*[^6]

Melina's arc mirrors the Tarnished's: both were given a purpose by Marika, and both must decide whether to follow it or deviate. Marika designed the system, but the individuals within it can choose differently. This is the game's central tension: destiny versus agency, the script versus the player.

### Grace and the Outer Gods

Grace comes from the Erdtree, which is the Greater Will's instrument. But Marika controls when Grace is given and taken. This creates ambiguity: is Grace the Greater Will's tool, Marika's tool, or both? If Marika can independently grant and revoke Grace, then the Greater Will's control is not absolute. Grace may be a shared resource -- or a contested one, with Marika and the Greater Will pulling the Tarnished in different directions.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "Does Grace have its own will, or is it purely instrumental?"
    **Interpretation A:** Grace is a passive force directed by whoever controls the Erdtree (Marika, the Greater Will, or both). It has no independent agency.
    **Interpretation B:** Grace has developed a form of semi-autonomous behavior, guiding Tarnished toward outcomes that may not align with any single controller's intentions.
    **Gap:** The golden wisps sometimes point toward optional content or hidden paths, raising the question of whether Grace always serves the main objective or has broader purposes.

!!! question "Why was the player's Finger Maiden killed?"
    **Interpretation A:** The Grafted Scion at the Chapel of Anticipation killed the maiden as part of the game's "you will fail first" tutorial. It was bad luck.
    **Interpretation B:** The maiden's death was orchestrated to ensure that Melina would serve as the player's companion, inserting Marika's daughter directly into the Tarnished's journey.
    **Gap:** No source explains why the player begins at the Chapel of Anticipation or who sent the Grafted Scion there.

!!! question "What happens to Grace after the Elden Ring is reconfigured?"
    **Interpretation A:** In most endings, Grace presumably continues under the new Elden Lord's authority, guiding future souls.
    **Interpretation B:** In Ranni's ending, Grace is replaced by the cold light of stars and moon. In the Frenzied Flame ending, Grace is destroyed along with everything else.
    **Gap:** None of the endings explicitly describe Grace's fate.
