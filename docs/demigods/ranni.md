# Ranni the Witch

> Empyrean daughter of Rennala and Radagon who orchestrated the Night of the Black Knives to kill her own body, freed herself from the Two Fingers, and pursues the Age of Stars -- a new order that would remove divine influence from the world entirely.

## Overview

Ranni is the daughter of [Rennala](rennala.md) and Radagon, making her -- through Radagon's identity as [Marika](marika-radagon.md) -- both a Carian princess and a child of the god-queen. She was chosen by the Two Fingers as an Empyrean, a potential successor to Marika as vessel of the Elden Ring. She rejected this destiny absolutely.

Ranni's rebellion is the most complete in the game. She stole a fragment of the Rune of Death from Maliketh, Marika's shadow-bound beast. She forged it into the weapons used by the Black Knife Assassins on the Night of the Black Knives. She orchestrated the murder of Godwyn the Golden's soul. And she used the same stolen death to kill her own Empyrean body, severing her connection to the Two Fingers and abandoning her Great Rune.

Her soul now inhabits a doll crafted in the image of her snow witch mentor. From this diminished form, she pursues the Age of Stars: a new cosmic order aligned with the Dark Moon rather than the Greater Will, one that would place the gods and their influence "far from this land."

## Lore Details

### The Night of the Black Knives

Ranni's defining act. She states it plainly: "I stole Death long ago, and search now for the dark path." The Night of the Black Knives was a coordinated assassination that killed Godwyn the Golden's soul while killing Ranni's body. This "half-death" -- one dying in body, the other in soul -- was not accidental but required by the mechanics of Destined Death when split into fragments.

The Black Knife Assassins who carried out the attack were Numen women -- the same race as [Marika](marika-radagon.md). Ranni's dialogue confirms she stole the death rune and forged the Fingerslayer Blade, but the assassins' ethnic connection to Marika raises the question of whether Marika was complicit. Ranni claims credit, but the logistics suggest collaboration between mother and (technical) daughter.

The consequences were catastrophic. Godwyn's soul-death corrupted the natural order: his body survived but rotted, producing Deathroot that spread through the Erdtree's roots. Those Who Live in Death -- beings in a state of undeath neither sanctioned by the Golden Order nor released by true death -- emerged as a new category of cursed existence. The Night of the Black Knives did not merely kill a demigod; it broke the boundary between life and death.

### The Doll Body

After her Empyrean body died, Ranni's soul transferred to a doll crafted in the image of the snow witch who mentored her. This is not a comfortable existence. She describes herself as searching for "the dark path," and her current form is fragile, dependent on magical infrastructure. But it is free. The Two Fingers cannot control a soul inhabiting a doll.

The snow witch herself is a mysterious figure -- a teacher who instructed Ranni in sorcery and, possibly, in the philosophy that led to her rebellion. The witch's face is the face Ranni now wears. In a game about identity, inheritance, and the masks people choose, Ranni wearing her teacher's face rather than her mother's is a deliberate statement.

### The Questline: Fingerslayer Blade and Cursemark

Ranni's questline tasks the player with recovering two items: the Fingerslayer Blade (forged from the stolen death rune) and the Cursemark of Death (the imprint of death left on Ranni's discarded body atop the Divine Tower of Liurnia). With both, she can permanently sever the Two Fingers' hold on the world.

The path to the Fingerslayer Blade runs through Nokron, the Eternal City -- accessible only after [Radahn](radahn.md) is defeated and the stars resume their movement. This is the most direct connection between Radahn's starholding and Ranni's plans: as long as Radahn held the stars, Ranni could not complete her quest. His defeat -- whether the player kills him at the Radahn Festival or he falls by other means -- is a prerequisite for Ranni's path.

### The Age of Stars

Ranni's ending monologue establishes her vision: "Mine will be an order not of gold, but the stars and moon of the chill night. I would keep them far from this land." She does not destroy the Elden Ring or the concept of cosmic order. She replaces the Golden Order with a new one -- aligned with the Dark Moon rather than the Greater Will, characterized by distance rather than dominion.

"Here beginneth the chill night that encompasses all, reaching the great beyond. Into fear, doubt, and loneliness. As the path stretcheth into darkness." This is not a utopian ending. Ranni's order offers freedom from divine control, but it explicitly offers "fear, doubt, and loneliness" in return. The chill night is liberation, but it is also cold.

### Blaidd, the Shadow

Blaidd is Ranni's shadow -- a half-wolf warrior assigned to her by the Two Fingers as both protector and spy. Ranni describes him with genuine warmth: "He is my brother, more than their spy. He has fought for me, always." When Ranni defies the Fingers, Blaidd's purpose and his loyalty come into irreconcilable conflict. He is programmed to serve the Fingers but loves Ranni. The result is madness: the player finds Blaidd at the end of the questline, hostile and broken, outside Ranni's Rise.

Blaidd's fate is an indictment of the Two Fingers' system. They assign shadows to Empyreans as both guardians and leashes. When the Empyrean rebels, the shadow is destroyed by the contradiction. Blaidd did nothing wrong; the system that created him made his destruction inevitable.

## Connections & Analysis

### Ranni and Marika: Parallel Rebellions

Ranni's rebellion against the Two Fingers parallels [Marika's](marika-radagon.md) rebellion against the Golden Order. Both women were chosen by the Greater Will's agents. Both accepted their roles initially. Both eventually concluded that the system was intolerable and orchestrated its destruction.

The parallels are structural:

- Marika sealed Destined Death; Ranni stole it back.
- Marika exiled Godfrey as part of a long-term plan; Ranni killed her own body as part of hers.
- Marika shattered the Elden Ring from within; Ranni severed herself from it entirely.
- Both collaborated on the Night of the Black Knives (the Numen assassins connect them).

The key difference is their endgames. Marika's plans resulted in the Shattering -- a catastrophe with no clear constructive goal. Ranni's plans culminate in the Age of Stars -- a specific, articulated alternative. Ranni learned from Marika's example and went further: not just destruction, but replacement.

### Ranni Knew About Destined Death Through Maliketh

Ranni states she stole the Rune of Death from Maliketh. But Maliketh was Marika's shadow -- her personal guardian and the keeper of her most dangerous secret. The Rune of Death was sealed within his black blade precisely because it was too powerful to leave unsecured. For Ranni to have stolen a fragment, she would have needed either extraordinary capability or inside help. The Remembrance of the Black Blade states Maliketh "upon being betrayed, sealed away the blade so that it might never be stolen again" -- the betrayal was the Night of the Black Knives, and the sealing happened after the theft, not before.

This implies the Rune of Death was less secure before the Night. Did Marika leave an opening for Ranni? Did the Two Fingers' own system (assigning shadows) create a vulnerability that Ranni exploited through her shadow-adjacent knowledge? The logistics of the theft are never fully explained, but they point to a deeper collaboration between Ranni and forces within Marika's own household.

### Ranni and Miquella: Empyrean Opposites

Both Ranni and [Miquella](miquella.md) were Empyreans who rejected the Two Fingers. Both pursued paths outside the Golden Order. But their methods and goals are antithetical:

- **Ranni** removed herself from divine power, transferring her soul to a doll and ultimately pushing the gods "far from this land."
- **Miquella** pursued godhood, shedding his humanity to ascend.
- **Ranni** preserved her identity and autonomy, even in a diminished form.
- **Miquella** systematically destroyed his identity, shedding love, charm, and compassion.

Ranni's Age of Stars says: the world does not need gods nearby. Miquella's ascent says: the world needs a better god. The DLC positions these as the two Empyrean answers to the Greater Will's failure, and the game allows the player to defeat Miquella's vision while Ranni's remains available as an ending.

### The Dark Moon

Ranni's order is aligned with the Dark Moon -- a celestial entity associated with the Carian royal family, cold, distant, and mysterious. The Dark Moon is functionally an Outer God (or its equivalent), meaning Ranni's "freedom from divine influence" is more accurately "replacement of one divine influence with another." The difference is that the Dark Moon appears to govern from a distance rather than through direct intervention.

The Dark Moon Ring, given to the player during the questline, is an engagement ring: "O Tarnished, I would have thee be my Lord Consort." Ranni's ending is framed as a marriage and a departure -- she and the player leave the Lands Between together, taking the Elden Ring into the stars. The romantic framing is genuine but also strategic: Ranni needs a consort to become the new divine authority, just as Marika needed Godfrey and then Radagon.

### The Ethical Calculus of the Night of the Black Knives

Ranni's liberation came at enormous cost. Godwyn's soul-death corrupted the Erdtree's root system, creating Deathroot and Those Who Live in Death. Entire populations were cursed by the consequences of the Night. Ranni does not express guilt about this. Her focus is entirely on her own freedom and the new order she envisions.

This raises a difficult ethical question: was Ranni's rebellion justified given its collateral damage? The game does not answer this, but it presents the consequences plainly. Every encounter with Those Who Live in Death, every instance of Deathroot corruption, traces back to the Night Ranni orchestrated. Freedom from the Two Fingers cost thousands of innocents their natural death.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "Did Ranni and Marika coordinate the Night of the Black Knives?"
    **Interpretation A:** Yes. The Numen assassins were Marika's people. The theft of Destined Death from Maliketh required inside access. The Night served both women's goals.
    **Interpretation B:** Ranni acted independently, exploiting Marika's system (the Numen connections) without Marika's direct knowledge.
    **Interpretation C:** Marika allowed the theft to happen passively -- she did not stop it because she wanted the consequences, but she did not actively coordinate.
    **Gap:** No source definitively establishes communication between Ranni and Marika before the Night.

!!! question "Is the Dark Moon truly different from the Greater Will?"
    **Interpretation A:** Yes. The Dark Moon represents a fundamentally different cosmic principle: distance, mystery, and non-intervention, as opposed to the Greater Will's control and order.
    **Interpretation B:** No. The Dark Moon is another Outer God with its own agenda. Ranni's "freedom" is simply a change of master, and the chill night will prove as constraining as the golden day.
    **Gap:** The Dark Moon's nature is deliberately obscure. Its intentions, if it has any, are never stated.

!!! question "What happens to the Lands Between after the Age of Stars?"
    **Interpretation A:** The world is freed from divine interference. Mortals govern themselves under a distant, non-interventionist cosmic order. It is frightening but liberating.
    **Interpretation B:** The "chill night" is a period of darkness and hardship. Without divine structure, the world may descend into chaos before stabilizing.
    **Interpretation C:** Ranni's departure with the Elden Ring removes the fundamental organizing principle of reality, and the consequences are unknowable.
    **Gap:** The ending provides Ranni's monologue but no view of the world she leaves behind.
