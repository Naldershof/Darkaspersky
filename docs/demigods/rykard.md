# Rykard, Lord of Blasphemy

> Praetor of the Volcano Manor who fed himself to the God-Devouring Serpent, merging with it to gain the power to devour the very gods -- a rebellion against the Golden Order pursued not through rejection or destruction but through consumption.

## Overview

Rykard is the son of Radagon and [Rennala](rennala.md), brother to [Ranni](ranni.md) and [Radahn](radahn.md). Of the three Carian children, Rykard chose the most visceral form of rebellion. Where Ranni orchestrated assassinations and pursued cosmic restructuring, and Radahn competed for dominance through martial strength, Rykard sought to devour the divine order itself -- literally.

As Praetor (a legal and military title suggesting governance), Rykard oversaw Volcano Manor on [Mt. Gelmir](../locations/mt-gelmir.md), a stronghold that became a haven for those who opposed the Erdtree. At some point, he encountered the God-Devouring Serpent -- an ancient creature whose hunger was said to be capable of consuming even the gods. Rather than fighting or taming the serpent, Rykard offered himself to it, allowing the serpent to consume him so that they would merge. The result is a grotesque fusion: a massive serpent with Rykard's face protruding from its body, still conscious, still ambitious, and now armed with an insatiable appetite for divine power.

The Remembrance of the Blasphemous states: "The serpent's hunger was insatiable; it was said to devour even the very gods themselves."

## Lore Details

### The Praetor of Volcano Manor

Before his transformation, Rykard served as Praetor of Volcano Manor -- a title that implies legal authority and military command. The Manor operates as an organized resistance against the Erdtree's order, sending Tarnished assassins (including the player) to hunt down champions of the Golden Order. Tanith, the Manor's spokesperson, manages operations in Rykard's name, suggesting that even after his transformation, his political infrastructure continued to function.

The Manor's existence is significant: it is not a chaotic uprising but a structured insurgency with recruitment, missions, and a clear chain of command. This distinguishes Rykard's rebellion from [Mohg's](mohg.md) cultish blood worship or [Ranni's](ranni.md) esoteric cosmic plan. Rykard built an organization.

### The God-Devouring Serpent

The serpent Rykard merged with is an entity whose origin is obscure. It predates Rykard's arrival and may predate the Golden Order itself. Its title -- "God-Devouring" -- implies a creature that exists specifically as a counter to divine power. In a world where gods and Outer Gods impose their will through cosmic structures (the Elden Ring, the Erdtree, the stars), the serpent represents the possibility of simply eating those structures.

The merger was voluntary. Rykard fed himself to the serpent, choosing consumption over every other option. This was not desperation; it was strategy. By becoming one with the serpent, Rykard gained access to its hunger -- a hunger he intended to direct at the gods themselves.

### Rykard's Great Rune

Rykard's Great Rune "restores HP upon defeating enemies," reflecting the serpent's hunger-as-sustenance mechanic. The description notes that Rykard "fed it to the serpent, granting the power to restore HP upon defeating enemies." Even the shard of the Elden Ring was consumed by the serpent's appetite, repurposed from a fragment of divine order into a tool of predation.

### The Serpent-Hunter and the Intended Death

Rykard's arena contains the Serpent-Hunter -- a specialized weapon designed specifically to kill him. This is a unique detail: no other demigod provides the player with the means of their destruction. The Serpent-Hunter's presence suggests that either Rykard left it there intentionally (a challenge, or a failsafe in case he lost control of the merger), or that someone who foresaw the need to kill Rykard placed it where a challenger would find it.

If Rykard placed it himself, this adds a layer of self-awareness to his blasphemy: he knew the merger might need to be undone, and he prepared for that possibility. This would make him less a monster consumed by hunger and more a calculated rebel who understood the risks of his chosen path.

## Connections & Analysis

### Three Siblings, Three Rebellions

Rykard, [Ranni](ranni.md), and [Radahn](radahn.md) are all children of Radagon and Rennala. Each responded to the Golden Order differently:

- **Ranni** rejected the Order through subterfuge, orchestrating the Night of the Black Knives and pursuing a replacement cosmic system (the Age of Stars).
- **Radahn** competed within the Order's framework, seeking dominance through martial strength and conquest.
- **Rykard** sought to devour the Order -- not replace it, not win within it, but consume it entirely.

The three approaches map to different critiques: Ranni says the system is wrong and must be replaced. Radahn says the system is a competition and must be won. Rykard says the system is food and must be eaten. Of the three, Rykard's is the most nihilistic -- there is no new order in his vision, only the serpent's endless hunger.

### Consumption as Anti-Theology

Rykard's blasphemy is not merely theological rebellion; it is a systematic inversion of the Golden Order's principles. The Erdtree absorbs the dead, recycling souls through its roots. The Golden Order is itself a system of cosmic consumption dressed in sacred language. Rykard's serpent does the same thing but without the pretense: it eats, it feeds, and it makes no claims of sanctity.

This makes Rykard an accidental critic of the Golden Order's hypocrisy. The Erdtree consumes the dead; Rykard consumes the living. The difference is aesthetic, not moral. The Golden Order calls its consumption "grace" and "returning to the Erdtree." Rykard calls his "blasphemy." But the mechanism is the same.

### Volcano Manor as Parallel Institution

Volcano Manor functions as a shadow government. It has leadership (Tanith), a military arm (the Recusants), intelligence operations (the assassination contracts), and a divine patron (Rykard). This mirrors the Erdtree's own structure: the Two Fingers as intermediaries, the Roundtable Hold as a gathering place for champions, and Marika as the divine authority.

The parallel is deliberate. Rykard did not merely rebel; he built a replacement structure. The Manor's Recusants target Tarnished who serve the Erdtree, directly undermining the competition. Patches, Diallos, and the player are all recruited through a process that mirrors being guided by grace -- but the guiding force is hunger, not light.

### Tanith and the Question of Devotion

After Rykard's defeat, Tanith can be found in his boss arena, consuming his remains. This is one of the most disturbing scenes in the game, and it raises questions about the nature of devotion within Rykard's organization. Did Tanith serve Rykard out of genuine belief in his anti-divine crusade? Was she influenced by the serpent's power? Or does her consumption of Rykard represent the logical endpoint of his philosophy -- in a system built on eating, even the leader is eventually food?

### The Blasphemous Blade and the Serpent's Legacy

The Blasphemous Blade, Rykard's signature weapon, heals the wielder upon killing enemies -- mechanically identical to Rykard's Great Rune. Both are expressions of the same principle: death feeds the wielder. This is the serpent's gift, and it persists even after Rykard's physical defeat. The philosophy of consumption outlives its prophet.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "Was the God-Devouring Serpent an Outer God's agent?"
    **Interpretation A:** The serpent was an independent entity, predating the Outer Gods' influence. Its ability to devour gods made it a threat to all cosmic powers, including the Greater Will.
    **Interpretation B:** The serpent was an agent or creation of an unknown Outer God -- a weapon designed to destroy rival divine systems.
    **Interpretation C:** The serpent was a natural creature of the Lands Between, predating the Erdtree, similar to the dragons and the crucible-era beings.
    **Gap:** The serpent's origin is never explained. Its title implies a specific purpose (devouring gods), but who or what created it for that purpose is unknown.

!!! question "Did Rykard retain his consciousness after the merger?"
    **Interpretation A:** Yes. Rykard's face is visible within the serpent, and his dialogue suggests awareness. The merger was a calculated union, not a loss of self.
    **Interpretation B:** Partially. The serpent's hunger dominated Rykard's will over time, and the figure the player fights is more serpent than man. Rykard's ambitions were consumed along with his body.
    **Gap:** Rykard's dialogue in the boss fight is limited and focused on hunger, making it difficult to assess his cognitive state.

!!! question "Could Rykard's method have actually worked?"
    **Interpretation A:** If left unchecked, the God-Devouring Serpent, empowered by a demigod's Great Rune and fed a steady diet of powerful warriors, might have eventually grown powerful enough to threaten the Erdtree and the Elden Beast.
    **Interpretation B:** The serpent's hunger is an endless cycle with no endpoint. It would consume forever without achieving anything beyond consumption. Rykard's rebellion has no victory condition.
    **Gap:** The game never tests this hypothesis. Rykard is defeated before his plan can be fully realized.
