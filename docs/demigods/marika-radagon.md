# Queen Marika the Eternal / Radagon of the Golden Order

> Marika and Radagon are two personas inhabiting a single body -- the vessel of the Elden Ring, architect of the Golden Order, and the one who ultimately shattered both.

## Overview

Queen Marika the Eternal is the god-queen of the Lands Between, chosen by the Greater Will to serve as the vessel of the Elden Ring. She established the Golden Order, waged war against the Giants, sealed away Destined Death, and presided over an age of divine rule centered on the Erdtree. She is also Radagon of the Golden Order -- a seemingly separate being who first served as champion of the Academy, married Rennala of Caria, fathered three children, and then inexplicably left Rennala to return to Marika and become her consort.

The revelation that Marika and Radagon share a single body is one of the game's central mysteries. Whether they are two souls in one vessel, two aspects of one soul, or something else entirely remains debated. What is clear is that they were sometimes in conflict: Marika shattered the Elden Ring while Radagon attempted to repair it, and Marika's own words address Radagon as "mine other self" and "leal hound of the Golden Order."

The Shadow of the Erdtree DLC reveals an even darker history: before the events of the game, Marika ordered a genocide in the Land of Shadow, sending her son [Messmer](../dlc/messmer.md) to purge the Hornsent people. This atrocity was buried and the entire land hidden from history, adding another layer to Marika's character as someone willing to commit horrific acts in pursuit of her vision.

## Lore Details

### The Ascent to Godhood

Marika was originally a Numen -- a member of an otherworldly race from beyond the Lands Between. She was chosen by the Greater Will and the Two Fingers as an Empyrean vessel, elevated to godhood through union with the Elden Ring. The Elden Beast, a vassal beast sent by the Greater Will, became the living embodiment of the Ring within the Erdtree (Elden Remembrance: "The Elden Beast is a vassal beast, sent to the Lands Between long ago by the Greater Will").

Her first consort was [Godfrey](godfrey.md), the warrior Hoarah Loux, who suppressed his battle-frenzy to become the First Elden Lord. Together they waged the wars of conquest that established the Golden Order: defeating the dragons, subjugating the Fire Giants, and extending the Erdtree's dominion across the continent.

### The Sealing of Destined Death

One of Marika's most consequential acts was removing the Rune of Death from the Elden Ring and entrusting it to her shadow-bound beast, [Maliketh](../lore-concepts/destined-death.md). With Destined Death sealed, true death was removed from the natural order. The dead were returned to the Erdtree, and the cycle of life was governed entirely by the Golden Order. This act had profound consequences: it made the demigods functionally immortal, but it also created the conditions for the corruption of death itself -- when Destined Death was later stolen and used improperly during the Night of the Black Knives, it produced the abomination of "half-death" and the spread of Deathroot.

### Radagon's Separate Life

Radagon's history as a seemingly independent figure is deeply strange. He appeared as a champion of the Golden Order, fought against the Carian royal family, and then married Queen [Rennala](rennala.md) after being captivated by her. They had three children: [Ranni](ranni.md), [Radahn](radahn.md), and [Rykard](rykard.md). Yet Radagon eventually left Rennala to return to the capital and become Marika's consort -- a union with himself.

He left Rennala the amber egg containing the Great Rune of the Unborn (Great Rune of the Unborn: "This Great Rune is the amber egg that was given to Rennala by her beloved Radagon, upon their union"). This parting gift suggests either genuine affection or calculated consolation. Rennala's subsequent madness -- endlessly attempting to use the egg to rebirth -- indicates the departure shattered her.

### The Exile of Godfrey

Before Radagon's return, Marika stripped [Godfrey](godfrey.md) and his warriors of grace, exiling them from the Lands Between. Melina conveys Marika's words at the Third Church of Marika: "My Lord, and thy warriors. I divest each of thee of thy grace. With thine eyes dimmed, ye will be driven from the Lands Between." This was not punishment -- it was a plan. Marika continues: "Then, after thy death, I will give back what I once claimed. Return to the Lands Between, and have thy deathbed companion." She foresaw the Tarnished's return.

### The Shattering

At the Church of Irith, Melina reads Marika's words: "O Radagon, leal hound of the Golden Order. Thou'rt yet to become me, thou'rt yet to become a god. Let us both be shattered, mine other self." The Shattering was deliberate. Marika broke the Elden Ring -- and herself -- while Radagon tried to mend it. In the final boss encounter, the player sees Marika's shattered body hanging from the cruciform within the Erdtree, and Radagon emerges from within her to fight.

At the Queen's Bedchamber, Marika's words confirm the emotional reality: "Then, a Loss. One which should not have been possible. An impossible Loss." This "impossible Loss" is widely understood to be the death of Godwyn the Golden during the Night of the Black Knives -- the first true death of a demigod since Destined Death was sealed.

### The Land of Shadow and the DLC

The Shadow of the Erdtree DLC reveals that Marika's history is even darker than the base game suggests. Before establishing the Golden Order in its final form, Marika sent her son [Messmer](../dlc/messmer.md) to the Land of Shadow to purge the Hornsent -- a civilization that practiced crucible-adjacent rituals and may have posed a theological threat to the Erdtree's supremacy. The genocide was systematic and thorough, and the entire Land of Shadow was subsequently hidden from history and memory. This revelation reframes Marika not merely as a god who lost faith in her own order, but as someone who built that order on a foundation of atrocity.

## Connections & Analysis

### The Dual Nature: Not Schizophrenia but Strategy

The Marika/Radagon split is often read as a simple duality -- order versus chaos, maker versus breaker. But the evidence suggests something more calculated. Marika orchestrated at least three of the game's most pivotal events:

1. **The exile of Godfrey** -- removing her first consort once his wars were done, then planning the Tarnished's return
2. **The Night of the Black Knives** -- [Ranni](ranni.md) stole a fragment of Destined Death, but the Black Knife Assassins are Numen women, Marika's own people. Marika's complicity is strongly implied.
3. **The Shattering itself** -- explicitly stated in her own words

This suggests Marika was systematically dismantling the very order she created. The question is why. The Goldmask ending provides one answer: the Mending Rune of Perfect Order states that "the current imperfection of the Golden Order is the result of the fickleness of the gods." Marika may have come to see her own system as fundamentally flawed -- or the Greater Will's control as intolerable.

### Parallel with Miquella

[Miquella](miquella.md)'s arc in the DLC mirrors Marika's ascent. Both began with seemingly benevolent goals, both committed to increasingly extreme acts to achieve them, and both sacrificed their humanity in the process. Marika committed genocide in the Land of Shadow and sealed away death itself; Miquella shed his love, his charm, and his compassion to become a god. The DLC explicitly draws this parallel -- Miquella's journey to godhood follows the same path Marika walked, raising the question of whether all divine ascent in this world requires the abandonment of human morality.

### The Night of the Black Knives: Marika's Role

[Ranni](ranni.md) claims credit for orchestrating the Night of the Black Knives, but the logistics implicate Marika. The Black Knife Assassins are Numen -- Marika's kin. They used a stolen fragment of Destined Death, which was guarded by Maliketh, Marika's own shadow-bound beast. Either Maliketh was impossibly negligent, or Marika allowed the theft. The Night killed Godwyn's soul (while Ranni's body died) -- and Godwyn was Marika's own son by Godfrey. This raises the unsettling possibility that Marika sacrificed her firstborn as the catalyst for shattering the order she herself built.

### Radagon's Resistance

The final boss fight shows Radagon attempting to repair the Elden Ring even as Marika's body hangs broken. This suggests Radagon genuinely served the Golden Order -- making him the Greater Will's loyalist trapped inside the same body as its greatest rebel. Whether Radagon had independent will or was simply the Greater Will's mechanism of control within Marika is unresolved.

### Connection to Melina

[Melina](melina.md) states her mother gave her purpose "inside the Erdtree." She can read Marika's words at the churches, suggesting a unique spiritual link. If Melina is Marika's daughter, she may represent Marika's final contingency: a guide for the Tarnished who would return, a kindling maiden who could burn the Erdtree's thorns, and -- as the Frenzied Flame ending reveals -- a bearer of Destined Death capable of slaying a god.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "Why did Marika shatter the Elden Ring?"
    **Interpretation A:** Grief over Godwyn's death drove her to an act of despair. The "impossible Loss" she describes supports this reading. She shattered the Ring and herself because the system that was supposed to prevent death had failed.
    **Interpretation B:** The Shattering was the culmination of a long-planned rebellion against the Greater Will. The exile of Godfrey, the complicity in the Night of the Black Knives, and the Shattering itself form a coherent sequence of deliberate destruction. Godwyn's death was the trigger, but the intent preceded it.
    **Interpretation C:** Both. Marika had been planning to dismantle the order but needed a genuine emotional catalyst to commit to the act. Godwyn's death provided it.
    **Gap:** The precise timeline of Marika's disillusionment is unclear. When did she stop believing in the Golden Order?

!!! question "Are Marika and Radagon truly one being, or two?"
    **Interpretation A:** They are two souls in one body, capable of independent action and disagreement (the Shattering vs. the mending).
    **Interpretation B:** Radagon is an aspect of Marika created or imposed by the Greater Will -- a control mechanism to keep her loyal.
    **Interpretation C:** They were originally separate and were merged by the Greater Will, explaining Radagon's independent history with Rennala.
    **Gap:** No source definitively explains how or when the merger occurred, or whether Radagon existed independently before becoming Marika's consort.

!!! question "Did Marika know about Miquella's true plans?"
    **Interpretation A:** Marika, imprisoned within the Erdtree, had no knowledge of the DLC events.
    **Interpretation B:** Marika, who planned multiple steps ahead throughout her reign, may have anticipated that one of her Empyrean children would attempt what she herself had done -- ascend to godhood through sacrifice and ruthlessness.
    **Gap:** The DLC does not directly address Marika's awareness of Miquella's journey.
