# Crumbling Farum Azula

> A floating ruin that exists outside of time, perpetually crumbling in an eternal storm -- the seat of a pre-Erdtree dragon civilization, now serving as the prison for Destined Death and the final waypoint before the Erdtree.

## Overview

Crumbling Farum Azula is unlike any other location in the Lands Between. It does not exist on the map in any conventional sense. The player is transported here by the flames of the Forge of the Giants, arriving in a place that exists simultaneously in the past, present, and future -- a ruin caught in a temporal loop of perpetual collapse. Stone platforms drift in a void of storms. Architecture fractures and reassembles. The sky is an endless tempest.

This was once the seat of the dragon civilization that predated the Erdtree age. Dragonlord Placidusax ruled here as Elden Lord before [Marika's](../demigods/marika-radagon.md) ascension, served by Beastmen who represented a pre-human civilization with their own culture, religion, and governance. When Placidusax's god fled (an unnamed Outer God who may or may not have been the Greater Will), the Dragonlord entered a state of eternal waiting, and Farum Azula began its endless crumbling.

In the present, the ruin serves a second purpose: it is where [Maliketh](../demigods/marika-radagon.md), Marika's shadow-bound beast, guards the sealed Rune of Destined Death. The player must defeat Maliketh to reclaim Destined Death and proceed to the Ashen Capital for the endgame.

## Lore Details

### Dragonlord Placidusax

Placidusax is found in a hidden arena accessible by lying in a specific spot and being transported backward in time. He is a two-headed dragon (originally five-headed, based on his model showing three severed neck stumps) of extraordinary size and power. His remembrance states: "The Dragonlord whose seat lies at the heart of the storm beyond time is said to have been Elden Lord in the age before the Erdtree. Once his god was fled, the lord continued to await its return."

Placidusax has been waiting since before the Erdtree existed. His god -- whatever entity preceded the Greater Will's dominion -- departed and never returned. The Dragonlord's response was not rebellion, not grief, not madness, but patience. He entered a state of temporal stasis, and Farum Azula's time-distortion may be a consequence of or a condition for his waiting. The arena where he fights is visibly older than the rest of Farum Azula, as if the player travels further back in time to reach him.

The loss of three of his five heads suggests tremendous battles in the past, possibly during the transition from the dragon age to the Erdtree age. That he still fights with devastating power despite his diminished state speaks to the staggering scale of the original dragon civilization.

### The Beastmen of Farum Azula

Throughout the ruin, the player encounters Beastmen -- muscular, bestial humanoids who wield primitive weapons and worship at beast-themed altars. These are the remnants of a pre-Erdtree civilization that served the dragon lords. Their presence establishes that before humans, before the Golden Order, before the Erdtree, there was an organized society with its own hierarchies and faith.

The Beastmen's religious practices appear centered on the dragons and on a concept of sacred beasts. Their altars, their weapons, and their combat styles all suggest a culture oriented around physical strength and animal reverence. This stands in stark contrast to the Golden Order's emphasis on golden grace and spiritual hierarchies. The Beastmen are evidence that the Greater Will's order was not the first -- and may not be the last.

Gurranq, the Beast Clergyman found at the Bestial Sanctum in [Caelid's](caelid.md) Dragonbarrow, is connected to this civilization and is revealed to be Maliketh himself in another guise. His hunger for deathroot and his erratic behavior reflect the corruption of Destined Death's influence.

### Maliketh and the Black Blade

Maliketh, the Black Blade, is Marika's shadow -- a beast given to her by the Two Fingers, similar to how Blaidd was given to Ranni. He alone was entrusted with the Rune of Destined Death, sealed within his blade after a fragment was stolen during the Night of the Black Knives. His remembrance describes him as "the gravest of the empyrean assassins, and yet upon being betrayed, sealed away the blade so that it might never be stolen again."

The betrayal referenced is the Night of the Black Knives, when [Ranni](../demigods/ranni.md) orchestrated the theft of a Destined Death fragment to kill Godwyn's soul and her own body. Maliketh's response -- sealing himself and the blade in Farum Azula, a place outside of time -- represents the most extreme possible containment measure. He guards Destined Death in a temporal prison, ensuring it can never be stolen again.

The boss fight against Maliketh is two-phased: first the Beast Clergyman (Gurranq's true form), then Maliketh with the Black Blade unsheathed. His attacks inflict a percentage-based HP reduction that cannot be healed for a duration, mechanically representing what Destined Death does: it causes true, irreversible death. Defeating him releases Destined Death back into the world, which is why the Erdtree burns fully and Leyndell becomes the Ashen Capital.

### The Architecture of Temporal Collapse

Farum Azula's architecture is simultaneously ancient and impossible. Columns hang in mid-air. Stairways lead into voids. Buildings are split in half, their interiors exposed to the storm. The visual language suggests a place frozen in the act of being destroyed -- not ruined as in decayed over time, but captured in the moment of its breaking, stretched across eternity.

The storm itself may be the Dragonlord's power made manifest, or it may be the residue of whatever catastrophe ended the dragon age. Tornadoes carry debris in loops. Lightning strikes with patterns that suggest intelligence or intent. The weather is not ambient; it is the environment.

## Connections & Analysis

Farum Azula is the game's most explicit demonstration that the Golden Order is not the original order. Placidusax was Elden Lord before Marika. The Beastmen had a civilization before humans. The dragons had a god before the Greater Will. Everything the Golden Order presents as eternal and foundational -- the Erdtree, the Elden Ring, the grace system -- is actually the second (or third, or fourth) iteration of cosmic governance in the Lands Between. Farum Azula preserves the evidence of what came before, and the Golden Order's decision to exile Destined Death here (in Maliketh's keeping) is telling: they hid their most dangerous weapon in the ruins of the previous age, as if the past were the safest vault.

The connection between Farum Azula and the [underground cities](siofra-ainsel-river.md) is thematic rather than spatial. Both represent pre-Erdtree civilizations that were destroyed or suppressed. The Nox were cast underground for heresy. The dragons were conquered and their seat left to crumble in a time-locked storm. The pattern is consistent: the Greater Will's order eliminates competitors and erases their history, but never quite completely.

Placidusax's waiting god creates one of the game's most haunting unresolved threads. Who was this god? Was it the Greater Will itself, which later chose Marika? A different Outer God entirely? The answer changes the entire cosmological history of the Lands Between. If the Greater Will abandoned Placidusax for Marika, then the current Order is built on an act of divine betrayal that mirrors Marika's own later betrayals.

The player's journey through Farum Azula -- arriving from the Forge's flames, fighting through a time-displaced ruin, defeating the guardian of Death itself -- is a passage through cosmic history compressed into a single dungeon. By the time they reach Maliketh, they have walked through the wreckage of a civilization older than the Erdtree and taken back the one force the Golden Order tried hardest to suppress.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "Who was Placidusax's god, and why did it flee?"
    **Interpretation A:** The Greater Will itself was Placidusax's god. It abandoned the dragon civilization for reasons unknown (perhaps the dragons were insufficient vessels) and later chose Marika, sending the Elden Beast to establish the Erdtree. This would make the Greater Will a serial patron, discarding servants when they no longer serve.
    **Interpretation B:** Placidusax's god was a different Outer God entirely, one that has no other presence in the current game. Its departure was unrelated to the Greater Will's arrival and may have been caused by the same cosmic forces that send the Outer Gods wandering between worlds.
    **Gap:** No source names Placidusax's god or explains its departure. The phrase "once his god was fled" is the entirety of what is known.

!!! question "Why is Farum Azula stuck in time?"
    **Interpretation A:** Placidusax's temporal stasis -- his act of waiting outside of time -- caused the entire ruin to become time-locked. The Dragonlord's power is so immense that his state of being literally affects the space around him.
    **Interpretation B:** Farum Azula was deliberately removed from time by some external force (the Greater Will, Marika, or another entity) to serve as a sealed vault for Destined Death. The temporal displacement is a security measure, not a side effect.
    **Gap:** No mechanism for the time distortion is explained. The visual evidence (perpetual crumbling, storm loops) supports either theory equally.
