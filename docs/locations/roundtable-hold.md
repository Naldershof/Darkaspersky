# Roundtable Hold

> The Tarnished's staging ground, existing outside normal space -- a place between places, built by Marika to gather her chosen warriors and guide them toward a single purpose: slaying a god. Possibly including herself.

## Overview

The Roundtable Hold is the player's hub throughout Elden Ring. It is reached not by walking but by grace -- Melina transports the player there after resting at certain Sites of Grace. The Hold exists in an extradimensional space, disconnected from the map of the Lands Between. NPCs gather here between their own journeys, and the Two Fingers sit in an upper chamber, communicating divine will through their interpreter, Enia.

The Hold's physical layout mirrors a real location: the fortified manor in [Leyndell, Royal Capital](leyndell.md), accessible late in the game. This physical counterpart is ruined, its round table broken, its halls haunted by invaders. The parallel confirms that the Roundtable Hold the player uses is not the original building but a copy -- a projection maintained by grace, sustained for as long as the Tarnished are needed.

## Lore Details

### A Gathering by Design

[Marika](../demigods/marika-radagon.md) exiled the Tarnished, stripping them of grace and sending them beyond the Lands Between. When she later shattered the Elden Ring, grace was restored to select Tarnished, calling them back. The Roundtable Hold is where they converge -- a waystation between exile and purpose. Every NPC present has been summoned by grace and guided here: [Fia](../npcs/fia.md) the Deathbed Companion, [Rogier](../npcs/rogier.md) the sorcerer, D the Hunter of the Dead, Diallos, Gideon Ofnir, and others.

The Two Fingers preside from their chamber, providing guidance through Enia. But the Fingers' guidance grows increasingly inadequate as the game progresses. They fall silent, unable to communicate with the Greater Will, and Enia admits their age and confusion. The divine hotline is broken. The Tarnished must proceed without celestial instruction.

### Hewg the Blacksmith

Master Hewg is chained to the Hold. He cannot leave, bound by a duty he did not choose: to forge a weapon capable of slaying a god. His dialogue reveals that [Marika](../demigods/marika-radagon.md) herself visited him and asked him to craft this weapon. The implication is staggering -- the god-queen commissioned a god-killing blade, which means she anticipated (or planned) the moment when a Tarnished would need to destroy a god. The most likely target was herself, or the Elden Beast, or both.

Hewg's chains are literal and metaphorical. He is a prisoner of purpose, compelled to serve a mission he did not design. When the Hold begins to burn near the endgame, Hewg refuses to leave. His chains are part of him now. He forgets the player's name, forgets his task, but keeps smithing -- muscle memory outlasting identity.

### The Basement and the Mad Tarnished

Beneath the Hold lies a basement where a mad Tarnished invades the player. This figure has lost themselves to the violence of the journey -- a Tarnished who came to the Hold, descended into its depths, and never returned to reason. The basement also connects thematically to the Subterranean Shunning-Grounds beneath [Leyndell](leyndell.md), reinforcing the pattern of buried madness beneath structures of order.

## Connections & Analysis

The Roundtable Hold is Marika's trap -- and the Tarnished are the teeth. She exiled them, recalled them, gave them grace, gathered them in a purpose-built staging ground, chained a blacksmith there to arm them, and pointed them at the Erdtree. Every element of the Hold serves a single function: producing a warrior capable of reaching the Elden Ring and remaking it. The Two Fingers believe they are directing this process, but the Fingers' growing silence suggests they have lost control of a plan that was never theirs.

Marika's commissioning of Hewg is the Hold's most damning detail. A god who asks a smith to forge a god-slaying weapon is a god who expects -- or wants -- to be slain. This connects directly to the theory that Marika shattered the Elden Ring not out of grief alone but as the first move in a plan to destroy the Golden Order from within. The Roundtable Hold is the second move: assembling the army that will finish what she started.

The Hold's extradimensional nature raises questions about who maintains it. If grace sustains it, and grace flows from the Erdtree, then the Greater Will is unknowingly housing the agents of its own destruction. If Marika sustains it independently, then she retains power even in her shattered state -- power the Greater Will cannot see or control.

The physical Roundtable in Leyndell -- ruined, its table split, its halls dead -- is what the Hold will become when the Tarnished's mission ends. The functional Hold is a ghost of this ruin, projected backward in time or sideways in space, existing only as long as it is needed. When the Elden Ring is mended, the Hold's purpose expires. The building remains; the magic does not.

Gideon Ofnir, the All-Knowing, uses the Hold as his intelligence center, dispatching Tarnished to gather information on the demigods. But Gideon's ultimate conclusion -- that Marika does not want the Elden Ring mended, that the Tarnished are meant to struggle and fail -- positions him as the one resident who understood the Hold's true purpose and rejected it. His betrayal at the end is the act of a man who saw behind the curtain.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "Does the Hold exist in time or outside it?"
    The Hold's physical counterpart in Leyndell is already ruined when the player visits it. If the Hold is a projection of that location, is it projecting the past (the building before its ruin) or an idealized version that never existed? The extradimensional nature of the Hold is never mechanically explained.

!!! question "Did Marika intend the Tarnished to mend the Ring or destroy it?"
    Hewg was told to forge a god-slaying weapon. The Tarnished are guided to the Elden Ring. But "mending" and "slaying" are different verbs. Did Marika want restoration, revolution, or annihilation? The Hold's design supports all three interpretations.

!!! question "What happens to the Hold after the ending?"
    If grace ceases or changes, does the Hold vanish? Do the NPCs who remain there -- Hewg, the Twin Maiden Husks, Roderika -- simply disappear when the extradimensional space collapses?
