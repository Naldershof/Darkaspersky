# Roundtable Hold

> The Tarnished's gathering place -- a pocket dimension mirroring a physical location in Leyndell, purpose-built by Marika as a staging ground for the warriors she exiled and planned to summon back, where every resident is both ally and potential threat.

## Overview

The Roundtable Hold is the player's hub area in Elden Ring, a place outside the normal geography of the Lands Between. [Melina](../demigods/melina.md) transports the player here after sufficient progress, introducing it as "a gathering place for Tarnished, guided by grace." The Hold exists as a separate dimension -- it cannot be reached by walking, only by teleportation from Sites of Grace. Yet a physical counterpart exists within [Leyndell, Royal Capital](leyndell.md), confirming that the extradimensional space mirrors (or was copied from) a real location.

The Hold is presided over by the Two Fingers and their interpreter, Finger Reader Enia. Gideon Ofnir, the All-Knowing, serves as de facto leader of the resident Tarnished. A smith, a spirit tuner, a deathbed companion, and various warriors occupy the rooms and corridors. The atmosphere is one of tense cooperation: every resident has their own agenda, and the Hold's compact space forces uneasy coexistence between figures whose goals are fundamentally incompatible.

## Lore Details

### The Hold's Dimensional Nature

The Roundtable Hold exists in a pocket dimension accessible only through grace-based teleportation. The player cannot leave by walking out a door; they can only fast-travel back to Sites of Grace. This spatial isolation serves multiple purposes: it protects the Tarnished from external threats while confining them in close quarters, it ensures that only those guided by grace can enter, and it places the Tarnished directly under the Two Fingers' observation.

The physical counterpart in Leyndell -- a ruined, deserted version of the same building -- can be explored in the capital. The layout is identical but the atmosphere is utterly different: empty, burnt, haunted by a single mad warrior (the Roundtable Knight). This duality suggests that [Marika](../demigods/marika-radagon.md) (or the Greater Will through her) created the pocket dimension as a copy of a real space, perhaps one that had significance in an earlier age when the original Tarnished (Godfrey and his warriors) served the Order.

### Hewg the Blacksmith

Hewg is chained to his smithing station, unable to leave the Hold. He is a Misbegotten -- a race treated as subhuman by the Golden Order -- and he smiths weapons for the Tarnished with single-minded focus. When asked about his chains, he reveals that he has been tasked (or cursed) to forge a weapon capable of slaying a god. He does not remember who gave him this purpose but suspects it was "the one who put me in these chains."

The implication is that Marika herself chained Hewg to the Hold and tasked him with forging a god-slaying weapon. This is one of the strongest pieces of evidence that Marika planned the Tarnished's return well in advance of the Shattering -- she not only exiled the Tarnished and arranged for their recall, but she also prepared a weapon for them to use against the gods (potentially herself/Radagon or the Elden Beast). Hewg's chains are not punishment; they are dedication enforced through bondage.

As the game progresses and the Erdtree burns, Hewg begins to forget his purpose. His memory degrades, his speech becomes confused, and he clings to the smithing that is all he knows. His decline mirrors the broader collapse of the systems Marika built.

### Roderika the Spirit Tuner

Roderika arrives at the Hold after fleeing [Stormveil Castle](limgrave.md), traumatized by the grafting she witnessed. With the player's encouragement, she discovers an affinity for spirit tuning -- the ability to strengthen spirit ashes (the summoned spirits that fight alongside the player). She takes up a station opposite Hewg, and the two develop a quiet companionship.

Spirit tuning involves communing with the dead -- specifically with the spirits bound to ashes. Roderika's ability to do this may be connected to her experience at Stormveil, where she was surrounded by the violently dead. Her transformation from a traumatized victim into a purposeful artisan is one of the Hold's few hopeful arcs, and her partnership with Hewg (both outcasts serving the Tarnished) creates a small island of warmth in a space defined by manipulation and hidden agendas.

### Fia, Deathbed Companion

[Fia](../npcs/fia.md) occupies a bedchamber in the Hold, offering embraces that grant a consumable item (Baldachin's Blessing) while secretly draining the player's vitality. She is a Deathbed Companion -- one who lies with the dying to absorb their warmth -- and she is collecting vitality from the Hold's champions to birth the Mending Rune of the Death-Prince.

Fia's presence in the Hold is ideologically subversive. She opposes the Golden Order's prohibition on death, sympathizes with Those Who Live in Death, and ultimately murders D (a hunter who persecutes the undead) using the vitality gathered from the player's embraces. Her questline takes her from the Hold to the Deeproot Depths, where she lies with Godwyn's soulless body to complete her purpose.

That the Hold houses both Fia (who seeks to restore death) and D (who hunts the undead on behalf of the Order) in adjacent rooms illustrates the institution's fundamental tension: it gathers Tarnished of wildly different ideologies under one roof and provides no mechanism for resolving their conflicts.

### The Basement and the Mad Tarnished

Below the Hold's main floor, a basement area contains the Dung Eater's cell (empty until his questline advances) and a corridor haunted by an invading NPC -- a Tarnished driven mad, presumably by the same process that drives all Tarnished eventually. The basement's existence beneath a space of ostensible cooperation suggests that madness and imprisonment are never far from the surface, even in the Order's designated safe house.

### Gideon Ofnir, the All-Knowing

Sir Gideon Ofnir serves as the Hold's information broker and effective leader, cataloging knowledge about every demigod and their domains. He is helpful and erudite, providing briefings on each shardbearer the player seeks. But his helpfulness masks a darker pragmatism: he ordered the massacre at the [Albinauric Village](liurnia.md) and ultimately opposes the player in [Leyndell](leyndell.md), having concluded that the Erdtree's will is for no one to become Elden Lord.

Gideon's arc -- from helpful guide to final obstacle -- reflects the Hold's own nature as a tool that serves its users only until their purpose conflicts with the institution's hidden agenda.

## Connections & Analysis

The Roundtable Hold is Marika's most intricate creation: a purpose-built staging ground for the warriors she exiled and planned to recall. Hewg's chains (forging a god-slaying weapon), Melina's guidance (leading the Tarnished to the Erdtree), and the Two Fingers' observation (monitoring progress) all point to a system designed with extraordinary foresight. Marika stripped grace from [Godfrey](../demigods/godfrey.md) and his warriors, sent them to "wage war in a land afar," and then built the infrastructure for their return: the Hold, the smith, the maiden, the guidance system. The Shattering was not an accident but the trigger for a plan that required the Tarnished to return and finish what Marika started.

The Hold's mirrored existence -- a pocket dimension copied from a physical location in Leyndell -- connects it spatially to the capital and thematically to the Golden Order's habit of creating shadow versions of itself. Just as [Morgott](../demigods/morgott.md) operates as Margit (a shadow of the king), and the Omen exist as shadow citizens beneath Leyndell, the Hold is a shadow court: a parallel institution that serves the Order's goals while remaining hidden from its official structures.

The collection of NPCs in the Hold represents every major faction and ideology in the game compressed into a single building. Fia opposes the Golden Order's death prohibition. D enforces it. [Rogier](../npcs/rogier.md) investigates the Night of the Black Knives. [Nepheli](../npcs/nepheli-loux.md) seeks justice for the oppressed. [The Dung Eater](../npcs/dungeater.md) seeks to curse all living things. Gideon seeks knowledge above all else. The Hold does not resolve these tensions -- it merely contains them, like the [Shunning-Grounds](leyndell.md) beneath Leyndell contain the Omen.

The contrast between Hewg and the Elden Beast encapsulates Marika's plan: she chained a smith to forge the weapon that would kill the god she was imprisoned within. The Hold is a forge for killing gods, disguised as a rest stop.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "Who created the Roundtable Hold's pocket dimension?"
    **Interpretation A:** Marika created it as part of her long-term plan to recall the Tarnished. The physical counterpart in Leyndell was the original, and the pocket dimension is a magical copy maintained by the Erdtree's power.
    **Interpretation B:** The Greater Will (through the Two Fingers) created the Hold as a mechanism for monitoring and directing the Tarnished. Marika's plan and the Greater Will's plan overlap but are not identical -- the Hold serves both.
    **Gap:** No source explicitly states who created the pocket dimension or when. The physical counterpart in Leyndell is uninhabited and shows signs of fire damage, suggesting it was used and abandoned before the dimensional copy was made.

!!! question "What happens to the Hold after the Erdtree burns?"
    **Interpretation A:** The Hold persists because the pocket dimension is sustained by a power independent of the Erdtree. Hewg's degrading memory suggests it is weakening but not collapsing.
    **Interpretation B:** The Hold is slowly unraveling as the Erdtree's power fails. Hewg forgets his purpose. The Two Fingers become unresponsive. The institution that was built to support the Tarnished's mission is dying alongside the Order it was built to serve (and undermine).
    **Gap:** The Hold is mechanically accessible through the endgame, but narratively its status is ambiguous. The NPCs who remain show signs of decline, but the space itself does not visibly change.
