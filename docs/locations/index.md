# Locations of the Lands Between

> The Lands Between is a continent shaped by divine power and ruined by divine war, where every crumbling wall and blighted field tells the story of the Golden Order's rise and collapse.

## Overview

The geography of the Lands Between is not merely a backdrop -- it is the primary text of Elden Ring's narrative. FromSoftware's environmental storytelling encodes centuries of history into architecture, decay, enemy placement, and spatial relationships. Each region is the domain of a demigod (or the remnant of a pre-Erdtree civilization), and the physical state of the land directly reflects the nature and fate of its ruler.

The world is structured vertically and horizontally. The Erdtree towers over everything from its seat in Leyndell at the center. Regions radiate outward: Limgrave to the southeast, Liurnia to the northwest, Caelid to the east, the Altus Plateau and Mt. Gelmir to the west. The Mountaintops of the Giants rise in the far north. Below the surface, the underground rivers and Eternal Cities form a parallel world with its own history. And outside of time entirely, Crumbling Farum Azula floats in the storm.

## Thematic Geography

The Lands Between can be read as a series of concentric rings around the Erdtree:

- **Inner ring (Leyndell):** The seat of power, where the Golden Order's authority is strongest -- and where its deepest secrets (the Shunning-Grounds, the Omen) are buried.
- **Middle ring (Liurnia, Altus, Gelmir):** Regions that once served the Order but have fractured into independence or rebellion.
- **Outer ring (Limgrave, Caelid, Mountaintops):** Frontier territories where the Order's grip has weakened or was never fully established.
- **Below (Siofra, Ainsel, Deeproot):** The civilizations the Order suppressed, literally pushed underground.
- **Beyond (Farum Azula, Mohgwyn):** Places that exist outside the Order's cosmology entirely -- one in timeless storm, the other in blood-soaked darkness.

Each layer tells a different chapter of the same story: power claimed, power contested, power decayed.
