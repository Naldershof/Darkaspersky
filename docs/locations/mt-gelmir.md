# Mt. Gelmir & Volcano Manor

> The volcanic domain of Rykard, Lord of Blasphemy -- a region where rebellion against the Golden Order has been literalized as a serpent devouring the gods, and where the line between revolutionary and monster has been consumed along with everything else.

## Overview

Mt. Gelmir occupies the western edge of the Altus Plateau, a volcanic region of lava flows, toxic fumes, and precarious cliff paths. At its heart sits Volcano Manor, the seat of [Rykard, Lord of Blasphemy](../demigods/rykard.md), who fed himself to the God-Devouring Serpent in pursuit of power sufficient to destroy the Golden Order and all its gods. The manor serves as a headquarters for the recusants -- Tarnished and others who have rejected the Erdtree's grace and fight against the established order.

Rykard's transformation from Carian prince and Praetor (a title implying judicial or governmental authority) into a serpent-fused abomination mirrors the trajectory of many revolutionary movements in the game's lore: beginning with legitimate grievance and ending in something monstrous. The volcano itself is the metaphor made physical -- a place of heat, consumption, and transformation from which nothing emerges unchanged.

## Lore Details

### Volcano Manor and the Recusants

Volcano Manor is a grand estate built improbably on the slopes of an active volcano. Its interior is lavishly furnished, with drawing rooms, libraries, and bedchambers that seem almost mundane compared to the eldritch horrors elsewhere in the Lands Between. Lady Tanith presides over the manor's operations, recruiting Tarnished to serve as assassins targeting champions of the Golden Order.

The recusants are anti-Erdtree fighters who have been organized into a coherent resistance movement. They are not the desperate, individual rebels found elsewhere -- they operate with structure, targets, and ideology. The player can join them and receive assassination contracts: invade the worlds of specific Tarnished and kill them. The targets are invariably figures loyal to or protected by the Golden Order.

This organized rebellion distinguishes Mt. Gelmir from other locations of defiance. Where [Ranni](../demigods/ranni.md) operates through stealth and cosmic manipulation, and the Frenzied Flame adherents seek total annihilation, Rykard's faction fights a material war. They have a manor, a chain of command, and a list of targets. The revolution has been institutionalized.

### The God-Devouring Serpent and Rykard's Transformation

At the volcano's deepest point, Rykard has fused with the God-Devouring Serpent, becoming a towering abomination of scales, magma, and consumed flesh. The serpent's hunger is described as insatiable -- "it was said to devour even the very gods themselves." Rykard offered himself to the serpent willingly, believing that by becoming one with an entity that could devour gods, he would gain the power to destroy the Golden Order entirely.

The boss encounter is grotesque and spectacular. Rykard's human face emerges from the serpent's body mid-fight, screaming blasphemous declarations. The arena is a lake of magma. The Serpent-Hunter greatspear, found at the entrance, is the only practical weapon for the fight -- a weapon specifically designed to kill this creature, implying that someone anticipated the need.

Rykard's transformation raises the question of whether any of the original Praetor remains. His dialogue suggests consciousness and intent persist, but the serpent's hunger has clearly become the dominant drive. He recruits followers not merely to fight the Order but to be consumed -- "join the family," in Tanith's euphemism, ultimately means feeding oneself to the serpent.

### Prison Town

Below the manor lies the Prison Town, a settlement of captives and unfortunates pressed into service or simply warehoused. The town is populated by man-serpent hybrids -- beings partway through a serpentine transformation -- alongside human prisoners, iron maidens (mechanical torture devices that serve as both enemies and transportation), and abducted scholars.

The man-serpent transformation is never fully explained but appears to be the result of proximity to Rykard's influence or deliberate experimentation. Some man-serpents retain weapons and fighting technique, suggesting they were warriors before their change. Others are more degraded, closer to beasts. The transformation gradient visible across the Prison Town's population tells a story of progressive corruption -- the closer one gets to Rykard, the less human the inhabitants become.

### The Gelmir Hero's Grave and Historical Layers

Mt. Gelmir contains buried hero's graves and catacombs that predate Rykard's occupation, indicating the volcano was significant before the Shattering. Lava has consumed much of the lower architecture, but the remaining structures suggest a history of volcanic activity being either harnessed or endured by earlier civilizations. The Full-Grown Fallingstar Beast on the mountain's exterior connects to cosmic elements -- these creatures are said to have fallen from the stars, linking Mt. Gelmir to the same celestial forces that Radahn held at bay.

### Patches and the Abyssal Wood

The Patches encounter on Mt. Gelmir, where the recurring FromSoftware trickster attempts to push the player off a cliff, is tonally jarring -- a moment of dark comedy in a region of serious horror. But Patches' presence here, running a scheme near the volcano, reinforces that even in the most dangerous places, opportunists thrive. His subsequent appearances connect to the broader web of NPC questlines.

## Connections & Analysis

Rykard's rebellion is the most physically transformative response to the Golden Order's failure. Where [Morgott](../demigods/morgott.md) devoted himself to the Order despite its cruelty, and Ranni sought to replace it with something new, Rykard chose to become the thing that destroys. The volcano as his domain is perfect environmental storytelling: volcanoes consume and reshape the land, turning solid ground into flowing magma and rebuilding topography through destruction. Rykard does the same -- he consumes the strong to become stronger, endlessly, with no endpoint except the eventual devouring of everything.

The spatial relationship between Volcano Manor and [Leyndell](leyndell.md) is significant. They occupy opposite sides of the Altus Plateau: the Golden Order's capital to the east, its most organized opposition to the west. The player can see one from the other. This geographic opposition mirrors the ideological one. Where Leyndell builds upward toward the Erdtree, Volcano Manor burrows downward into magma.

The recusant system connects Mt. Gelmir to [Roundtable Hold](roundtable-hold.md) as a dark mirror. The Hold sends Tarnished outward to collect Great Runes and restore the Elden Ring. Volcano Manor sends Tarnished outward to assassinate those same champions. Both institutions recruit the same pool of warriors; the difference is ideology, not method.

Rykard's Great Rune -- which restores HP upon defeating enemies -- mechanically encodes the serpent's devouring nature. Every kill feeds the wielder. This is the revolution reduced to its essential logic: survival through consumption.

The Serpent-Hunter greatspear found at Rykard's arena entrance raises the question of who placed it there and why. It may have been left by a prior challenger, by a faction within the manor that turned against Rykard, or by Lady Tanith herself as an insurance policy. Its presence implies that even within Rykard's own court, the possibility of his destruction was anticipated.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "What was the God-Devouring Serpent before Rykard merged with it?"
    **Interpretation A:** An ancient entity predating the Erdtree, possibly connected to the primordial crucible or to a serpentine Outer God. Its ability to devour gods suggests a power source independent of the Golden Order.
    **Interpretation B:** A creature of the volcano itself, empowered by some local phenomenon. Rykard chose it specifically because its nature aligned with his goals.
    **Gap:** No source material describes the serpent's origin. Its title ("God-Devouring") may be aspirational rather than historical -- it may never have actually devoured a god before Rykard joined it.

!!! question "What drives the man-serpent transformations in Prison Town?"
    **Interpretation A:** Proximity to Rykard's merged form creates a field of serpentine corruption, passively transforming those who remain near the volcano too long.
    **Interpretation B:** The transformations are deliberate, part of Rykard's program to build an army of serpent-human hybrids loyal to his cause.
    **Gap:** No item description explains the mechanism. The gradient of transformation (more human at the edges, more serpentine near Rykard) could support either theory.
