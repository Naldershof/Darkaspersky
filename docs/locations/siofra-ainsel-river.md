# Siofra River, Ainsel River & the Eternal Cities

> The vast underground world beneath the Lands Between -- home to pre-Erdtree civilizations that studied the stars and created artificial life, destroyed by the Greater Will for their heresy, yet persisting in ruin as evidence that the Golden Order was not the first order and may not be the last.

## Overview

Beneath the surface of the Lands Between lies an underground realm of staggering scale. Accessed through lifts in [Limgrave](limgrave.md) (Siofra River Well) and [Liurnia](liurnia.md) (Ainsel River Well), these subterranean spaces contain rivers, forests, ruined cities, and an artificial sky of glowing stars -- a complete parallel world that existed before the Erdtree's dominion and was cast underground by the Greater Will as punishment for heresy.

The underground encompasses several distinct areas:

- **Siofra River:** A cavernous expanse with flowing water, spectral animals, and the Ancestral Followers who worship beast spirits.
- **Ainsel River:** A more structured area with architectural ruins, giant ants, and access to the Lake of Rot.
- **Nokron, Eternal City:** A ruined city accessible after [Radahn's](../demigods/radahn.md) defeat, home to the Mimic Tear and the Fingerslayer Blade.
- **Nokstella, Eternal City:** A sister city deeper underground, associated with the moon and accessible through Ainsel River.
- **Deeproot Depths:** The root system beneath the Erdtree itself, where Godwyn's corrupted body festers.
- **Lake of Rot:** A vast underground lake of Scarlet Rot, connected to the sealed Outer God of Rot.

These spaces are not merely dungeons. They are the archaeological record of civilizations that the Golden Order erased from the surface -- civilizations that may have understood the cosmos more accurately than the Order that destroyed them.

## Lore Details

### Siofra River and the Ancestral Followers

Siofra River is accessed via a lift in the Mistwood of Limgrave. The descent is long and dramatic, the lift plunging deep into the earth before opening onto a vast cavern with flowing water, bioluminescent plants, and an artificial sky studded with glowing lights that mimic stars. The scale is breathtaking: this is not a cave but an underground landscape complete with forests and ruins.

The Ancestral Followers inhabit Siofra River, a tribal people who worship animal spirits through horn-calling rituals. Their Ancestral Spirit -- a massive, ethereal stag -- is summoned by lighting braziers throughout the area. The Followers' worship predates the Erdtree's faith entirely; they revere the natural cycle of life, death, and rebirth through animal spirits rather than through golden grace. Their presence suggests that before the Erdtree imposed its system of grace, other forms of spiritual practice existed -- and that these were pushed underground along with their practitioners.

The Regal Ancestor Spirit's remembrance notes: "The spirits of the ancestors are honoured and revered among the people of the Eternal Cities." This links the Ancestral Followers to the broader Nox civilization and confirms that ancestor worship was not primitive superstition but a coherent spiritual tradition with institutional support.

### Nokron and the Fingerslayer Blade

Nokron, Eternal City, becomes accessible when a star crashes into Limgrave after Radahn's defeat. The fallen star literally tears open the earth, revealing the city buried beneath. Nokron is a ruin of elegant stonework, silver-blue architecture, and Nox warriors -- the remnants of the civilization that the Greater Will destroyed.

The city's most important artifact is the Fingerslayer Blade, hidden in a chest beyond the Mimic Tear boss fight. [Ranni](../demigods/ranni.md) sends the player to retrieve it: "The Fingerslayer Blade was forged to slay the servants of the Two Fingers. With it, I will sever the hold the Fingers have upon this world." That the Nox created a weapon specifically designed to kill the Greater Will's emissaries proves that their heresy was not passive philosophical disagreement but active resistance. They forged a counter-weapon against the cosmic order itself.

The Mimic Tear -- a silver blob that copies the player's form and equipment -- represents the Nox's mastery of artificial life creation. The Nox could create beings that perfectly replicated existing forms, a capability that the Greater Will may have considered a direct challenge to its authority over creation.

### Nokstella, Eternal City of the Moon

Nokstella, the sister Eternal City, is accessible through Ainsel River and features even more extensive ruins than Nokron. The city is permeated with lunar imagery -- moon-themed architecture, Nox Swordstresses and Night Maidens carrying moon-aspected equipment, and a general aesthetic of silver and pale blue that mirrors [Liurnia's](liurnia.md) Carian royal family.

This lunar orientation connects the underground to the surface through a shared astronomical tradition. The Carian royal family's moon worship, Rennala's title as Queen of the Full Moon, and Ranni's pursuit of the Dark Moon may all trace their roots to the Nox's original stellar studies. The Academy of Raya Lucaria's celestial motifs could be a surface-world continuation of knowledge that originated in the Eternal Cities. If so, the Golden Order's tolerance of the Academy was always conditional -- the sorcerers were permitted their star-gazing so long as they did not reach the conclusions the Nox reached.

### The Nox and Their Heresy

The Nox civilization was "cast underground" by the Greater Will for an act of heresy. The exact nature of this heresy is distributed across multiple sources but can be reconstructed:

1. **They studied the stars:** The artificial starlit sky in Siofra River and the celestial architecture of Nokron and Nokstella indicate an advanced astronomical culture that understood cosmic forces the Greater Will preferred to remain obscure.
2. **They created artificial life:** The Mimic Tear, the Silver Tears, and possibly the Albinaurics originated from Nox research. Creating life independent of the Erdtree's grace challenged the Greater Will's monopoly on creation.
3. **They forged weapons against the Fingers:** The Fingerslayer Blade proves they actively resisted the Greater Will's authority, moving from heretical thought to heretical action.
4. **They attempted to create a Lord of Night:** An artificial sovereign who owed nothing to the Greater Will's selection process, a direct challenge to the Erdtree's system of appointing Elden Lords through grace.

The punishment was comprehensive: the Greater Will sent Astel, Naturalborn of the Void -- "a malformed star born from the void" whose appearance "heralded the end of a once-great civilization." The Nox were destroyed by the very stars they studied.

### Deeproot Depths and Godwyn's Body

Below the Eternal Cities, the Deeproot Depths hold the most disturbing secret in the underground: Godwyn the Golden's body, fused with the roots of the Erdtree, growing like a tumor through the world's foundation. Godwyn was killed in soul during the Night of the Black Knives but persisted in body -- a half-death that left his corpse alive, spreading Deathroot through the Erdtree's root system and spawning Those Who Live in Death across the Lands Between.

[Fia](../npcs/fia.md) travels here to lie with Godwyn's corpse, using the accumulated vitality of the champions she embraced to birth the Mending Rune of the Death-Prince. Lichdragon Fortissax, an ancient dragon who loved Godwyn and entered his death dream to fight the corruption, is found here as a boss -- the dragon lost its battle against the death within. Fortissax's remembrance describes how the dragon "fought the Death within him for the longest time, but was at last broken by it."

The Deeproot Depths are thus the site where [Destined Death](../lore-concepts/destined-death.md) was broken (Godwyn's half-death) and where it can be repaired (Fia's Mending Rune, enabling the [Age of Duskborn](../endings/age-of-duskborn.md)).

### The Lake of Rot

At the lowest point of the underground system lies the Lake of Rot -- a vast body of liquid Scarlet Rot stretching across an enormous cavern. The player must traverse it during [Ranni's questline](../npcs/ranni-questline.md), one of the game's most hostile environmental challenges. The lake is not merely a hazard; it is a containment structure. The Grand Cloister at the far end, along with the sealed temple beneath, suggests the lake was created (or expanded) to seal away the influence of the [Outer God of Rot](../lore-concepts/outer-god-of-rot.md).

Beyond the Lake of Rot, a coffin leads to Astel, Naturalborn of the Void -- the entity that destroyed the Eternal Cities. The juxtaposition of the rot's containment and the void-born destroyer in adjacent spaces suggests that the underground's catastrophes are layered: the Nox's destruction, the Rot God's imprisonment, and the corruption of death all converge in the same subterranean complex, creating a stratigraphy of the Lands Between's suppressed history.

## Connections & Analysis

The underground is the Lands Between's subconscious -- the repressed history that the Golden Order built itself upon and then denied. Every major theme of the game exists in proto-form underground: star worship (predating the Academy's sorcery), artificial life (predating the Albinaurics' persecution on the surface), resistance to the Fingers (predating Ranni's rebellion), and the corruption of death (predating the Shattering). The surface world is a palimpsest, and the underground is the erased text showing through.

The spatial relationship between the underground and the surface creates the game's most powerful vertical metaphor. The player descends from [Limgrave's](limgrave.md) gentle hills into Siofra's ancient forests, from [Liurnia's](liurnia.md) drowned surface into Ainsel's deeper waters, and ultimately from the Erdtree's branches into its corrupted roots at the Deeproot Depths. Down is older, truer, and more dangerous. The Golden Order's authority weakens with every step of descent.

Ranni's questline threads through the underground more than any other, and this is no coincidence. She sends the player to Nokron for the Fingerslayer Blade, through Ainsel River with her miniature doll form, past the Lake of Rot, and beyond Astel to the Moonlight Altar. Her rebellion literally passes through the ruins of the previous rebellion. The Nox forged the weapon she needs; the Eternal Cities' fate is the evidence for why she fights. Ranni's [Age of Stars](../endings/age-of-stars.md) ending can be read as the fulfillment of the Nox's original vision -- a world ordered by stars and moon, free from the Greater Will -- achieved thousands of years after those who first envisioned it were destroyed.

The false sky in the Eternal Cities is the underground's most poignant architectural detail. The Nox, denied the real stars, built their own. This is not decoration -- it is an act of defiance frozen in stone. The Greater Will punished them for looking upward, so they recreated what was taken. Their entire aesthetic -- lunar, stellar, silver -- is a permanent monument to the worldview they refused to abandon.

Godwyn's corpse growing through the Deeproot Depths connects the underground to the surface in the most literal way possible. The Golden Order's failure to deal with death -- Marika's removal of Destined Death from the Elden Ring -- has produced a corruption that spreads through the Erdtree's own root system. The Order's sin leaks downward, contaminating the underground it already defiled. The civilizations the Greater Will buried are now being poisoned by the consequences of the Greater Will's own decisions.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "Were the Eternal Cities destroyed by the Greater Will, or by their own experiments?"
    **Interpretation A:** The Greater Will sent Astel, Naturalborn of the Void, as a weapon to crush the Nox for their heresy. The destruction was divine punishment, deliberate and targeted.
    **Interpretation B:** The Nox's own experiments -- particularly their study of the void and the stars -- summoned or created Astel inadvertently. The Greater Will merely sealed the aftermath underground. The "casting down" was containment, not punishment.
    **Gap:** Astel's remembrance says its appearance "heralded the end" of the civilization but does not state who sent it or whether the Nox caused it themselves. The distinction between divine punishment and cosmic accident is never clarified.

!!! question "What is the relationship between the Nox, the Albinaurics, and the creation of artificial life?"
    **Interpretation A:** The Nox created the Albinaurics as part of their artificial life program. First-generation Albinaurics are sophisticated but flawed (their legs fail); second-generation are cruder mass productions. The knowledge survived the Nox's destruction and was practiced on the surface.
    **Interpretation B:** The Albinaurics were created by a different group using fragments of Nox knowledge recovered from underground. The technology diffused rather than being directly transferred.
    **Gap:** No source explicitly states who created the Albinaurics. The connection to the Nox is inferred from the artificial life theme and the Mimic Tear's existence, but no item description directly confirms it.

!!! question "How many Eternal Cities exist, and what remains undiscovered?"
    **Interpretation A:** Nokron and Nokstella are the only two. Item descriptions reference the Nox as a single civilization with two major urban centers, and no other cities are named.
    **Interpretation B:** The underground is far more extensive than the game reveals. References to other unnamed Nox settlements and the sheer scale of the underground river system suggest entire civilizations still buried and undiscovered. The game shows only a fraction of what lies below.
    **Gap:** No definitive count exists. The underground's explorable area feels vast but is clearly bounded by game design. Lore-wise, the full extent is unknown.
