# Siofra River, Ainsel River & the Eternal Cities

> The vast underground world beneath the Lands Between -- home to pre-Erdtree civilizations that studied the stars and created artificial life, destroyed by the Greater Will for their heresy, yet persisting in ruin as evidence that the Golden Order was not the first order and may not be the last.

## Overview

Beneath the surface of the Lands Between lies an underground realm of staggering scale. Accessed through lifts in [Limgrave](limgrave.md) (Siofra River Well) and [Liurnia](liurnia.md) (Ainsel River Well), these subterranean spaces contain rivers, forests, ruined cities, and an artificial sky of glowing stars -- a complete parallel world that existed before the Erdtree's dominion and was cast underground by the Greater Will as punishment for heresy.

The underground encompasses several distinct areas:

- **Siofra River:** A cavernous expanse with flowing water, spectral animals, and the Ancestral Followers who worship beast spirits.
- **Ainsel River:** A more structured area with architectural ruins, giant ants, and access to the Lake of Rot.
- **Nokron, Eternal City:** A ruined city accessible after [Radahn's](../demigods/radahn.md) defeat, home to the Mimic Tear and the Fingerslayer Blade.
- **Nokstella, Eternal City:** A sister city deeper underground, associated with the moon and accessible through Ainsel River.
- **Deeproot Depths:** The root system beneath the Erdtree itself, where Godwyn's corrupted body festers.
- **Lake of Rot:** A vast underground lake of Scarlet Rot, connected to the sealed Outer God of Rot.

These spaces are not merely dungeons. They are the archaeological record of civilizations that the Golden Order erased from the surface -- civilizations that may have understood the cosmos more accurately than the Order that destroyed them.

## Lore Details

### Siofra River and the Ancestral Followers

The Ancestral Followers inhabit Siofra River, a tribal people who worship animal spirits through horn-calling rituals. Their Ancestral Spirit -- a massive, ethereal stag -- is summoned by lighting braziers throughout the area. The Followers' worship predates the Erdtree's faith entirely; they revere the natural cycle of life, death, and rebirth through animal spirits rather than through golden grace. Their presence suggests that before the Erdtree imposed its system of grace, other forms of spiritual practice existed -- and that these were pushed underground along with their practitioners.

### The Eternal Cities: Nokron and Nokstella

Nokron becomes accessible when a star crashes into Limgrave after Radahn's defeat -- the fallen star literally tears open the earth. The city's most important artifact is the Fingerslayer Blade, which [Ranni](../demigods/ranni.md) sends the player to retrieve. That the Nox created a weapon specifically designed to kill the Two Fingers proves that their heresy was not passive disagreement but active resistance. The Mimic Tear -- a silver blob that copies the player's form -- represents the Nox's mastery of artificial life creation, a capability the Greater Will considered a direct challenge to its authority over creation.

Nokstella, the sister city, is permeated with lunar imagery that mirrors [Liurnia's](liurnia.md) Carian royal family. This connection suggests the Carian moon worship and the Academy's celestial studies may trace their roots to the Nox's original stellar knowledge -- surface-world continuations of underground heresy, tolerated only so long as they did not reach the conclusions the Nox reached.

### The Nox and Their Heresy

The Nox were cast underground for a compound heresy: studying the stars, creating artificial life (the Mimic Tear, the Silver Tears, possibly the Albinaurics), forging the Fingerslayer Blade against the Greater Will's emissaries, and attempting to create a Lord of Night -- an artificial sovereign independent of the Erdtree's grace system. The punishment was comprehensive: the Greater Will sent Astel, Naturalborn of the Void, whose appearance "heralded the end of a once-great civilization." The Nox were destroyed by the very stars they studied.

### Deeproot Depths and Godwyn's Body

Below the Eternal Cities, the Deeproot Depths hold Godwyn the Golden's body, fused with the Erdtree's roots, growing like a tumor through the world's foundation. Godwyn's half-death during the Night of the Black Knives left his corpse alive, spreading Deathroot and spawning Those Who Live in Death. [Fia](../npcs/fia.md) travels here to lie with Godwyn's corpse and birth the Mending Rune of the Death-Prince, making the Deeproot Depths both the site where [Destined Death](../lore-concepts/destined-death.md) was broken and where it can be repaired ([Age of Duskborn](../endings/age-of-duskborn.md)).

### The Lake of Rot

The Lake of Rot, traversed during [Ranni's questline](../npcs/ranni-questline.md), is not merely a hazard but a containment structure sealing the influence of an Outer God of Rot. Beyond the lake, a coffin leads to Astel, Naturalborn of the Void -- the entity that destroyed the Eternal Cities. The underground's catastrophes are layered: the Nox's destruction, the Rot God's imprisonment, and the corruption of death all converge in the same subterranean complex.

## Connections & Analysis

The underground is the Lands Between's subconscious -- the repressed history that the Golden Order built itself upon and then denied. Every major theme of the game exists in proto-form underground: star worship predating the Academy's sorcery, artificial life predating the Albinaurics' persecution, resistance to the Fingers predating Ranni's rebellion. The surface world is a palimpsest, and the underground is the erased text showing through. Down is older, truer, and more dangerous. The Golden Order's authority weakens with every step of descent.

Ranni's questline threads through the underground more than any other, and this is no coincidence. She sends the player to Nokron for the Fingerslayer Blade, through Ainsel River, past the Lake of Rot, and beyond Astel. Her rebellion literally passes through the ruins of the previous rebellion. Ranni's [Age of Stars](../endings/age-of-stars.md) ending is the fulfillment of the Nox's original vision -- a world ordered by stars and moon, free from the Greater Will -- achieved thousands of years after those who first envisioned it were destroyed.

The false sky in the Eternal Cities is the underground's most poignant detail. The Nox, denied the real stars, built their own. This is defiance frozen in stone -- a permanent monument to the worldview they refused to abandon.

Godwyn's corpse growing through the Deeproot Depths connects the underground to the surface in the most literal way possible. The Golden Order's failure to deal with death has produced a corruption that spreads through the Erdtree's own root system. The Order's sin leaks downward, contaminating the underground it already defiled.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "Were the Eternal Cities destroyed by the Greater Will, or by their own experiments?"
    Astel's remembrance says its appearance "heralded the end" of the civilization but does not state who sent it. The Greater Will may have deployed Astel as divine punishment, or the Nox's own void-studies may have summoned the entity inadvertently. The distinction between divine punishment and cosmic accident is never clarified.

!!! question "Who created the Albinaurics?"
    The Nox's artificial life program (Mimic Tear, Silver Tears) suggests they may have created the Albinaurics. But no source explicitly confirms this. The connection is inferred from shared themes of artificial creation, not from direct evidence.

!!! question "How many Eternal Cities exist?"
    Nokron and Nokstella are named, but item descriptions reference other unnamed settlements. The underground may be far more extensive than the game reveals, with entire civilizations still buried and undiscovered.
