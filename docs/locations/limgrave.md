# Limgrave

> The starting region of the Lands Between -- a once-prosperous frontier that serves as a microcosm of the world's beauty and decay, dominated by Stormveil Castle and the weakest demigod, Godrick the Grafted.

## Overview

Limgrave is the southeastern region of the Lands Between and the first area the Tarnished encounters upon returning from exile. Its rolling green hills, golden light, and relatively gentle landscape belie the violence and corruption festering beneath the surface. The Erdtree dominates the skyline from the moment the player emerges from the Stranded Graveyard, establishing the spiritual and physical center of the world.

The region is controlled, loosely, by Godrick the Grafted, who rules from Stormveil Castle at the northern edge. Godrick is the last of the golden lineage and the weakest of the demigod children. His domain reflects his nature: a once-grand region now diminished, its infrastructure crumbling, its ruler compensating for inadequacy through grotesque violence.

Limgrave functions narratively as an introduction to every major theme in the game. The ruined churches speak to the Erdtree's fading power. The grafted soldiers and corpse piles in Stormveil reveal the demigods' corruption. The entrance to Siofra River hints at civilizations older than the Golden Order. Dragon Agheel at the lake introduces the dragon lineage. The Waypoint Ruins conceal a persecuted sorceress. Fort Haight's fall demonstrates the collapse of feudal order. And the constant presence of dead Tarnished -- bodies at every gate, hanging from gibbets, piled in Stormveil's corridors -- tells the player they are not the first to try.

## Lore Details

### The First Step and the Tarnished's Return

The Tarnished arrives via a stone coffin carried by spectral currents through the Stranded Graveyard, a catacomb lined with imp statues and burial vessels. This funerary arrival is significant: the Tarnished were exiled from the Lands Between by Marika herself, stripped of grace, and told to "wage war in a land afar, where ye will live, and die." Their return in a coffin implies a journey through death itself.

At the First Step, the grace trails -- golden wisps emanating from Sites of Grace -- point northward toward Stormveil. This guidance system, established by the Erdtree (or by Melina on Marika's behalf), immediately channels the Tarnished toward the demigods and their Great Runes. A dead body sits at the cliff's edge, positioned as if gazing at the Erdtree before dying -- one of many who returned but could not complete the journey.

### The Church of Elleh

A ruined church containing a statue of Marika in benediction, a blacksmith anvil, and the merchant Kale. At night, [Ranni](../demigods/ranni.md) appears here under the alias "Renna" to give the player the Spirit Calling Bell. That she chooses this humble, abandoned church for first contact -- rather than any grand location -- reflects her preference for operating outside established power structures. The church's ruined state is itself a statement: the Erdtree faith once maintained a network of functional places of worship, now reduced to rubble across the land.

### Stormveil Castle: Godrick's Domain

Stormveil is the legacy dungeon of Limgrave and Godrick's seat of power. The castle's original identity predates Godrick entirely -- stormhawk imagery, wind-aspected decorations, and the Stormhawk King spirit ashes all point to a lineage of storm-aspected rulers. Godrick inherited or conquered this legacy but does not embody it; the castle's own nature seems to resist his grafted corruption.

The grafting is Stormveil's defining horror. Arms, legs, and hands protrude from walls and ceilings. Entire walls are composed of compressed limbs stacked like cordwood. Grafted Scions patrol the grounds. The corpse pile before Godrick's throne room is multi-story in height, representing the industrial-scale harvesting of bodies required to fuel the grafting operation. Soldiers, prisoners, and Tarnished alike have been reduced to raw material.

[Margit the Fell Omen](../demigods/morgott.md) guards the approach to Stormveil -- and Margit is actually Morgott, the Omen King of Leyndell. The fact that the capital's own king personally comes to this distant frontier to block a path to a lesser demigod implies that the defense of the Golden Order's hierarchy requires stopping Tarnished at every chokepoint.

### The Chrysalid Face Beneath Stormveil

At the lowest point of Stormveil, accessible through perilous cliff platforming, lies a massive face embedded in rock and roots -- cadaverous, ancient, and unexplained. An ulcerated tree spirit (a corrupted Erdtree root manifestation) guards the area, and jellyfish drift in the eerie quiet. This face may represent a primordial being upon which Stormveil was built, predating the Erdtree entirely. Rogier investigates this area and is killed by the deathroot corruption he finds there, driving his entire questline about "what lies below Stormveil."

### Agheel Lake and the Dragon Communion

Central Limgrave's lake is named for Flying Dragon Agheel, who ranges far from the Dragonbarrow where most dragons reside. Partially submerged ruins dot the lake, their architecture predating the Erdtree era. The Dragon Communion altar on an isolated island nearby represents an older, more primal form of power acquisition -- consuming dragon hearts for draconic abilities. Its hidden, island location implies heresy or at least unsanctioned practice.

### Fort Haight and the Collapse of Order

Fort Haight, overrun by demi-humans, exemplifies the post-Shattering collapse. Kenneth Haight's attempt to restore feudal governance here connects to the larger question of who should rule after the old systems have failed. The fort overlooks the Mistwood and faces eastward toward [Caelid](caelid.md), suggesting it once guarded against threats from that direction.

## Connections & Analysis

Limgrave's physical environment tells a story of layered time. The Erdtree-era churches and roads sit atop older ruins visible at Agheel Lake and beneath Stormveil. The entrance to [Siofra River](siofra-ainsel-river.md) -- accessible via a lift in the Mistwood -- reveals that beneath this gentle green landscape lies an entire civilization the Golden Order suppressed. The vertical architecture of the game begins here: beauty on the surface, darkness below.

Godrick's grafting is the physical manifestation of inadequacy. His Great Rune is explicitly described as "the weakest among them," and his response is to forcibly attach the strength of others to himself. The castle's storm-king heritage stands in pointed contrast -- where the original rulers commanded natural forces, Godrick can only steal from corpses. Even Gostoc, his own gatekeeper, stomps on his body after defeat.

The presence of [Morgott](../demigods/morgott.md) as Margit at the castle approach creates a direct spatial link between Limgrave and [Leyndell](leyndell.md). Morgott's obsessive defense of every chokepoint reveals a king who cannot delegate, who trusts no one else to defend the order he loves. His disguise as a common Omen rather than revealing his royal identity speaks to the shame he carries.

Melina's appearance at the Third Church of Marika to deliver Marika's words about exiling the Tarnished grounds the player's journey in its theological origin. The Tarnished are not accidental visitors -- they are returning soldiers in a war Marika planned.

## Unresolved Mysteries

!!! question "What is the face beneath Stormveil Castle?"
    **Interpretation A:** A remnant of the primordial Crucible -- a being or entity that existed before the Erdtree, upon which the castle was built. The presence of jellyfish (spirits of the dead) and deathroot supports a connection to pre-Erdtree death rites.
    **Interpretation B:** A manifestation of Godwyn's spreading corruption, as Godwyn's body fuses with the Erdtree's roots in the Deeproot Depths and this face bears a resemblance to his death-corrupted visage.
    **Gap:** No in-game text directly identifies this face. Its relationship to the ulcerated tree spirit and Rogier's investigation is contextual, not explicit.

!!! question "Why does Ranni choose the Church of Elleh for first contact?"
    **Interpretation A:** The church is associated with an older tradition (the name "Elleh" may reference pre-Erdtree worship), making it symbolically appropriate for Ranni's anti-Order agenda.
    **Interpretation B:** It is simply the first safe, quiet location near the Tarnished's starting point -- pragmatic rather than symbolic.
    **Gap:** The etymology of "Elleh" is never explained in-game.
